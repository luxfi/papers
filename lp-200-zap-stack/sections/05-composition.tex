\subsection{Reading the table}

Every cell in the ``Layer'' column points to one row of
Table~\ref{tab:tenlayer}. An LP that spans multiple layers (LP-202
pipelining touches ZapDB layer~1 and the executor layer~7; LP-218
rollup touches the frame layer~0, the schema layer~2, and the
executor layer~7) is honestly multi-layer; we do not pretend
otherwise.

\subsection{Decomplection status}

The reflection transaction codec is fully removed. \texttt{Tx.Sign} in
\texttt{platformvm} and \texttt{xvm} no longer routes through
\texttt{codec.Manager}: the P-Chain and X-Chain transaction \emph{is}
the ZAP buffer (struct $=$ wire), signed over $\mathrm{hash}(\text{unsignedBytes})$.
Zero \texttt{codec.Manager} usages remain in node production, the
reflection dispatcher (\texttt{pcodecs}) is deleted, and every VM's
tx/block wire is native ZAP (P, X, and \texttt{dexvm}, \texttt{mpcvm},
\texttt{zkvm}, \texttt{bridgevm}, \texttt{schain}, \texttt{identityvm},
\texttt{graphvm}). The remaining \texttt{luxfi/codec} dependency is the
leaf \texttt{codec/wrappers} byte-packer used by peer-handshake framing
plus Warp's own cross-chain message codec --- separate leaf subsystems,
not the reflection tx codec.

The residual transport migration is orthogonal to the wire kill:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item \texttt{luxfi/p2p} still imported in eleven files. LP-201
  replaces it with QUIC + ZAP + mDNS + DHT. The replacement is in
  flight, not landed.
\item \texttt{service/info} still speaks the legacy jsonrpc envelope on
  top of \texttt{luxfi/p2p}; replaced when LP-201 lands.
\end{itemize}

Neither mutates the architecture. The architecture is the ten-layer
model; these are transport migrations, not codec ones.
