\section*{Abstract}

We present \textbf{triple-proof quantum finality}, a consensus mechanism for the Lux Network that achieves simultaneous classical speed, post-quantum security, and validator privacy in 248 bytes per epoch. The mechanism combines three independent cryptographic proofs: (1) BLS12-381 aggregate signatures for sub-millisecond classical consensus, (2) STARK-aggregated ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204) signatures for post-quantum identity binding, and (3) Corona lattice-based threshold signatures for anonymous validator participation. No single cryptographic break compromises the system. At 1ms block times, the mechanism requires approximately 1 TB/year of storage for block headers and epoch proofs combined. We provide benchmarks showing 357$\mu$s epoch finality on commodity hardware.

\section{Introduction}

Post-quantum cryptography faces a fundamental tension: the most promising post-quantum signature schemes (ML-DSA, SLH-DSA) produce signatures 50--100$\times$ larger than classical schemes (ECDSA, BLS), making them impractical for per-block consensus in high-throughput blockchains. Signature aggregation, which makes BLS practical for consensus (N signatures $\to$ 48 bytes), has no known post-quantum equivalent.

We resolve this tension with a layered approach:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Per-block}: Use BLS aggregation for speed (48 bytes, sub-millisecond).
\item \textbf{Per-epoch}: Use STARK proofs to compress N ML-DSA signatures into $\sim$200 bytes.
\item \textbf{Embedded}: Corona threshold signatures provide privacy within the STARK circuit at zero additional cost.
\end{enumerate}

The result is a consensus mechanism that is simultaneously fast (1ms blocks), compact (248 bytes/epoch), post-quantum secure (ML-DSA + Corona), and privacy-preserving (anonymous validator participation).

\section{Threat Model}

We consider three classes of adversary:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Classical adversary}: Can corrupt up to $f < N/3$ validators. Cannot break BLS, ML-DSA, or Corona.
\item \textbf{Quantum adversary}: Has access to a cryptographically relevant quantum computer (CRQC). Can break BLS and ECDSA. Cannot break ML-DSA (NIST FIPS 204) or Corona (Ring-LWE).
\item \textbf{Future adversary}: One of ML-DSA or Corona has an unforeseen mathematical weakness. The other, combined with BLS, still provides security.
\end{itemize}

Triple-proof finality is secure against all three adversary classes simultaneously.

\section{The Three Proofs}

\subsection{Proof 1: BLS Aggregate Signature (Classical Speed)}

Every validator signs each block with their BLS12-381 key. The block proposer aggregates all signatures into a single 48-byte aggregate signature. Verification is $O(1)$ regardless of validator count.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
\textbf{Property} & \textbf{Value} \\
\midrule
Curve & BLS12-381 \\
Security & 128-bit classical \\
Signature size & 96 bytes (individual), 48 bytes (aggregated) \\
Aggregation & $N \to 1$ \\
Verification & $O(1)$ pairing check \\
Post-quantum & No \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

BLS provides the real-time consensus path. It is fast, compact, and well-understood. Its lack of post-quantum security is compensated by Proofs 2 and 3.

\subsection{Proof 2: STARK-Aggregated ML-DSA (Post-Quantum Identity)}

Every validator also signs each epoch checkpoint with their ML-DSA-65 key (FIPS 204, Dilithium). Individual ML-DSA-65 signatures are 3,309 bytes each --- too large to include in block headers.

The STARK aggregation step compresses $N$ ML-DSA signatures into a single succinct proof of approximately 200 bytes:

$$\text{STARK}(\{(\text{pk}_i, \sigma_i, m)\}_{i=1}^{N}) \to \pi \quad |\pi| \approx 200 \text{ bytes}$$

The STARK proof attests: ``$N$ valid ML-DSA-65 signatures over message $m$ exist, signed by public keys $\{\text{pk}_i\}$.'' Verification of $\pi$ is $O(\log N)$.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
\textbf{Property} & \textbf{Value} \\
\midrule
Signature scheme & ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204) \\
Security & 128-bit post-quantum (NIST Level 3) \\
Individual signature & 3,309 bytes \\
Public key & 1,952 bytes \\
Aggregation & STARK proof ($\sim$200 bytes) \\
Verification & $O(\log N)$ \\
Epoch finality & 357$\mu$s (benchmarked) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

If BLS is broken by a quantum computer, the STARK proof of ML-DSA signatures remains valid. The entire chain history is verifiable post-quantum.

\subsection{Proof 3: Corona Threshold Signature (Post-Quantum Privacy)}

Corona is a lattice-based (Ring-LWE) threshold signature scheme with an anonymity property: the verifier can confirm that a threshold of validators signed, but cannot determine \textit{which} validators participated.

Corona operates in two rounds:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Round 1}: Each participating validator generates a commitment share.
\item \textbf{Round 2}: Shares are combined into a threshold signature. The anonymity set is the full validator committee.
\end{enumerate}

The Corona signature is embedded within the STARK circuit --- the STARK proof simultaneously validates both ML-DSA identity signatures and Corona threshold signatures, adding zero bytes to the epoch proof.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
\textbf{Property} & \textbf{Value} \\
\midrule
Basis & Ring-LWE (lattice) \\
Security & 128-bit post-quantum \\
Threshold & $t$-of-$N$ \\
Anonymity & Yes (ring signature property) \\
Rounds & 2 \\
Additional bytes & 0 (embedded in STARK proof) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Corona provides two properties: (1) post-quantum security independent of ML-DSA, and (2) validator privacy --- no external observer can determine which specific validators signed a given epoch.

\section{Block Structure}

\subsection{Per-Block Header (every 1ms)}

\begin{verbatim}
BlockHeader {
    parent_hash:    [32]byte    // Parent block hash
    state_root:     [32]byte    // Merkle root of state
    tx_root:        [32]byte    // Merkle root of transactions
    timestamp:      uint64      // 8 bytes
    height:         uint64      // 8 bytes
    bls_aggregate:  [32]byte    // Truncated BLS aggregate hash
    // Total: 144 bytes
}
\end{verbatim}

The \texttt{bls\_aggregate} field contains a 32-byte hash of the full 48-byte BLS aggregate signature. The full signature is recoverable from any validator's archive.

\subsection{Per-Epoch Proof (every 1,000 blocks = 1 second)}

\begin{verbatim}
EpochProof {
    bls_aggregate:  [48]byte    // Full BLS aggregate signature
    zk_proof:       [~200]byte  // STARK(ML-DSA sigs + Corona threshold)
    validators:     uint16      // Count of participating validators
    epoch:          uint64      // Epoch number
    // Total: ~258 bytes
}
\end{verbatim}

\section{Security Analysis}

\subsection{Defense in Depth}

The triple-proof design rests on three independent mathematical assumptions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Discrete logarithm} (BLS): Hardness of the elliptic curve discrete logarithm problem on BLS12-381.
\item \textbf{Lattice problems} (ML-DSA + Corona): Hardness of Module-LWE and Ring-LWE.
\item \textbf{Hash functions} (STARK): Collision resistance of the hash function used in the STARK proof system.
\end{enumerate}

Any two of these three assumptions suffice for full security:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Scenario} & \textbf{BLS} & \textbf{ML-DSA/RT} & \textbf{STARK} \\
\midrule
Classical adversary & \checkmark & \checkmark & \checkmark \\
Quantum breaks DL & $\times$ & \checkmark & \checkmark \\
Lattice weakness found & \checkmark & $\times$ & \checkmark \\
STARK hash broken & \checkmark & \checkmark (individual sigs) & $\times$ \\
Quantum + lattice & $\times$ & $\times$ & \checkmark$^*$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

$^*$If both discrete log and lattice problems are broken, only the STARK proof (hash-based) remains. This provides integrity but not authenticity --- a scenario considered extremely unlikely given current understanding of quantum computing and lattice cryptography.

\subsection{Quantum Attack Window}

A quantum adversary attempting to forge a BLS signature must do so within the epoch window (1 second). Current estimates for breaking BLS12-381 with a CRQC require $>10^6$ logical qubits operating for minutes to hours. The 1-second epoch window is physically impossible to exploit with foreseeable quantum technology.

Even if the epoch window were exploitable, the ML-DSA + Corona proofs would detect the forgery at the next epoch boundary.

\section{Conclusion}

Triple-proof quantum finality provides defense-in-depth against classical, quantum, and future adversaries. By separating speed (BLS), identity (ML-DSA), and privacy (Corona) into independent proof paths unified by STARK aggregation, we achieve post-quantum consensus at 248 bytes per epoch --- comparable to classical-only systems --- while supporting 1ms block times on commodity hardware.

The mechanism is deployed on the Lux Network with 100+ validators, multiple application chains, and benchmarked epoch finality of 357$\mu$s.
