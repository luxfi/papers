\begin{abstract}
We present an implementation framework for Halo2, a zero-knowledge proof system achieving recursive proof composition without trusted setup. Building on the Halo technique of nested amortization via inner product arguments (IPA), Halo2 combines the expressiveness of PLONK-style arithmetization with the trust minimization of transparent setup. Our framework details the construction of IPA-based polynomial commitments, the accumulation scheme enabling efficient recursion, and practical circuit design patterns. We demonstrate that Halo2 enables fully trustless recursive proofs with verification complexity logarithmic in circuit size, providing the foundation for incrementally verifiable computation without ceremony-based trust assumptions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The requirement for trusted setup ceremonies in pairing-based zkSNARKs presents a fundamental tension between cryptographic efficiency and trust minimization. While systems like Groth16 and PLONK achieve remarkable proof compactness, their security relies on the assumption that at least one participant in the setup ceremony honestly deleted their randomness---an assumption that cannot be publicly verified.

Halo \cite{halo} introduced a breakthrough technique: recursive proof composition via inner product arguments without pairings, eliminating trusted setup entirely. Halo2 \cite{halo2} refines this approach, combining IPA-based commitments with PLONK's flexible arithmetization to create a practical system for trustless recursive proofs.

\subsection{Key Innovations}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \textbf{Transparent setup}: Public randomness from hash functions; no toxic waste
    \item \textbf{Accumulation-based recursion}: Defer verification work across proof layers
    \item \textbf{Logarithmic verification}: IPA verification in $O(\log n)$ group operations
    \item \textbf{PLONK compatibility}: Full custom gate and lookup support
\end{enumerate}

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

\subsection{Discrete Logarithm Groups}

Unlike pairing-based systems, Halo2 operates over standard discrete logarithm groups.

\begin{definition}[Discrete Log Group]
Let $\G$ be a cyclic group of prime order $p$ with generators $G, H \in \G$. The discrete logarithm assumption states that computing $x$ from $G^x$ is computationally hard.
\end{definition}

We instantiate over the Pallas/Vesta cycle for efficient recursion or secp256k1 for Bitcoin compatibility.

\subsection{Pedersen Commitments}

\begin{definition}[Vector Pedersen Commitment]
For vector $\vec{a} = (a_1, \ldots, a_n) \in \F^n$ with generators $\vec{G} = (G_1, \ldots, G_n) \in \G^n$:
\[
\com(\vec{a}) = \prod_{i=1}^n G_i^{a_i}
\]
With blinding factor $r$ and additional generator $H$:
\[
\com(\vec{a}; r) = H^r \cdot \prod_{i=1}^n G_i^{a_i}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Binding and Hiding]
Under the discrete log assumption, Pedersen commitments are computationally binding and perfectly hiding (with blinding).
\end{proposition}

\section{Inner Product Arguments}
\label{sec:ipa}

The inner product argument enables proving knowledge of vectors $\vec{a}, \vec{b}$ such that $\gen{\vec{a}, \vec{b}} = c$ with logarithmic communication.

\subsection{The Bulletproofs IPA}

\begin{definition}[Inner Product Relation]
Given commitment $P = \com(\vec{a}) \cdot H^{\gen{\vec{a}, \vec{b}}}$ and evaluation $c$, prove:
\[
\gen{\vec{a}, \vec{b}} = c \quad \text{where} \quad \gen{\vec{a}, \vec{b}} = \sum_{i=1}^n a_i b_i
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Inner Product Argument (Prover)}
\label{alg:ipa-prover}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Vectors $\vec{a}, \vec{b} \in \F^n$, generators $\vec{G}, \vec{H} \in \G^n$, $U \in \G$
\Ensure Proof $\pi = (L_1, R_1, \ldots, L_{\log n}, R_{\log n}, a', b')$
\State $n' \gets n$
\While{$n' > 1$}
    \State $m \gets n'/2$
    \State $\vec{a}_L, \vec{a}_R \gets \vec{a}[1..m], \vec{a}[m+1..n']$
    \State $\vec{b}_L, \vec{b}_R \gets \vec{b}[1..m], \vec{b}[m+1..n']$
    \State $c_L \gets \gen{\vec{a}_L, \vec{b}_R}$, $c_R \gets \gen{\vec{a}_R, \vec{b}_L}$
    \State $L \gets \com_{\vec{G}_R}(\vec{a}_L) \cdot \com_{\vec{H}_L}(\vec{b}_R) \cdot U^{c_L}$
    \State $R \gets \com_{\vec{G}_L}(\vec{a}_R) \cdot \com_{\vec{H}_R}(\vec{b}_L) \cdot U^{c_R}$
    \State Output $(L, R)$
    \State $x \gets \mathcal{H}(L, R)$ \Comment{Challenge}
    \State $\vec{a} \gets x \cdot \vec{a}_L + x^{-1} \cdot \vec{a}_R$
    \State $\vec{b} \gets x^{-1} \cdot \vec{b}_L + x \cdot \vec{b}_R$
    \State $\vec{G} \gets x^{-1} \cdot \vec{G}_L + x \cdot \vec{G}_R$ \Comment{Compressed generators}
    \State $\vec{H} \gets x \cdot \vec{H}_L + x^{-1} \cdot \vec{H}_R$
    \State $n' \gets m$
\EndWhile
\State \Return $(\{L_i, R_i\}, a', b')$ where $a' = a_1, b' = b_1$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Verification}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Inner Product Argument (Verifier)}
\label{alg:ipa-verifier}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Commitment $P$, proof $\pi = (\{L_i, R_i\}, a', b')$, generators $\vec{G}, \vec{H}$
\Ensure Accept or Reject
\State Recompute challenges $x_1, \ldots, x_{\log n}$ from transcript
\State Compute combined commitment:
\[
P' = P \cdot \prod_{i=1}^{\log n} (L_i^{x_i^2} \cdot R_i^{x_i^{-2}})
\]
\State Compute combined generators:
\[
G' = \prod_{i=1}^n G_i^{s_i}, \quad H' = \prod_{i=1}^n H_i^{s_i^{-1}}
\]
where $s_i = \prod_{j: i_j = 1} x_j \cdot \prod_{j: i_j = 0} x_j^{-1}$ (binary decomposition of $i$)
\State \textbf{Accept} iff $P' = G'^{a'} \cdot H'^{b'} \cdot U^{a'b'}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{theorem}[IPA Complexity]
The inner product argument achieves:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Proof size: $2\log n$ group elements + 2 field elements
    \item Prover: $O(n)$ field and group operations
    \item Verifier: $O(n)$ group operations (or $O(\log n)$ with precomputation)
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\section{Polynomial Commitments from IPA}
\label{sec:commitment}

\subsection{Construction}

\begin{definition}[IPA Polynomial Commitment]
For polynomial $f(X) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} f_i X^i$ with coefficients $\vec{f} = (f_0, \ldots, f_{n-1})$:
\[
\com(f) = \prod_{i=0}^{n-1} G_i^{f_i}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Polynomial Evaluation Proof}
\label{alg:poly-eval}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Polynomial $f$, evaluation point $z$, commitment $C = \com(f)$
\Ensure Proof that $f(z) = y$
\State Let $\vec{a} = (f_0, f_1, \ldots, f_{n-1})$ \Comment{Coefficients}
\State Let $\vec{b} = (1, z, z^2, \ldots, z^{n-1})$ \Comment{Powers}
\State Note: $\gen{\vec{a}, \vec{b}} = f(z) = y$
\State Run IPA prover on $(\vec{a}, \vec{b})$ with commitment $C \cdot U^y$
\State \Return IPA proof
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Batched Opening}

For multiple polynomials $f_1, \ldots, f_k$ at point $z$:

\begin{align*}
f'(X) &= \sum_{i=1}^k \nu^{i-1} f_i(X) \\
y' &= \sum_{i=1}^k \nu^{i-1} f_i(z) \\
C' &= \prod_{i=1}^k C_i^{\nu^{i-1}}
\end{align*}

Single IPA proves all evaluations.

\section{Accumulation Scheme}
\label{sec:accumulation}

The key insight enabling efficient Halo recursion: instead of fully verifying each proof, accumulate verification instances.

\subsection{Accumulator Definition}

\begin{definition}[IPA Accumulator]
An accumulator instance consists of:
\[
\text{acc} = (C, z, y, \pi_{partial})
\]
where $C$ is a commitment, $z$ is an evaluation point, $y$ is the claimed evaluation, and $\pi_{partial}$ contains IPA challenges without final verification.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Accumulation Protocol}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Accumulator Folding}
\label{alg:fold}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Accumulators $\text{acc}_1 = (C_1, z_1, y_1, \vec{x}_1)$, $\text{acc}_2 = (C_2, z_2, y_2, \vec{x}_2)$
\Ensure Folded accumulator $\text{acc}'$
\State Sample random $\rho$ via Fiat-Shamir
\State $C' \gets C_1 \cdot C_2^\rho$
\State $(z', y') \gets $ combined evaluation instance
\State $\vec{x}' \gets $ folded challenge vectors
\State \Return $\text{acc}' = (C', z', y', \vec{x}')$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{theorem}[Accumulation Soundness]
If accumulator $\text{acc}$ verifies, then with overwhelming probability either:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item All accumulated instances are valid, or
    \item The prover broke the discrete log assumption
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Deferred Verification}

\begin{proposition}[Amortized Verification]
Verifying $k$ accumulated proofs requires:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $O(k \log n)$ field operations (for folding)
    \item $O(n)$ group operations (single final IPA check)
\end{itemize}
versus $O(kn)$ for naive verification.
\end{proposition}

\section{Recursive Proof Composition}
\label{sec:recursion}

\subsection{The Recursion Circuit}

To prove statement $\phi_k$, the recursive circuit verifies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item Previous proof $\pi_{k-1}$ for statement $\phi_{k-1}$
    \item Transition $\phi_{k-1} \to \phi_k$ is valid
    \item Accumulator folding is correct
\end{enumerate}

\begin{definition}[Incrementally Verifiable Computation]
An IVC scheme for function $F$ produces proofs $\pi_i$ such that:
\[
\mathsf{Verify}(\pi_i, x_0, x_i) = 1 \implies x_i = F^i(x_0)
\]
\end{definition}

\subsection{In-Circuit Operations}

The recursion circuit must perform:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Scalar multiplication on the curve (for commitment checks)
    \item Challenge computation (Poseidon hash)
    \item Accumulator folding (field arithmetic)
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}[Recursion Overhead]
The Halo2 recursion circuit requires approximately 20,000 constraints for accumulator verification, independent of the accumulated circuit size.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Cycle of Curves}

For native field arithmetic in recursion:

\begin{definition}[Pasta Curves]
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pallas}: $y^2 = x^3 + 5$ over $\F_p$, scalar field $\F_q$
    \item \textbf{Vesta}: $y^2 = x^3 + 5$ over $\F_q$, scalar field $\F_p$
\end{itemize}
where $p$ and $q$ are both approximately $2^{255}$.
\end{definition}

Circuits on Pallas prove statements about Vesta computations and vice versa, enabling efficient alternating recursion.

\section{The Halo2 Construction}
\label{sec:halo2}

\subsection{Arithmetization}

Halo2 uses PLONK-style arithmetization with additional features:

\begin{definition}[Halo2 Circuit]
A Halo2 circuit consists of:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Columns}: advice (witness), instance (public), fixed (selector), lookup
    \item \textbf{Regions}: Rectangular areas assigned by circuits
    \item \textbf{Gates}: Polynomial constraints over column values
    \item \textbf{Lookups}: Table membership constraints
    \item \textbf{Permutations}: Copy constraints between cells
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\subsection{The Proving System}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Halo2 Prover (Simplified)}
\label{alg:halo2-prove}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Circuit $\mathcal{C}$, witness $w$, instance $x$
\Ensure Proof $\pi$
\State \textbf{Round 1}: Commit to advice columns
\For{each advice column $a$}
    \State $A \gets \IPA.\com(a(X))$
\EndFor

\State \textbf{Round 2}: Permutation argument
\State Compute permutation grand product $z(X)$
\State $Z \gets \IPA.\com(z(X))$

\State \textbf{Round 3}: Lookup argument
\State Compute lookup grand product polynomials
\State Commit to lookup polynomials

\State \textbf{Round 4}: Quotient polynomial
\State Compute combined constraint polynomial $t(X)$
\State Split and commit: $T_0, T_1, \ldots$

\State \textbf{Round 5}: Evaluations and opening
\State Evaluate all polynomials at challenge $\zeta$
\State Create batched IPA opening proof
\State Output accumulator instance

\State \Return $\pi = (\text{commitments}, \text{evaluations}, \text{IPA proof}, \text{accumulator})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Verification}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Halo2 Verifier}
\label{alg:halo2-verify}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Verification key $\mathsf{vk}$, instance $x$, proof $\pi$
\Ensure Accept/Reject (or accumulator)
\State Recompute all challenges via Fiat-Shamir
\State Check evaluation consistency
\State Fold IPA instances into accumulator
\State Either:
\State \quad (a) Verify accumulator directly, or
\State \quad (b) Return accumulator for recursive verification
\State \Return result
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Circuit API}

Halo2 provides a structured API for circuit development:

\begin{verbatim}
trait Circuit<F: Field> {
    fn configure(meta: &mut ConstraintSystem<F>) -> Self::Config;
    fn synthesize(
        &self,
        config: Self::Config,
        layouter: impl Layouter<F>,
    ) -> Result<(), Error>;
}
\end{verbatim}

Key concepts:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Chip}: Reusable circuit component (e.g., range check, hash)
    \item \textbf{Region}: Isolated area for constraint satisfaction
    \item \textbf{Layouter}: Manages region allocation and cell assignment
\end{itemize}

\section{Security Analysis}

\subsection{Soundness}

\begin{theorem}[Halo2 Soundness]
Under the discrete logarithm assumption, Halo2 achieves knowledge soundness with negligible extraction failure probability.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Zero-Knowledge}

\begin{theorem}[Statistical Zero-Knowledge]
With appropriate blinding in polynomial commitments, Halo2 achieves statistical zero-knowledge.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Recursion Soundness}

\begin{theorem}[IVC Soundness]
The accumulation-based recursion is sound: accepting an invalid accumulated proof requires breaking discrete log.
\end{theorem}

\section{Conclusion}

Halo2 represents a significant advancement in practical zero-knowledge systems, eliminating trusted setup while maintaining expressiveness through PLONK-style arithmetization. The accumulation-based recursion enables unbounded computation verification with constant verification cost, opening new possibilities for incrementally verifiable computation and scalable blockchain systems.

The trade-off of larger proofs ($\sim$5KB vs 192B) and longer verification time compared to pairing-based systems is acceptable for applications prioritizing trust minimization. Future work includes hardware acceleration for IPA verification and integration with folding schemes for improved prover efficiency.
