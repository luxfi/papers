\section{The DEX Adapter Precompile (0x9010)}
\label{sec:dex-adapter}

The twenty precompiles documented above are \emph{cryptographic}
primitives: pure functions of their input bytes with no external state.
Lux adds one precompile of a different kind --- an
\emph{application adapter} --- at address \texttt{0x9010}, in the
application range \texttt{0x9000}--\texttt{0x90ff} reserved above the
cryptographic block. It is the EVM-side entry point to the Lux DEX, and
its engineering is instructive precisely because it is not a pure
function: it is a deterministic facade over an external matching chain.

\subsection{A CLOB Facade, Not an AMM}

\texttt{0x9010} exposes a Uniswap-V4-\texttt{PoolManager}-style singleton
interface --- pool keys, hooks, flash accounting --- but it is a
\textbf{central-limit-order-book (CLOB) facade}, not an automated market
maker. A call does not move along a bonding curve; it relays an order to
the D-Chain matching VM (Lux Lightspeed DEX \cite{lux-lightspeed-dex})
and reports the resulting fill. The V4-shaped surface is retained so that
existing V4-aware routers and Solidity integrators compose unchanged,
while the execution semantics underneath are price-time priority CLOB
matching rather than constant-product swaps.

This is deliberately distinct from the plain Solidity AMMs Lux also
ships: the V2 (constant-product) and V3 (concentrated-liquidity) pools
are ordinary contracts with no precompile involvement. The precompile
exists only for the CLOB path, where on-chain bytecode cannot match a
limit-order book at the throughput the D-Chain achieves natively.

\subsection{Inert by Default}

The precompile is \textbf{inert unless the validator is configured with a
\texttt{dex-zap-endpoint}}. With no endpoint configured, \texttt{0x9010}
is registered but performs no relay: it is dormant, exactly as a
reserved-but-unwired entry would be. This keeps the public \texttt{luxd}
binary free of any dependency on the private D-Chain --- the adapter is
present in the precompile table but does nothing until an operator
explicitly wires it to a matching endpoint. Determinism is preserved
trivially in the inert state, and the configured state is made
deterministic by the idempotency discipline below.

\subsection{Native LUX as \texttt{address(0)}}

The pool-key encoding follows the V4 convention that the native asset is
the zero address. A pool key referencing \texttt{address(0)} denotes
native LUX; ERC-20 securities use their token address. This removes the
wrapped-native special case from the adapter ABI: every asset, native or
token, is a 20-byte address in the pool key, and \texttt{address(0)}
routes to the D-Chain's native-balance path.

\subsection{Content-Addressed Idempotency}

A relay precompile faces a hazard a pure precompile does not: the same
EVM call may be re-executed (block re-org, validator restart,
speculative execution) and must not double-submit the underlying order.
The adapter keys idempotency on a \textbf{content-addressed transaction
identifier} --- the \texttt{txID} is a hash of the order's canonical
bytes, so an identical order produces an identical \texttt{txID}, and the
D-Chain deduplicates on it. Re-execution of the same EVM call relays the
same \texttt{txID}, which the matcher recognizes as already-seen and does
not re-match. The precompile thus presents exactly-once semantics to the
EVM despite being a relay rather than a pure computation --- the same
determinism contract every other precompile honors, achieved by
content-addressing instead of statelessness.

\subsection{Why This Belongs in the Precompile Layer}

A CLOB cannot be matched in EVM bytecode at competitive cost --- the same
argument that justifies the cryptographic precompiles. The adapter
keeps the EVM as the settlement and composition surface (V4-compatible,
hook-friendly) while delegating the latency-critical matching to a chain
engineered for it. The full D-Chain VM architecture --- matcher-at-Verify,
\texttt{zapdb} commit, crash-restart durability, and the GPU CLOB matcher
behind it --- is specified in the Lux Lightspeed DEX paper
\cite{lux-lightspeed-dex}; this section documents only the EVM-side
adapter and the engineering discipline (inert-by-default,
\texttt{address(0)} native, content-addressed idempotency) that makes a
stateful relay safe to expose as a precompile.
