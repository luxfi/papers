\section{Related Work}

\label{sec:related}

\textbf{FHE on the EVM.} Prior FHE-on-EVM approaches rely on Rust FFI
into external TFHE libraries or run FHE off-chain in a coprocessor with
the EVM holding only ciphertext handles. Our approach is native: the
\texttt{lux/precompile/fhe} package implements FHE at the precompile
layer (addresses \texttt{0x...80}--\texttt{0x...83}, see
\texttt{IFHE.sol}) backed by the \texttt{lux/fhe} Go TFHE library on
\texttt{luxfi/lattice}. No cross-language FFI on the consensus path; the
GPU acceleration story routes through the \texttt{luxcpp/gpu}
dispatcher with backend plugins (Metal/CUDA/WebGPU) sourced from
\texttt{lux-private/gpu-kernels/}. The result is consensus-deterministic
keygen across every validator, deterministic per-op gas, and an audit
surface bounded by one Go module rather than a multi-language toolchain.

\textbf{Threshold decryption.} Boneh et al.~\cite{boneh2018threshold}
formalized threshold FHE for general circuits. Our LSSS implementation
in \texttt{lux/fhe/pkg/threshold/} adapts the M-of-N Shamir-over-$\mathbb{Z}_q$
construction to the blockchain validator-set rotation model, with
noise-flooded partial decryption + Lagrange combine.

\textbf{FHE compilers.} High-level circuit synthesis from a host
language is the next milestone (Refractor, see
\texttt{lux/refractor}; design pending). Today, developers compose
gates programmatically through the \texttt{lux/fhe} integer-radix layer
(\texttt{integers.go}, \texttt{integer\_ops.go}) or call the precompile
ABI directly from Solidity.
