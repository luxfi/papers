\begin{abstract}
\noindent The canonical Lux topology is nine orthogonal chains ---
\chain{P} \chain{C} \chain{X} \chain{Q} \chain{Z} \chain{A} \chain{B} \chain{M}
\chain{F} (LP-134). This note fixes the \emph{economic} invariants that keep
that topology clean: which chains own canonical asset state, and how fees flow.
The rule is deliberately narrow. Only \chain{P} and \chain{X} are canonical
UTXO state machines: \chain{P} is the validator/stake/control-plane ledger and
\chain{X} is the asset/settlement/payment ledger. \chain{X} is additionally the
canonical fee asset. Every other chain (\chain{C} \chain{D} \chain{Q} \chain{Z}
\chain{A} \chain{B} \chain{M} \chain{F}) is a domain-specific execution lane
that holds VM-local state plus an \chain{X}-funded fee balance, meters its own
work, and settles rewards and remainders back to \chain{X} (with validator
economics accounted on \chain{P}). We give the fee-funding model (per-user
\chain{X} import/export plus per-chain fee-root settlement, finalized on
\chain{Q}), a per-chain fee policy, and seven conservation invariants that
prevent any lane from silently becoming a second asset ledger. Shielded state on
\chain{Z} and encrypted balances on \chain{F} are UTXO-\emph{like} in form but
are explicitly \emph{not} the canonical transparent \chain{X} set --- they are
commitments over \chain{X}-backed assets and must prove conservation against it.
\end{abstract}

\section{The invariant: only P and X own UTXOs}
% ============================================================================

A recurring failure mode in multi-chain systems is that specialized chains
quietly accrete the powers of a money ledger --- they mint balances, hold
custody of value, and become a second, unreconciled source of asset truth. Lux
forecloses this by convention:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Only \chain{P} and \chain{X} are canonical UTXO state machines. No other
chain becomes an asset ledger unless it imports/exports value through \chain{X}.}
\end{quote}

\paragraph{\chain{P}-Chain --- stake / control plane.} \chain{P} holds
UTXO-style state for validator stake, delegation stake, validator-set
transitions, staking rewards, sovereign-network / L1 registration, and
chain-creation / network-governance control transactions. \chain{P} is the
\emph{stake and reward} rail.

\paragraph{\chain{X}-Chain --- assets / settlement.} \chain{X} holds UTXO-style
state for native assets, transfers, settlement, bridge lock/release settlement,
DEX deposit/withdraw settlement, and cross-chain import/export accounting.
\chain{X} is the \emph{money} rail and the canonical fee asset.

Everything else consumes \chain{P}/\chain{X} settlement or maintains its own
VM-local domain state --- never a canonical asset UTXO set of its own.

\section{The fee model}
% ============================================================================

\begin{quote}
\chain{X} is the canonical fee asset and payment rail. Each chain meters its own
execution. Fees are paid from local imported balances or settled back to
\chain{X}. \chain{P} handles validator economics and reward distribution.
\end{quote}

So \chain{X} = money rail, \chain{P} = staking/reward rail, and \chain{C}
\chain{D} \chain{A} \chain{B} \chain{M} \chain{F} \chain{Q} \chain{Z} are
execution lanes that consume fees.

\subsection{Option 1 --- \chain{X}-native fee import (sovereign clarity)}

A user imports a fee budget from \chain{X} into the target chain; execution
charges the local balance; any remainder can be exported back:
\[
\text{\chain{X} ExportTx} \to \text{target ImportTx} \to \text{local fee balance}
\to \text{execution charge} \to \text{(optional) export remainder to \chain{X}.}
\]
Clean for \chain{D} \chain{F} \chain{A} \chain{B} \chain{M}: they meter
domain-specific work without mutating \chain{X} on every operation.

\emph{Example (FHE job on \chain{F}).} (1) user holds LUX on \chain{X};
(2) exports a 10-LUX budget \chain{X}$\to$\chain{F}; (3) \chain{F} imports it as
local fee credit; (4) GPU workers execute the encrypted job; (5) \chain{F} pays
workers/validators from the local pool; (6) remaining credit exports back to
\chain{X}.

\subsection{Option 2 --- fee-root settlement (throughput)}

Each chain accumulates fees internally and periodically commits a fee
settlement root to \chain{X}:
\[
\text{local fee accounting} \to \text{epoch fee root} \to
\text{\chain{X} settlement / distribution.}
\]
Better for high-throughput \chain{D}/\chain{F}/\chain{A} where per-tx \chain{X}
imports would be too heavy.

\subsection{Recommended hybrid}

Use both. User-facing funding via \chain{X} import/export into local balances;
high-throughput settlement via per-chain fee roots periodically settled to
\chain{X}/\chain{P}; validator/operator rewards distributed by \chain{P} using
\emph{finalized} fee roots. This yields: \chain{X} = source of fee currency;
local chain = execution metering; \chain{Q} = finality of fee roots; \chain{P} =
validator reward / stake accounting.

\section{UTXO-like $\neq$ canonical UTXO: Z and F}
% ============================================================================

\chain{Z} may carry commitments, nullifiers, shielded notes, and rollup exits;
\chain{F} may carry encrypted balances and ciphertext state. In \emph{form} these
resemble a UTXO set, but architecturally they are \textbf{not} the canonical
transparent \chain{X} asset ledger:
\[
\text{\chain{X}} = \text{canonical transparent UTXO assets}, \qquad
\text{\chain{Z}/\chain{F}} = \text{shielded / encrypted commitments over
\chain{X}-backed assets.}
\]
Consequently \chain{Z} and \chain{F} private state must \emph{prove conservation}
against \chain{X}-backed commitments (a shielded or encrypted balance may never
exceed, or mint outside, the \chain{X} value it represents).

% ============================================================================
\section{Fee \& conservation invariants}
% ============================================================================

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{I\arabic*.}]
\item No chain may mint canonical asset supply except through \chain{X}/\chain{P}
defined rules.
\item Every non-\chain{P}/\chain{X} fee balance must be funded by an
\chain{X}/\chain{P} import, a genesis allocation, or a finalized fee settlement.
\item \chain{D}/\chain{F}/\chain{A}/\chain{M}/\chain{B} worker rewards must be
claimable / settled back to \chain{X}.
\item \chain{Q} finality fees cannot block safety or finality.
\item \chain{Z}/\chain{F} private state must prove conservation against
\chain{X}-backed commitments.
\item Bridge fees on \chain{B} are message/relayer fees --- not arbitrary custody
of canonical assets.
\item \chain{M} signs attestations but never becomes the asset ledger.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Summary}
% ============================================================================

\textbf{UTXO chains:} \chain{P} = staking/validator UTXO; \chain{X} =
asset/settlement UTXO. \textbf{Fees:} \chain{X} is the canonical fee asset rail;
\chain{P} handles staking/reward economics; every other chain meters locally with
fee balances funded from \chain{X}, then settles/rewards back to \chain{X}/\chain{P}
through finalized roots (finalized on \chain{Q}). That keeps the nine-chain
architecture clean: \chain{P}/\chain{X} are the money/stake ledgers, and \chain{C}
\chain{D} \chain{Q} \chain{Z} \chain{A} \chain{B} \chain{M} \chain{F} are
specialized execution lanes. It is the same discipline as the \chain{T}-braid
removal: separate what a primitive \emph{is} (asset value, threshold crypto) from
\emph{where} it is applied (an execution lane) --- values, not places.
