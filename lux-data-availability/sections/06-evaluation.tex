\subsection{Experimental Setup}

We deploy Lux-DAS on a 200-validator Fuji testnet with 16 appchains. Each appchain produces
blobs at a configurable rate. Validators run on AWS \texttt{c6g.xlarge} instances (4 vCPU
ARM Graviton2, 8 GiB RAM, 10 Gbps network). Light clients run on \texttt{t4g.micro} instances
(2 vCPU, 1 GiB RAM) to simulate resource-constrained devices.

\subsection{Throughput}

\midrule
$k=32$, 1 appchain & 64 & 1.9 & 4.2 \\

\end{table}

The DA overhead measures the ratio of commitment and proof data to raw blob data. As $k$
increases, the overhead decreases because commitment size grows as $O(k)$ while blob size
grows as $O(k^2)$.

\subsection{Comparison with EIP-4844 and Celestia}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Comparison of DAS systems}
\label{tab:comparison}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Lux-DAS} & \textbf{EIP-4844} & \textbf{Celestia} \\
\midrule
Commitment type & KZG & KZG & NMT (Merkle) \\

Light client latency (ms, $s=75$) & 42 & N/A\footnotemark & 180 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}
\addtocounter{footnote}{-1}
\footnotetext{EIP-4844 uses single-dimensional KZG, so proofs are one group element.}
\stepcounter{footnote}
\footnotetext{EIP-4844 does not implement DAS; light clients must trust full nodes or use
the beacon chain attestation for DA.}

Lux-DAS achieves higher throughput than Celestia primarily because KZG proofs are constant-size
(two group elements per cell), whereas NMT Merkle proofs grow logarithmically. The amortized
verification advantage from \Cref{prop:batched-verification} further improves the gap when
verifying cells across multiple appchains in the same slot.

\subsection{Bandwidth Analysis}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Per-validator bandwidth (Mbps) by number of appchains validated}
\label{tab:bandwidth}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Appchains validated} & 1 & 4 & 8 & 16 \\
\midrule
Without aggregation & 2.1 & 8.4 & 16.8 & 33.6 \\
With aggregation & 2.1 & 3.2 & 4.1 & 5.3 \\
Reduction factor & 1.0$\times$ & 2.6$\times$ & 4.1$\times$ & 6.3$\times$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Appchain-scoped aggregation provides near-logarithmic bandwidth scaling with the number of
appchains, because validators only download full data for their assigned appchains and compact
Merkle proofs for cross-appchain DA verification.
