\section{Contrast with Regulated Model}

\label{sec:contrast}

The regulated custody model (deployed by a regulated financial services provider\ for the the regulated ATS)
and the sovereign model (deployed on Lux Network) use identical cryptographic
primitives but make opposite trust assumptions.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{3.2cm}p{5cm}p{5cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Property} & \textbf{Regulated (exchange partner)} & \textbf{Sovereign (Lux)} \\
\midrule
Data encryption &
  AES-256-GCM, per-org DEK from HMAC-SHA256 &
  AES-256-GCM, per-vault DEK from ML-KEM-1024 \\[4pt]
Master key holder &
  Operator (Google Cloud KMS, HSM-backed) &
  User (Argon2id from passphrase) \\[4pt]
Can operator decrypt? &
  Yes (required by SEC 17a-4) &
  No (by design) \\[4pt]
Key encapsulation &
  Not used (HMAC derivation) &
  ML-KEM-1024 (FIPS 203) \\[4pt]
Signatures &
  HMAC-SHA256 (migrating to ML-DSA-87) &
  ML-DSA-87 (FIPS 204) \\[4pt]
Threshold signing &
  CGGMP21/FROST, operator holds 1 shard &
  CGGMP21/FROST, operator holds 0 shards \\[4pt]
Recovery &
  Operator cold storage + KMS &
  Social recovery ($t$-of-$n$ shares) \\[4pt]
Data loss on key loss? &
  No (operator can re-derive DEK) &
  Yes (if $< t$ recovery parties available) \\[4pt]
Regulatory compliance &
  SEC 17a-4, FINRA 4511, BSA &
  User responsibility \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Full comparison of regulated and sovereign key architectures.}
\label{tab:full-comparison}
\end{table}

The choice between models is a policy decision, not a cryptographic one.
The same \texttt{luxfi/crypto} library implements both.  The difference
is in key hierarchy: who holds the root, and whether the provider can
derive decryption keys.

\begin{invariant}[Model Exclusivity]
A deployment is either regulated or sovereign.  There is no hybrid where
the operator ``sometimes'' can decrypt.  The trust model is determined at
deployment time by key hierarchy configuration.
\end{invariant}
