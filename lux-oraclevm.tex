\begin{abstract}
We present OracleVM, a specialized virtual machine for the Lux Network that provides decentralized, manipulation-resistant price feeds. OracleVM aggregates data from multiple independent sources using median-based aggregation with outlier detection. Our design provides cryptographic freshness guarantees through signed timestamps and economic security through staking and slashing. We prove bounds on price deviation under adversarial conditions and demonstrate sub-second update latency with configurable staleness thresholds. The system supports major asset pairs with deviation-triggered and heartbeat-triggered updates.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Price oracles are critical infrastructure for decentralized finance (DeFi). Lending protocols, derivatives, and stablecoins depend on accurate, timely price data. However, oracle manipulation has caused hundreds of millions of dollars in losses through:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Flash loan attacks}: Manipulating spot prices within a single transaction
    \item \textbf{Time-weighted average manipulation}: Sustained attacks over multiple blocks
    \item \textbf{Data source compromise}: Attacking upstream data providers
    \item \textbf{Staleness exploitation}: Using outdated prices during volatile periods
\end{itemize}

OracleVM addresses these challenges by implementing oracle functionality as a native Lux Network primitive with:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Multi-source aggregation with Byzantine fault tolerance
    \item Statistical outlier detection resistant to manipulation
    \item Cryptographic freshness proofs with configurable staleness bounds
    \item Economic incentives aligning reporter behavior with accuracy
\end{enumerate}

\section{System Model}

\subsection{Data Model}

\begin{definition}[Price Feed]
A price feed $F = (base, quote, \mathcal{R}, \theta, \tau)$ where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $base, quote$: asset identifiers
    \item $\mathcal{R} = \{r_1, \ldots, r_n\}$: set of authorized reporters
    \item $\theta$: deviation threshold for updates
    \item $\tau$: maximum staleness (heartbeat interval)
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Price Report]
A price report $\rho = (feedId, price, timestamp, reporter, \sigma)$ where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $feedId$: identifier of the target feed
    \item $price \in \mathbb{Q}^+$: reported price
    \item $timestamp \in \mathbb{N}$: reporter's observation time
    \item $reporter \in \mathcal{R}$: reporting node
    \item $\sigma$: reporter's signature over $(feedId, price, timestamp)$
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Trust Model}

We assume at most $f < n/3$ reporters are adversarial. Adversarial reporters may:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Report arbitrary prices
    \item Collude with other adversarial reporters
    \item Delay or withhold reports
    \item Observe honest reports before submitting
\end{itemize}

Honest reporters observe the true price $p^*$ with independent noise $\epsilon_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$.

\section{Data Aggregation}

\subsection{Median Aggregation}

OracleVM uses the median of reported prices as the canonical value:

\begin{definition}[Aggregated Price]
Given reports $\{\rho_1, \ldots, \rho_m\}$ with prices $\{p_1, \ldots, p_m\}$:
\[
p_{agg} = \text{median}(\{p_1, \ldots, p_m\})
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Manipulation Resistance]
With $n$ reporters and $f < n/3$ adversarial, the aggregated price deviation from truth is bounded:
\[
|p_{agg} - p^*| \leq \max_{i \in \text{honest}} |p_i - p^*|
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The median of $n$ values is determined by the middle value(s). With $f < n/3$ adversarial reporters, at least $n - f > 2n/3$ are honest. Since honest reporters form the majority, the median must fall within the range of honest reports. The honest report furthest from $p^*$ bounds the deviation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Outlier Detection}

Before aggregation, OracleVM filters statistical outliers using the Median Absolute Deviation (MAD):

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Outlier Detection}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Reports $\{\rho_1, \ldots, \rho_m\}$, threshold $k$
\Ensure Filtered reports
\State $prices \gets \{p_1, \ldots, p_m\}$
\State $\mu \gets \text{median}(prices)$
\State $MAD \gets \text{median}(\{|p_i - \mu| : p_i \in prices\})$
\State $\sigma_{robust} \gets 1.4826 \cdot MAD$ \Comment{Consistent estimator}
\For{each $\rho_i$}
    \If{$|p_i - \mu| > k \cdot \sigma_{robust}$}
        \State Mark $\rho_i$ as outlier
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return non-outlier reports
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The constant $1.4826$ makes MAD a consistent estimator of standard deviation for normal distributions.

\begin{lemma}[Outlier Detection Robustness]
The MAD-based outlier detector has breakdown point $0.5$, meaning up to $50\%$ of reports can be adversarial before the detector fails.
\end{lemma}

\subsection{Weighted Aggregation}

For feeds requiring higher precision, OracleVM supports stake-weighted medians:

\begin{definition}[Weighted Median]
Given reports with prices $\{p_i\}$ and weights $\{w_i\}$ (reporter stakes):
\[
p_{weighted} = \arg\min_p \sum_i w_i |p_i - p|
\]
Equivalently, $p_{weighted}$ is the price where cumulative weight reaches $\sum w_i / 2$.
\end{definition}

\section{Freshness Guarantees}

\subsection{Staleness Model}

\begin{definition}[Staleness]
The staleness of a price feed at time $t$ is:
\[
stale(F, t) = t - \max_{\rho \in F.reports} \rho.timestamp
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Freshness Requirement]
A consumer contract specifies maximum acceptable staleness $\tau_{max}$. A price is fresh if:
\[
stale(F, t_{current}) \leq \tau_{max}
\]
\end{definition}

\subsection{Heartbeat Mechanism}

Reporters must submit updates at minimum every $\tau$ seconds (heartbeat interval), regardless of price movement:

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Update Trigger}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Current price $p_{current}$, last reported $p_{last}$, time since last $\Delta t$
\Require Deviation threshold $\theta$, heartbeat $\tau$
\If{$|p_{current} - p_{last}| / p_{last} > \theta$}
    \State \Return DEVIATION\_TRIGGER
\ElsIf{$\Delta t > \tau$}
    \State \Return HEARTBEAT\_TRIGGER
\Else
    \State \Return NO\_UPDATE
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Signed Timestamps}

Each report includes a signed timestamp from the reporter:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Go]
type SignedReport struct {
    FeedID    FeedID
    Price     *big.Int  // Fixed-point with 18 decimals
    Timestamp uint64    // Unix timestamp (seconds)
    Reporter  Address
    Signature []byte    // Sign(feedID || price || timestamp)
}
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{theorem}[Timestamp Authenticity]
Under the unforgeability of the signature scheme, a valid signed report with timestamp $t$ proves the reporter observed price $p$ at time $t$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Slashing Conditions}

Reporters are slashed for:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Deviation from consensus}: Report differs from aggregated value by more than $\delta_{slash}$
    \item \textbf{Staleness}: Missing heartbeat deadline
    \item \textbf{Timestamp manipulation}: Report timestamp differs from block timestamp by more than $\Delta_{max}$
\end{enumerate}

\begin{definition}[Slashing Amount]
For violation severity $s \in [0, 1]$:
\[
slash(s) = \min(S_{reporter} \cdot s \cdot \alpha, S_{reporter})
\]
where $\alpha$ is the slashing multiplier (default: $0.1$).
\end{definition}

\subsection{Reward Distribution}

Accurate reporters receive rewards from:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Consumer fees}: Protocols pay per query
    \item \textbf{Protocol inflation}: Fixed LUX emission to oracle subnet
    \item \textbf{Slashed funds}: Redistributed to honest reporters
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}[Incentive Compatibility]
Under the staking and reward mechanism, honest reporting is a Nash equilibrium when:
\[
E[\text{reward}] > E[\text{slash penalty}] + c_{attack}
\]
where $c_{attack}$ is the cost of executing a price manipulation attack.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Update Flow}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Price Update Round}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{Collection Phase} ($\Delta t_1$):
\For{each reporter $r \in \mathcal{R}$}
    \State $r$ observes price from data sources
    \State $r$ signs and submits report to OracleVM
\EndFor
\State \textbf{Aggregation Phase}:
\State Collect all reports for feed $F$
\State Apply outlier detection
\State Compute $p_{agg} = \text{median}(filtered\_reports)$
\State Update $F.price \gets p_{agg}$
\State Increment $F.roundID$
\State \textbf{Settlement Phase}:
\State Identify reporters deviating from consensus
\State Apply slashing penalties
\State Distribute rewards to accurate reporters
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Security Analysis}

\subsection{Attack Scenarios}

\textbf{Flash Loan Price Manipulation}:
OracleVM prices are independent of on-chain DEX state. Flash loans cannot affect oracle prices within a single transaction.

\textbf{Reporter Collusion}:
With $f < n/3$ colluding reporters, median aggregation bounds price deviation to honest reporter range (Theorem 3.1).

\textbf{Data Source Compromise}:
Reporters should use multiple independent data sources. OracleVM's outlier detection filters compromised sources.

\textbf{Staleness Attacks}:
Consumers specify $\tau_{max}$; queries fail if price exceeds staleness bound.

\subsection{Formal Guarantees}

\begin{theorem}[Price Accuracy Bound]
Let $p^*$ be the true market price and $\sigma$ the standard deviation of honest reporter noise. With probability $1 - \delta$:
\[
|p_{agg} - p^*| \leq \sigma \cdot \Phi^{-1}\left(1 - \frac{\delta}{2(n-2f)}\right)
\]
where $\Phi^{-1}$ is the inverse normal CDF.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Liveness]
If at least $n - f$ reporters are online and honest, price updates occur within $\tau + \Delta_{network}$ where $\Delta_{network}$ is network propagation delay.
\end{theorem}

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Chainlink} pioneered decentralized oracles with off-chain reporting. OracleVM improves latency through native VM integration.

\textbf{Uniswap TWAP} provides manipulation-resistant prices from on-chain liquidity. OracleVM offers broader asset coverage independent of DEX liquidity.

\textbf{Pyth Network} achieves low latency through specialized infrastructure. OracleVM provides comparable latency within the Lux consensus model.

\textbf{Band Protocol} uses delegated proof of stake for reporter selection. OracleVM's direct staking model simplifies the security analysis.

\section{Conclusion}

OracleVM provides manipulation-resistant, low-latency price feeds as a native Lux Network primitive. Through median aggregation with outlier detection, cryptographic freshness proofs, and economic security via staking, OracleVM achieves provable accuracy bounds under Byzantine adversaries. With sub-second update latency and configurable staleness thresholds, OracleVM meets the requirements of sophisticated DeFi applications while maintaining decentralization guarantees.

Future work includes support for arbitrary data types beyond prices, ZK proofs for private reporting, and integration with hardware security modules for enhanced key protection.
