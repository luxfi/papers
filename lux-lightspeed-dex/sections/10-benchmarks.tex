\section{Benchmarks}
\label{sec:benchmarks}

This section consolidates the measured results from Sections~\ref{sec:gpu-matching},
\ref{sec:settlement}, and~\ref{sec:rollup} into one table, states the hardware and method,
and is explicit about which numbers are measured and which are aggregations or projections.

\subsection{Hardware and method}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{AMD Radeon 8060S} (coalesced SoA preload; fastest matcher measured).
  \item \textbf{NVIDIA GB10} (Grace-Blackwell, 128\,GB unified memory, \texttt{sm\_121},
  CUDA 13).
  \item \textbf{Apple M4 Max} (128\,GB unified memory, Metal).
  \item \textbf{Apple M1 Max} (Go reference, CPU settlement, verifier rates).
\end{itemize}

Matching and settlement throughput are reported as sustained rates over runs of tens of
millions of operations, with the GPU matching figures gated by byte-exact parity against the
CPU oracle (Section~\ref{sec:gpu-matching}). We report per-match latency where it is the
governing quantity. No figure in this paper is taken from a simulation or scaled by a fudge
factor.

\subsection{The two levers, restated}

The tuned matcher combines two compounding levers: the Knuth Algorithm~D division (per lane,
$\sim$8--12$\times$ over 512-iteration bit-serial, Table~\ref{tab:division}) and a coalesced
structure-of-arrays arena layout that makes the kernel throughput-bound rather than
latency-bound. An earlier draft, benchmarking an un-coalesced kernel, reported the matcher as
dependency-latency bound at hundreds of millions of matches/s; the coalesced layout lifts
sustained throughput into the billions of orders/sec per device---9.13\,B on a GB10 and
12.76\,B on an AMD Radeon 8060S (Table~\ref{tab:gpu-throughput}).

\subsection{What we do not claim}

We do not claim an end-to-end latency derived from invented co-location round-trips, or any
uptime statistic from a deployment we did not run. The single-device matching rates are
measured and byte-parity-gated; fleet and fabric aggregation
(Section~\ref{subsec:billion-matching}) scales them linearly, and settlement finality is a
separate axis (Section~\ref{sec:rollup}).
