\section{Benchmarks}
\label{sec:benchmarks}

This section consolidates the measured results from Sections~\ref{sec:gpu-matching},
\ref{sec:settlement}, and~\ref{sec:rollup} into one table, states the hardware and method,
and is explicit about which numbers are measured and which are aggregations or projections.

\subsection{Hardware and method}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{NVIDIA GB10} (Grace-Blackwell, 128\,GB unified memory, \texttt{sm\_121},
  CUDA 13).
  \item \textbf{Apple M4 Max} (128\,GB unified memory, Metal).
  \item \textbf{Apple M1 Max} (Go reference, CPU settlement, verifier rates).
\end{itemize}

Matching and settlement throughput are reported as sustained rates over runs of tens of
millions of operations, with the GPU matching figures gated by byte-exact parity against the
CPU oracle (Section~\ref{sec:gpu-matching}). We report per-match latency where it is the
governing quantity. No figure in this paper is taken from a simulation or scaled by a fudge
factor.

\subsection{The dominant lever, restated}

The single largest contributor to matching throughput is the replacement of the
512-iteration bit-serial \texttt{uint256} VWAP division with Knuth Algorithm~D, which alone
yields roughly $8\times$ on the GB10 and $12\times$ on the M4 Max (Table~\ref{tab:division}).
The matcher is dependency-latency bound, not occupancy or bandwidth bound: at peak the GB10
draws a fraction of its power envelope while reporting full GPU utilization, the signature of
a critical path waiting on data dependencies rather than starved of work.

\subsection{What we do not claim}

We do not claim a single-device rate above $\sim$250\,M matches/s for byte-exact
\texttt{uint256} matching, an end-to-end latency derived from invented co-location
round-trips, or any uptime statistic from a deployment we did not run. The honest ceilings
and the two routes past $10^{9}$ matches/s (fleet aggregation or a cheaper fixed-point
representation) are stated in Section~\ref{subsec:billion-matching}; the separation of
finality from matching is stated in Section~\ref{sec:rollup}.
