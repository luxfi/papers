\section{Signed-Quote RFQ and Atomic Settlement}
\label{sec:rfq}

The request-for-quote path is where universal liquidity earns its
correctness. We give the order object, the five-step lifecycle, the
on-chain checks, and the two theorems that make the path safe for both
parties.

\subsection{The signed order}

\begin{definition}[Quote order]
\label{def:order}
A quote order is the tuple
\[
\mathcal{O} = (\mathit{tokenIn},\ \mathit{tokenOut},\
\mathit{amountIn},\ \mathit{amountOut},\ \mathit{expiry},\
\mathit{nonce},\ \mathit{taker}),
\]
signed by the market maker under its ML-DSA-65 key to produce
$\sigma_{\mathrm{MM}} = \mathrm{Sign}_{sk_{\mathrm{MM}}}(\mathcal{O})$.
The market maker's commitment is precisely: ``a transaction submitted
before \emph{expiry} that delivers \emph{amountIn} of \emph{tokenIn}
will receive at least \emph{amountOut} of \emph{tokenOut}.''
\end{definition}

It is the \emph{maker} who signs the price. The taker authorizes the
movement of their own funds separately --- via a Permit2 signature
(\cite{permit2}) --- which is a gasless approval, not a price commitment.
This separation is the crux: the maker is bound to the price, the taker
is bound to the spend, and neither can unilaterally alter the other's
obligation.

\subsection{Lifecycle}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Quote.} The taker's frontend requests a price for
  $\mathit{amountIn}$ of $A\to B$. The backend reads the reference
  price, adds spread, \emph{reserves} the required $B$ inventory
  (\S\ref{sec:rfq-inv}), and returns a signed order $\mathcal{O}$ with a
  short expiry.

  \item \textbf{Sign (taker).} The taker signs a Permit2 message
  authorizing the settlement contract to pull $\mathit{amountIn}$ of
  $A$. This is off-chain and gasless.

  \item \textbf{Submit.} The backend relays one transaction to the
  settlement contract carrying $(\mathcal{O}, \sigma_{\mathrm{MM}},
  \mathit{permit2}_{\mathrm{taker}})$. The relayer pays gas; its cost is
  folded into the spread.

  \item \textbf{Settle (atomic).} The contract performs the checks
  below and, if all pass, pulls $A$ from the taker and delivers
  $\mathit{amountOut}$ of $B$ from maker inventory in the same call ---
  fully or not at all.

  \item \textbf{Hedge.} The backend restocks by trading $A\to B$ on the
  external venue to flatten the inventory move from step~4.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{On-chain checks (the settlement predicate)}

The settlement contract accepts $(\mathcal{O}, \sigma_{\mathrm{MM}},
\mathit{permit2})$ iff all of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(C\arabic*),leftmargin=3.2em]
  \item $\mathrm{Verify}_{pk_{\mathrm{MM}}}(\mathcal{O},
  \sigma_{\mathrm{MM}})$ holds --- the maker signed this exact order;
  \item $\mathit{block.timestamp} \le \mathcal{O}.\mathit{expiry}$ ---
  the quote is live;
  \item $\mathcal{O}.\mathit{nonce}$ is unused, then marked used ---
  no replay;
  \item the Permit2 pull of $\mathit{amountIn}$ from the taker
  succeeds; and
  \item maker inventory holds $\ge \mathit{amountOut}$ of
  $\mathit{tokenOut}$.
\end{enumerate}
Any failure reverts the entire transaction, restoring both balances.

\subsection{Inventory reservation}
\label{sec:rfq-inv}

A quote must be backed by inventory at the moment it is issued, not
hoped for at settlement. The backend reserves $\mathit{amountOut}$ of
$B$ against the live quote and releases the reservation on expiry or
fill. Without reservation a maker can sign more deliverable output than
it holds; check (C5) then turns oversubscription into user-visible
reverts. Reservation moves the failure to quote time, where it is
invisible to the taker (the maker simply quotes a smaller size or
declines).

\subsection{The two safety theorems}

\begin{theorem}[Quote integrity]
\label{thm:integrity}
If the settlement transaction succeeds, the taker receives at least
$\mathcal{O}.\mathit{amountOut}$ of $\mathit{tokenOut}$ for exactly
$\mathcal{O}.\mathit{amountIn}$ of $\mathit{tokenIn}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Success requires (C1)--(C5). (C1) binds the realized amounts to the
signed $\mathcal{O}$; (C4) fixes the input at $\mathit{amountIn}$; the
delivery in step~4 transfers $\mathit{amountOut}$ gated by (C5).
Atomicity makes the transfer all-or-nothing, so a successful
transaction realizes exactly the signed amounts. The maker cannot
substitute a worse price because any other amounts produce a different
$\mathcal{O}'$ for which $\sigma_{\mathrm{MM}}$ fails (C1).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[No free option]
\label{thm:nooption}
A taker holding a signed order $\mathcal{O}$ cannot execute it after
$\mathcal{O}.\mathit{expiry}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Execution requires (C2), $\mathit{block.timestamp} \le
\mathcal{O}.\mathit{expiry}$, checked against the inclusion block's
timestamp. After expiry every inclusion fails (C2) and reverts. The
window in which the quote is exercisable is therefore bounded by
$\mathcal{O}.\mathit{expiry} - t_{\mathrm{quote}}$, which the maker sets
to the order of seconds. The taker cannot hold the quote as a
free option on adverse price movement.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Hedge risk is the spread's job}
\label{sec:rfq-risk}

Between the on-chain fill (time $T$) and the completed external hedge
(time $T+\delta$) the reference price drifts. The maker's profit on a
filled order is
\[
\Pi \;=\; \underbrace{s\cdot \mathrm{ref}_{T}\cdot x}_{\text{spread captured}}
\;-\; \underbrace{\big(\mathrm{ref}_{T+\delta}-\mathrm{ref}_{T}\big)\cdot x}_{\text{hedge slippage}}
\;-\; g,
\]
with $g$ the relayer gas. The spread $s$ must dominate the expected
hedge slippage over the latency $\delta$ plus gas; for volatile pairs
this means a wider $s$ or pre-held inventory rather than
quote-then-hedge. Two operational invariants follow:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Hedge failure is not fill failure.} The on-chain fill
  is final the instant it confirms; if the subsequent external hedge
  fails (venue outage, insufficient balance) the maker is left
  directionally exposed and must retry or hedge on a secondary venue.
  The settlement path must never make the user's fill contingent on the
  maker's hedge.
  \item \textbf{Spread is priced for $\delta$, not for $0$.} A spread
  computed as if the hedge were instantaneous systematically loses on
  the tail of $\delta$; the maker prices the latency, not the mid.
\end{itemize}
