\begin{abstract}
We present the Wave protocol, a metastable consensus mechanism that achieves decision stability
through confidence monotonicity in directed acyclic graph (DAG) transaction structures.
Wave extends the Slush stochastic sampling primitive with persistent confidence counters
that track the cumulative history of supermajority observations per conflict color.
We prove that once a confidence counter for color $c$ exceeds a threshold $\beta_1$,
it can never be overtaken by a competing color under honest majority assumptions,
establishing \emph{confidence monotonicity} as a formal invariant.
The protocol achieves $O(\log n)$ convergence rounds with probability $1 - e^{-\Omega(k)}$
where $k$ is the sample size, tolerating up to $f < n/3$ Byzantine validators.
We formalize the DAG ancestry traversal that determines strong preference---a transaction
is strongly preferred only if every ancestor in its DAG cone is itself preferred---and
prove that strong preference is monotone under the Wave dynamics.
Our analysis introduces a Markov chain model of the confidence counter evolution,
deriving closed-form expressions for the expected convergence time and the probability
of decision reversal as a function of the adversarial stake fraction $\gamma$.
We demonstrate that for $k = 20$, $\alpha = 0.8$, and $\beta_1 = 11$, the probability
of a safety violation is bounded by $2^{-128}$ against an adversary controlling
$\gamma = 0.2$ of the validator set. Empirical evaluation on a 500-node testnet
confirms sub-second convergence with 99.97\% of transactions finalizing within 4 rounds.
\end{abstract}
