\begin{abstract}
We present Lux, a hybrid post-quantum consensus architecture for DAG-native chains. Lux separates fast operational finality from durable post-quantum finality. Validators emit Photons over a Nebula DAG substrate; classical BLS aggregates form Beams for low-latency confirmation, while ML-DSA attestations and Pulsar lattice-threshold Pulses provide post-quantum finality over Nebula roots. Prism binds all certificate lanes to the same transcript, and Quasar reaches Horizon finality when the bound lanes verify.

To support dynamic validator sets, Lux introduces LSS, a lifecycle framework for threshold cryptography that manages generations, snapshots, rollback, and activation across independent threshold kernels. Pulsar instantiates LSS with a two-round Module-LWE lattice threshold kernel under a SHA3 transcript profile; Lens provides the curve-threshold counterpart. Pulsar's distinguishing target is \emph{byte-equality with FIPS~204 ML-DSA}: its combined threshold output is byte-equal to a single-party ML-DSA-65/87 signature, achieved through a boundary-cleared nonce transcript with carry elimination (BCC/CEF, Section~\ref{sec:protocol:bcc}) that recovers the FIPS hint from public data and never reconstructs the secret-bearing intermediates $c\mathbf{s}_2$, $c\mathbf{t}_0$, or the full commitment $\mathbf{w}$. Warp 2.0 extends this finality model across chains by carrying Pulse-backed source finality in cross-chain message envelopes. The result is a coherent architecture for sovereign, co-validating, and validity-based chains under a hybrid classical/post-quantum security model.

\textbf{Status (\texttt{v1.2.0}).} Pulsar's byte-equal threshold construction --- which we call \emph{TALUS} (threshold ML-DSA composed of Boundary Clearance, Carry Elimination, and a dealerless nonce DKG) --- is realised at \texttt{github.com/luxfi/pulsar} tag \texttt{v1.2.0}. The combined threshold output verifies byte-equal under the \emph{unmodified} \texttt{cloudflare/circl} and \texttt{pq-crystals} FIPS~204 verifiers (ML-DSA-65 and ML-DSA-87). The earlier W-leak hazard --- a nonce certificate exposing the full commitment $\mathbf{w}$ --- is \emph{closed} on the TALUS-MPC path by the CSCP CarryCompare secure-comparison circuit (\S\ref{sec:protocol:bcc:cscp}): the $\mathsf{HighBits}$ decision is evaluated over secret-shared $\mathbf{w}$ so that no party forms $\mathbf{w}_0$ or the full $\mathbf{w}$, even transiently. This closure is proven \emph{semi-honest} in an in-process $N$-party simulation; a malicious party can at worst force an abort/retry (liveness), never a forge or a leak, because $\mathsf{FindHint}$ and the $\mathsf{ReleaseGate}$ reject any deviation. Two residuals are stated honestly: \textbf{(A)} the malicious-secure CSCP layer and a networked (non-simulation) MPC deployment remain open; \textbf{(B)} a \emph{dealerless} byte-FIPS-204 \emph{key} generation is provably unreachable (an additive sum of $N$ FIPS secrets escapes the small-norm set $S_\eta$, \S\ref{sec:protocol:bcc:keydkg}), so key generation stays trusted-dealer and the permissionless-public guarantee is carried by the natively-dealerless Corona leg \cite{corona} in the Quasar dual-PQ certificate.

\textbf{Honest claim.} The crypto primitives in this paper are not new --- proactive secret sharing, secret redistribution, verifiable redistribution, two-round lattice threshold signing, and identifiable-abort lattice threshold signing all exist in prior work. \emph{What is new is the composition}: a permissionless-consensus deployment architecture that turns static two-round PQ threshold signatures into a dynamic, leaderless, validator-rotation-tolerant finality layer. The paper specifies that systems layer at a level of detail sufficient to reproduce the Go canonical at \texttt{github.com/luxfi/pulsar} byte-for-byte across C++, Metal, CUDA, and WGSL ports.

This paper is the formal companion to LP-4450 \cite{lp4450} (canonical Pulsar) and the historical LP-073; the wire-format, lifecycle, byte-equality, and security claims here are normative. Pulsar and Corona \cite{corona} are sibling Module-LWE threshold schemes sharing the two-round kernel but differing in parameter regime and standard conformance: Corona is the Ringtail-derived leg (its own parameter set, $\sim 33$~KB signatures), while Pulsar is tuned for byte-equality with FIPS~204 ML-DSA via BCC/CEF. The canonical claims/evidence table and the ten architectural commitments live in LP-105 (Lux Stack Lexicon); other Lux documents reference them by section, never duplicate them.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Reproducibility}
\textbf{Source code.} \texttt{github.com/luxfi/pulsar} at tag \texttt{v1.2.0}; LSS adapter at \texttt{github.com/luxfi/threshold}; Quasar composition at \texttt{github.com/luxfi/consensus} (tag \texttt{v1.26.0}).\\
\textbf{Build.} \texttt{GOWORK=off go build ./...} and \texttt{GOWORK=off go test ./...} at \texttt{v1.2.0}. The TALUS path lives in \texttt{ref/go/pkg/pulsar/talus\_*.go}; the CSCP leak-free circuit and the byte-equal stock-verify ceremony are exercised by \texttt{TestCSCP\_*} and \texttt{TestTalus\_MPC\_EndToEnd\_StockVerify} / \texttt{TestTalus\_MPC\_Mode87}.\\
\textbf{Production threshold path (BCC/CEF).} The canonical no-leak threshold-signing path is the boundary-cleared / carry-elimination construction of Section~\ref{sec:protocol:bcc}: every secret-dependent ML-DSA decision is moved either offline and message-independent (boundary clearance on the aggregate nonce $\mathbf{w}$) or onto public data ($\mathsf{FindHint}$ over $\mathbf{w}' = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{z} - c\mathbf{t}_1 2^d$), so the protocol never reconstructs $c\mathbf{s}_2$, $c\mathbf{t}_0$, $\mathbf{r}_0$, or the full commitment $\mathbf{w}$, yet produces signatures byte-identical to single-party FIPS~204 ML-DSA-65/87. The \texttt{deriveKeyMaterial} expansion takes $\mathbf{A}$ directly in NTT domain (matching circl \texttt{NewKeyFromSeed} byte-for-byte). The headline cross-check is that a no-leak BCC/CEF signature decodes and verifies under the reference FIPS~204 verifier on the corresponding ML-DSA public key, demonstrated against both \texttt{cloudflare/circl} (\texttt{mldsa65}, \texttt{mldsa87}) and a \texttt{pq-crystals} cgo binding.

\textbf{Superseded path (V13, removed).} The earlier \texttt{v0.3 AlgebraicAggregate} construction broadcast the secret-bearing intermediates $\mathbf{C}\mathbf{S}_2 = c\mathbf{s}_2$ and $\mathbf{C}\mathbf{T}_0 = c\mathbf{t}_0$ to a combiner so it could run the FIPS sign-side rejection sequence and $\mathsf{MakeHint}$ verbatim. Broadcasting $c\mathbf{s}_2$ / $c\mathbf{t}_0$ \emph{leaks long-term key material} (the \textbf{V13 finding}, \texttt{PULSAR-V13-HINT-LEAK}; a sibling \texttt{PULSAR-V13-W-LEAK} exposed the full $\mathbf{w}$ in a nonce certificate). That construction is \emph{removed} --- no \texttt{AlgebraicAggregate} definition remains in the tree. TALUS (BCC $+$ CEF $+$ CSCP, \S\ref{sec:protocol:bcc}) is the no-leak replacement; the CSCP CarryCompare circuit closes \texttt{PULSAR-V13-W-LEAK} on the TALUS-MPC path so that no party forms $\mathbf{w}_0$ or the full $\mathbf{w}$, even transiently.

\textbf{Honest status (\texttt{v1.2.0}).} The byte-equal threshold signer is realised and verifies under stock FIPS~204 (\texttt{cloudflare/circl} and \texttt{pq-crystals}, ML-DSA-65/87). Two residuals remain, stated honestly: \textbf{(A)} CSCP is proven leak-free in the \emph{semi-honest} model in an in-process $N$-party simulation --- a malicious party is bounded to abort/retry (liveness) by $\mathsf{FindHint}$ and the $\mathsf{ReleaseGate}$, never a forge or a leak --- so the malicious-secure CSCP layer and a networked (non-simulation) MPC deployment are the open items; \textbf{(B)} dealerless byte-FIPS-204 \emph{key} generation is provably unreachable (\S\ref{sec:protocol:bcc:keydkg}), so key generation is trusted-dealer and the permissionless-public guarantee is delegated to the natively-dealerless Corona leg \cite{corona}. The stock FIPS~204 \emph{verifier} is always available.\\
