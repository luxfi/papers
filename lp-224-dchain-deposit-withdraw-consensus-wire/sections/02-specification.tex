\section{Specification}
\label{sec:spec}

This section specifies the carried custody payloads, their placement in
the block and DAG vertex, replay protection, the network-upgrade
activation height, and the strict backward-compatibility regime. The
specification is additive: the four frozen CLOB ZAP frames
(\texttt{clob\_ensure\_market}, \texttt{clob\_place},
\texttt{clob\_cancel}, \texttt{clob\_submit}) are untouched, and the
carried-fills section appended by the order-submission work is
unchanged. Custody uses the additive ZAP frames
\texttt{clob\_deposit}, \texttt{clob\_withdraw}, and
\texttt{clob\_open\_market}, which already exist on the venue rail; this
LP does not add or alter any ZAP frame --- it adds a \emph{carried
block payload} that records the results of those relays.

\subsection{Carried-payload model}

The proxy block already carries a self-delimiting fills section
appended after the transaction bytes, and the block identifier and the
DAG-vertex identifier already commit to that section (so tampering is
detectable). This LP adds two further carried sections, governed by the
same rules:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item The proposer produces the section once at \texttt{BuildBlock}, as
  the result of the venue relay it performs there.
\item The section is appended to the block bytes and to the DAG-vertex
  bytes, length-prefixed and self-delimiting.
\item The block identifier and the DAG-vertex identifier commit to the
  section (it is inside the hashed pre-image), so a proposer cannot
  carry one set of custody results in the bytes and a different set in
  the identifier.
\item Every validator applies the section deterministically at
  \texttt{Block.Accept}; no validator relays to the venue during
  \texttt{Verify} or \texttt{Accept}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Deposit acknowledgements}

A \texttt{DepositAck} records one import-then-credit. It is the datum
that lets every validator apply the identical credit to the venue
view without re-relaying.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Field} & \textbf{Type} & \textbf{Meaning} \\
\midrule
\texttt{account}      & \texttt{[16]byte} & D-Chain account the credit lands in \\
\texttt{asset}        & \texttt{[8]byte}  & asset identifier (matches ledger key) \\
\texttt{credited}     & \texttt{uint256}  & amount credited to \texttt{available} (wei-exact) \\
\texttt{source\_chain}& \texttt{uint32}   & originating chain (C or X) \\
\texttt{source\_utxo} & \texttt{[36]byte} & consumed UTXO reference (\texttt{txID[32]}$\|$\texttt{outIdx[4]}) \\
\texttt{tx\_index}    & \texttt{uint32}   & index of the deposit tx within the block \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{\texttt{DepositAck} carried fields. The credit applied by
every validator is \texttt{(account, asset) += credited}; the import
leg is identified by \texttt{(source\_chain, source\_utxo)} and is
consumed exactly once (Section~\ref{sec:replay}).}
\label{tab:depositack}
\end{table}

The proposer obtains a \texttt{DepositAck} by performing
\texttt{executeImport} (the atomic, consume-once debit of
\texttt{source\_utxo}) followed by the \texttt{clob\_deposit} relay,
which returns the credited amount and \emph{refuses if credited does
not equal imported}. The invariant \texttt{credited == imported} is
enforced at relay time by the venue and re-checkable by any validator
against the consumed UTXO's value (Section~\ref{sec:conservation}). The
carried \texttt{credited} is therefore not a free parameter: it is
pinned to the value of the consumed UTXO.

\subsection{Withdraw-realized / export commitments}

A \texttt{WithdrawCommit} records one debit-then-export. The export is
\emph{committed} in the block: the carried record names the exact
amount that will be released on the originating chain and the
identifier of the exported UTXO, so every validator applies the same
debit and the same export.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Field} & \textbf{Type} & \textbf{Meaning} \\
\midrule
\texttt{account}       & \texttt{[16]byte} & D-Chain account debited \\
\texttt{asset}         & \texttt{[8]byte}  & asset identifier \\
\texttt{realized}      & \texttt{uint256}  & amount debited from \texttt{available}
                                            and exported (wei-exact) \\
\texttt{dest\_chain}   & \texttt{uint32}   & destination chain (C or X) \\
\texttt{export\_utxo}  & \texttt{[36]byte} & exported UTXO id produced on \texttt{dest\_chain} \\
\texttt{tx\_index}     & \texttt{uint32}   & index of the withdraw tx within the block \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{\texttt{WithdrawCommit} carried fields. The debit applied by
every validator is \texttt{(account, asset) -= realized}; the export
leg releases exactly \texttt{realized} to \texttt{dest\_chain} as
\texttt{export\_utxo}.}
\label{tab:withdrawcommit}
\end{table}

The proposer obtains a \texttt{WithdrawCommit} by relaying
\texttt{clob\_withdraw}, which debits \texttt{available} and returns the
\emph{realized} amount \emph{clamped to the actually-available balance}
($\texttt{realized} \le \texttt{requested}$, and $\texttt{realized} \le
\texttt{available}$ at relay time), then performing
\texttt{executeExport} of exactly \texttt{realized}. The export leg
\emph{refuses if it would release more than realized}. Because the
realized amount is fixed in the carried record, every validator applies
the identical debit and the identical export; the moving-ledger
divergence of Section~1 cannot arise.

\subsection{Block / vertex layout}

The custody payload is two length-prefixed sub-sections appended after
the carried-fills section, preserving the existing self-delimiting
framing:

\begin{lstlisting}
block.Bytes() =
    txCount[4] | txBytes...                 // existing, self-delimiting
  | <carried-fills section>                 // existing (order-submission LP)
  | depositCount[4]  | depositCount  x DepositAck
  | withdrawCount[4] | withdrawCount x WithdrawCommit
  | sigLen[4] | sig                         // RESERVED (see below)
\end{lstlisting}

The DAG-vertex bytes carry the identical custody sub-sections, and the
vertex identifier computation folds them into the digest exactly as it
already folds the fills section. \texttt{DepositAck} and
\texttt{WithdrawCommit} are fixed-width, so each sub-section is fully
described by its \texttt{uint32} count prefix; the parser is
self-delimiting and rejects trailing or truncated bytes.

\subsection{Reuse of the reserved fill-signature field}
\label{sec:reserved}

The carried-fills layout already carved a single trailing
\texttt{sigLen[4] | sig} field --- empty in the default operator-venue
mode, reserved for the trustless path in which the venue signs its
emitted results and a P3Q~\cite{lp218,lp219} / STARK-FRI~\cite{lp221}
verifier checks them, so that no \emph{further} wire bump is needed when
the trustless path activates.

This LP \textbf{extends the meaning of that same reserved field to
cover the custody sub-sections}: when populated, the signature is over
the concatenation of the fills section \emph{and} the
\texttt{DepositAck}/\texttt{WithdrawCommit} sub-sections, i.e.\ over all
proposer-relayed venue results in the block. No new signature field is
introduced. In the default mode the field stays empty and the block is
accepted under the proposer-trust surface of
Section~\ref{sec:security}; in the trustless mode --- a strictly higher
cert profile in the sense of LP-217~\cite{lp217} --- the field carries
the venue's signature and validators verify it before applying any
carried fills \emph{or} custody deltas. This keeps the trustless escape hatch a
single field for the whole carried-payload family --- one and only one
way to make the carried results trustless.

\subsection{Replay protection}
\label{sec:replay}

Custody must be safe against two replays: crediting a deposit twice and
exporting a withdraw twice. Both reuse machinery already present; this
LP only binds the carried payloads to it.

\paragraph{Deposit double-credit.} The import leg consumes
\texttt{source\_utxo} from cross-chain shared memory exactly once: a
consumed UTXO cannot be consumed again, and the consumed-UTXO set is
part of the state the block's state root commits to. A
\texttt{DepositAck} whose \texttt{source\_utxo} is already in the
consumed set is invalid; a block carrying it is rejected. Additionally,
the venue's per-transaction \texttt{seen:<txID>} idempotency record
(content-addressed, established by the order-submission work and the
custody rail) makes a re-relayed \texttt{clob\_deposit} return the
\emph{original} outcome rather than crediting again. The two mechanisms
are complementary: the consumed-UTXO set prevents replaying the
\emph{import} (the value source), and \texttt{seen:<txID>} prevents
replaying the \emph{credit} (the venue effect). A deposit is therefore
credited at most once even across proposer re-builds, vertex re-issue,
or crash-before-commit.

\paragraph{Withdraw double-export.} The withdraw leg is bound the same
way from the other side. The debit of \texttt{available} occurs once on
the venue under \texttt{seen:<txID>}; a re-relayed \texttt{clob\_withdraw}
returns the original realized amount rather than debiting again. The
export leg produces \texttt{export\_utxo} exactly once into shared
memory; the same \texttt{export\_utxo} cannot be produced twice, and a
\texttt{WithdrawCommit} that names an already-produced
\texttt{export\_utxo} is invalid. A withdraw is therefore exported at
most once, with realized amount fixed in the carried record.

\paragraph{Determinism of replay outcomes.} Because both the
consumed-UTXO set and the \texttt{seen:<txID>} records are inputs the
state root commits to, two validators that disagree on whether a
deposit/withdraw was already applied would produce divergent roots ---
i.e.\ a replay-handling divergence is detected by the same state-root
check that detects every other custody divergence. There is no path by
which one validator credits/exports and another does not while both
accept the same block.

\subsection{Upgrade activation height}
\label{sec:activation}

The carried-custody payload format is a consensus-breaking change to
the block and DAG-vertex bytes and to the identifier pre-image. It
activates at a network-upgrade height $H$, exactly as other Lux network
upgrades are gated. $H$ is a single fixed height (per network) at which
all validators switch in lockstep; it is not negotiated per block and
not enabled by individual validator flags.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item For block height $h < H$: the carried-custody sub-sections MUST be
  absent. Deposit/withdraw proceeds as it does today --- proven against
  the venue VM directly and against the proxy atomic rail --- without
  being carried in the proxy consensus block.
\item For block height $h \ge H$: every deposit/withdraw effected by the
  block MUST be represented by a \texttt{DepositAck} /
  \texttt{WithdrawCommit} in the carried sub-sections. A post-$H$ block
  that effects a custody transition without carrying its record is
  invalid.
\end{itemize}

The activation height is published in the network upgrade descriptor
and is the same on every node; nodes that have not adopted the upgrade
descriptor cannot follow the chain past $H$, which is the intended
forcing function for a lockstep validator upgrade.

\subsection{Backward compatibility and pre-upgrade rejection}
\label{sec:compat}

There is no ambiguous window. The format is gated strictly on $H$:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Height} & \textbf{Carried custody present} & \textbf{Validator action} \\
\midrule
$h < H$ & yes & \textbf{REJECT} (format not yet active) \\
$h < H$ & no  & accept (legacy framing) \\
$h \ge H$ & no (but block effects custody) & \textbf{REJECT} (record required) \\
$h \ge H$ & yes & accept (verify framing, identifier commitment, replay) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Strict pre-/post-$H$ gating. Pre-$H$ blocks MUST reject the new
carried-custody payloads; post-$H$ blocks MUST require them whenever a
custody transition occurs. No height accepts both shapes.}
\label{tab:gate}
\end{table}

Because the block and vertex identifiers commit to the carried sections,
a pre-$H$ node and a post-$H$ node compute \emph{different} identifiers
for any block that carries custody payloads --- so the upgrade cannot
silently half-apply. The forced divergence at $H$ is exactly the
lockstep behaviour Lux network upgrades rely on: there is no window in
which a custody transition is ambiguous as to whether it must be
carried.

\subsection{Forward compatibility with the trustless path}

The reserved signature field (Section~\ref{sec:reserved}) means the
trustless custody path does not require a second wire bump after $H$:
moving the venue from operator-trusted to signature-verified populates
the existing field. The activation of \emph{signature requirement} (as
opposed to format) is itself a later policy gate; this LP specifies the
format that makes that gate a flag flip, not a wire change. One wire,
two policies (format at $H$, trust later) --- no third format.
