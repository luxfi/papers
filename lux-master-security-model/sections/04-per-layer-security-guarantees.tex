\subsection{Layer 1: Consensus}

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Consensus layer security guarantees.}
\label{tab:consensus-security}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
\textbf{Property} & \textbf{Guarantee} & \textbf{Assumption} & \textbf{Status} \\
\midrule
Safety & No conflicting finalized blocks & $f < n/3$, co-CDH & [Proven] \\
Liveness & Blocks finalized within $\Delta$ & Partial synchrony & [Proven] \\
PQ safety & Epoch-level finality & Module-LWE & [Assumed] \\
Privacy & Anonymous validator votes & Module-LWE (Pulsar) & [Assumed] \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Triple-proof quantum finality} combines three independent proofs per epoch:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{BLS aggregate signature} (48 bytes): Classical speed, sub-millisecond
        verification. \textbf{[Proven]} secure under co-CDH in the random oracle
        model~\cite{boneh2001bls}.
  \item \textbf{STARK-compressed ML-DSA} ($\sim$200 bytes): Post-quantum identity
        binding. \textbf{[Assumed]} secure under Module-LWE (NIST FIPS 204). STARK
        soundness \textbf{[Proven]} under collision-resistant hash functions.
  \item \textbf{Pulsar threshold signature} (embedded in STARK): Anonymous
        validator participation. \textbf{[Assumed]} secure under Ring-LWE with
        parameters providing $> 128$-bit classical security.
\end{enumerate}

Total epoch proof size: 248 bytes. Epoch verification time: ${\sim}2$--$5$\,ms CPU
(dominated by Groth16's 3 pairings on BLS12-381)~\cite{lux-benchmarks}.
Block time: 1\,ms.

Hybrid key exchange (X25519 + ML-KEM-768) provides forward secrecy against quantum
adversaries as long as ephemeral ML-KEM private keys are erased after each session.
\textbf{[Tested]} via integration tests with simulated key compromise.

Application-layer security is the responsibility of smart contract developers, not the
protocol. The protocol provides tools (precompiles, gas metering, access control
primitives) but cannot prevent application-level logic errors.
