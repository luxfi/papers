\section{C++ EVM vs Go EVM}


\subsection{EVM-Only Throughput (Signatures Pre-Cached)}

To isolate interpreter performance, signatures are pre-verified and cached. Measurements
reflect pure EVM opcode dispatch and state access.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Implementation} & \textbf{Throughput} & \textbf{Best Run} & \textbf{vs Go} \\
\midrule
Go EVM (full StateDB)       & 4.2--5.4\,Ggas/s & 5.4\,Ggas/s  & 1.0$\times$ \\
C++ evmone++ (full state)   & 20.9\,Ggas/s     & 23.4\,Ggas/s & \textbf{5.1$\times$} \\
C++ evmone++ (interpreter)  & 416\,Ggas/s      & ---           & 96$\times$$^*$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{EVM-only gas throughput, signatures pre-cached. 10{,}000 transactions per run.
$^*$Interpreter-only number excludes state access entirely (in-memory balance tracking,
no trie, no DB); it represents the ceiling of opcode dispatch speed.}
\label{tab:cpp_vs_go}
\end{table}

The 5.1$\times$ speedup with full state comes from:
\begin{itemize}
\item No garbage collector pauses (C++ deterministic deallocation)
\item Cache-friendly memory layout (no GC headers per object)
\item Compiler-optimized opcode dispatch (computed \texttt{goto} vs switch table)
\item Inlined hash operations (no cgo FFI overhead)
\end{itemize}
