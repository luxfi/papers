\section{Full Operation Accounting: EVM, DEX, and FHE Primitives}
\label{sec:accounting}

For reproducibility and to prevent a single headline figure from standing in for a whole engine,
this section accounts for every primitive family the unified engine dispatches, with its measured
cost. The engine comprises \textbf{5} EVM CUDA kernels
(\texttt{block\_stm}, \texttt{evm\_kernel}, \texttt{keccak256}, \texttt{state\_table},
\texttt{tx\_validate}), the DEX matching and AMM kernels
(\texttt{dex\_swap}, \texttt{amm\_xyk}, plus per-backend deterministic CLOB matchers), and
\textbf{27} FHE CUDA kernels, alongside the post-quantum and classical signature primitives.
Unless noted, latencies are single-core / single-launch measurements; the accelerator and host
are stated per table.

\subsection{A necessary correction: two DEX matching models}
\label{sec:accounting-dex-honesty}

The billion-order figures of Section~\ref{sec:production} measure the \emph{batch} matching kernel
(\texttt{dex\_swap.cu::batch\_match\_kernel}: one thread-block per taker order, parallel across
orders)---the model appropriate for the AMM/swap and aggregated-liquidity path. It must not be
conflated with the \emph{deterministic CLOB} matcher that the consensus path uses. The latter is,
by deliberate design, launched \verb|<<<1,1>>>|---single-thread, single-block---on every backend
(CUDA \texttt{dex\_match\_order\_seq}, the HIP \texttt{hipLaunchKernelGGL(\ldots,dim3(1),dim3(1))},
and the Vulkan \texttt{vkCmdDispatch(1,1,1)}), because any intra-book parallelism would reorder
fills non-deterministically and fork the chain between validators. The GPU here buys
\emph{verifiable determinism}, not throughput: it is an oracle that two validators can re-execute
bit-for-bit, and its per-order latency---not its parallelism---is the figure of merit. Batch
throughput and deterministic-CLOB determinism are therefore \emph{orthogonal} services of the same
engine, and we report them separately rather than blending them.

We further note that the Apple-Metal batch figures in Table~\ref{tab:dex-gpu-cpu} are
dispatch- and submit-bound at this kernel's weight: 16\,M single-order thread-groups doing little
work each measure command-buffer and dispatch overhead more than matching compute. The CUDA/HIP
kernel-only figures avoid the submit component; none of these microbenchmarks should be read as
the sustained deep-book matching rate, which the occupancy analysis
(Section~\ref{sec:production-occupancy}) bounds.

\paragraph{Resolution: determinism and throughput are not in tension.}
The apparent conflict---single-thread determinism per book versus GPU throughput---dissolves once
the parallelism is placed on the correct axis. We map \emph{one thread per book}, each thread
matching its book's taker stream sequentially in strict price-time priority (identical to the
\verb|<<<1,1>>>| oracle, hence bit-for-bit re-executable), and launch thousands-to-millions of
such threads, one per market, so the GPU is saturated across books while each book remains
deterministic. Measured on Apple Silicon with a deep-book workload (\num{1000000} books, depth 32,
32 takers each) and a \textbf{GPU-versus-CPU parity check that matches exactly}
(\num{38000000} fills both sides):

dispatch-bound batch microbenchmark, this kernel does real work per thread (\num{32} takers $\times$
depth-\num{32} book walk) and is therefore compute-bound, sustaining full clocks even over a
headless session.

\paragraph{Blended EVM+DEX+FHE throughput on the pure-GPU two-node fabric.}
Because the three primitive families differ by orders of magnitude---a DEX match at
\SI{0.18}{\nano\second} is $\sim$\num{270000}$\times$ cheaper than a bootstrapped FHE gate at
\SI{50}{\micro\second}---the blended composite-transaction rate is set entirely by the mix
(Table~\ref{tab:acct-blend}). Matching is effectively free; EVM settlement is the knee; FHE bounds
only the confidential fraction.

\subsection{What this accounting shows}

Three facts survive the full accounting. First, the DEX \emph{batch} path and the DEX
\emph{deterministic} path are distinct services with distinct figures of merit---parallel
throughput versus verifiable single-thread determinism---and honesty requires reporting both.
Second, the FHE NTT primitive is the one place a large GPU speedup ($23.6\times$) is both measured
and load-bearing, because every homomorphic gate is NTT-bound. Third, the classical ecrecover, at
\SI{242}{\micro\second} and 87\% of EVM block time, remains the single highest-leverage
optimization target and the strongest argument for the GPU/parallel crypto path---an argument that
holds independently of the DEX and FHE results. These are the primitives from which the unified
engine's aggregate behaviour is composed; no single one is the engine.
