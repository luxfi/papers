\section{Full Operation Accounting: EVM, DEX, and FHE Primitives}
\label{sec:accounting}

For reproducibility and to prevent a single headline figure from standing in for a whole engine,
this section accounts for every primitive family the unified engine dispatches, with its measured
cost. The engine comprises \textbf{5} EVM CUDA kernels
(\texttt{block\_stm}, \texttt{evm\_kernel}, \texttt{keccak256}, \texttt{state\_table},
\texttt{tx\_validate}), the DEX matching and AMM kernels
(\texttt{dex\_swap}, \texttt{amm\_xyk}, plus per-backend deterministic CLOB matchers), and
\textbf{27} FHE CUDA kernels, alongside the post-quantum and classical signature primitives.
Unless noted, latencies are single-core / single-launch measurements; the accelerator and host
are stated per table.

\subsection{Determinism and throughput are not in tension}
\label{sec:accounting-dex-honesty}

The billion-order figures of Section~\ref{sec:production} measure the \emph{deterministic CLOB}
matcher that the consensus path actually uses---the one a validator re-executes bit-for-bit---not
an aggregated-liquidity approximation. This resolves an apparent conflict that an earlier framing
had treated as fundamental: that consensus determinism forces a single-thread
\verb|<<<1,1>>>| launch and therefore forfeits GPU throughput. It does not. Determinism constrains
only the ordering \emph{within} a book; it says nothing about the axis \emph{across} books. Placing
the parallelism on the correct axis---\textbf{one thread per book}, each thread matching its book's
taker stream sequentially in strict price-time priority (bit-for-bit re-executable), with
thousands-to-millions of such threads launched one per market---saturates the GPU across books
while every book stays deterministic. A separate \emph{batch}/AMM kernel
(\texttt{dex\_swap.cu}: one thread-block per taker order over aggregated liquidity) remains
available for the swap path, but it is a distinct, lighter service and not the consensus figure of
merit; we report the deterministic matcher as the headline.

The deterministic matcher was measured across all three GPU makers with a
\textbf{GPU-versus-CPU parity check that matches exactly} at every point (e.g.\ \num{38000000}
fills both sides at \num{1000000} books, depth 32, 32 takers each). The settlement-fabric headline
is in Table~\ref{tab:dex-gpu-cpu}: \SI{12.76}{\giga\text{ord}/\second} (AMD 8060S),
\SI{9.13}{\giga\text{ord}/\second} (GB10). The Apple-Silicon workstation figures, at the same
deep-book workload, are:

\noindent The consensus-path headline is the settlement fabric: \textbf{12.76\,billion
deterministic taker-orders/sec} on the AMD 8060S and \textbf{9.13\,billion} on the GB10

identical to the CPU oracle. This kernel does real work per thread (\num{32} takers $\times$
depth-\num{32} book walk) and is compute- and memory-bound, sustaining full clocks even over a
headless session.

\paragraph{Blended EVM+DEX+FHE throughput on the pure-GPU two-node fabric.}
Because the three primitive families differ by orders of magnitude---a DEX match at
\SI{0.18}{\nano\second} is $\sim$\num{270000}$\times$ cheaper than a bootstrapped FHE gate at
\SI{50}{\micro\second}---the blended composite-transaction rate is set entirely by the mix
(Table~\ref{tab:acct-blend}). Matching is effectively free; EVM settlement is the knee; FHE bounds
only the confidential fraction.

\subsection{What this accounting shows}

Three facts survive the full accounting. First, the DEX \emph{deterministic} matcher delivers
consensus-safe determinism \emph{and} billions of orders per second at once---the two are not in
tension once parallelism is placed across books rather than within them---so the consensus-path
matcher, not a lighter aggregated approximation, is the honest headline
(\SI{12.76}{\giga\text{ord}/\second} AMD, \SI{9.13}{\giga\text{ord}/\second} GB10, parity-verified).
Second, the FHE NTT primitive is the one place a large GPU speedup ($23.6\times$) is both measured
and load-bearing, because every homomorphic gate is NTT-bound. Third, the classical ecrecover, at
\SI{242}{\micro\second} and 87\% of EVM block time, remains the single highest-leverage
optimization target and the strongest argument for the GPU/parallel crypto path---an argument that
holds independently of the DEX and FHE results. These are the primitives from which the unified
engine's aggregate behaviour is composed; no single one is the engine.
