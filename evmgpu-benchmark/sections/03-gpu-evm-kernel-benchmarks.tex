\section{GPU EVM Kernel Benchmarks}


\subsection{Peak Throughput}

The GPU EVM kernel dispatches EVM opcodes as Metal compute shader workgroups. Each
transaction runs as an independent GPU thread with its own stack and program counter.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{CPU} & \textbf{GPU (Metal)} & \textbf{Speedup} \\
\midrule
Opcodes/sec & 17.7\,M & 85.9\,M & \textbf{4.85$\times$} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Peak EVM opcode throughput. GPU measured on 50k transactions $\times$
5{,}000 opcodes each (250M total opcodes). CPU measured on the same workload
using the Go EVM interpreter.}
\label{tab:peak}
\end{table}

\subsection{GPU Crossover Analysis}

GPU acceleration incurs fixed overhead for kernel launch, buffer allocation, and command
buffer encoding. This overhead dominates at low per-transaction complexity. The crossover
point---where GPU matches CPU---occurs at approximately 500 opcodes per transaction.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{rrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Ops/tx} & \textbf{CPU (ms)} & \textbf{GPU (ms)} & \textbf{GPU Speedup} \\
\midrule
100    & ---  & ---  & $<$1$\times$ (CPU wins) \\
500    & ---  & ---  & $\approx$1.0$\times$ (crossover) \\
2{,}000  & ---  & ---  & 2.08$\times$ \\
5{,}000  & ---  & ---  & 2.83$\times$ \\
10{,}000 & ---  & ---  & 3.09$\times$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{GPU speedup as a function of per-transaction opcode count. Below 500 ops/tx,
CPU is faster due to GPU launch overhead. Speedup increases with complexity as GPU
parallelism amortizes fixed costs. All measurements at 50k transactions.}
\label{tab:crossover}
\end{table}

\subsection{GPU Memory Scaling}

Unified memory on Apple Silicon eliminates explicit CPU--GPU transfers but imposes a
total memory budget. GPU memory consumption scales linearly with transaction count:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{rrrl}
\toprule
\textbf{Transactions} & \textbf{GPU Memory} & \textbf{Per-tx} & \textbf{Notes} \\
\midrule
10{,}000  & 732\,MB  & 73\,KB  & Fits any Apple GPU \\
50{,}000  & 3.7\,GB  & 74\,KB  & M1 Max 32GB+ \\
100{,}000 & 7.3\,GB  & 73\,KB  & M1 Max 64GB \\
\midrule
\multicolumn{2}{l}{Persistent state DB} & \multicolumn{2}{l}{$\sim$5\,GB} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{GPU memory consumption. Per-transaction overhead is stable at $\sim$73\,KB,
encompassing stack, memory, calldata, and MvMemory versioned state. Persistent state
(the GPU-resident hash table) adds $\sim$5\,GB for a full Ethereum-scale state trie.}
\label{tab:gpumem}
\end{table}

At 73\,KB per transaction, a 64\,GB unified memory system can process blocks of up to
$\sim$800{,}000 transactions in a single GPU batch (excluding persistent state). With the
5\,GB persistent state buffer, practical capacity is $\sim$700{,}000 transactions per block.
