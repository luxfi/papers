\subsection{Analysis}

Narwhal+Tusk achieves higher raw throughput than Quasar because its DAG-based mempool
enables parallel block proposals from all validators simultaneously~\cite{narwhal-tusk}.
Solana's throughput benefits from Proof-of-History pipelining, which amortizes leader
rotation cost~\cite{solana-wp}. However, both trade finality latency for throughput.

\textbf{Throughput-weighted finality} (TPS $\div$ finality in seconds) reveals a
different ranking:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Protocol} & \textbf{TPS} & \textbf{Finality (s)} & \textbf{TPS/s finality} \\
\midrule
\textbf{Quasar}   & 10{,}400 & 0.204 & \textbf{50{,}980} \\
Narwhal+Tusk      & 38{,}500 & 0.464 & 82{,}974 \\
Snow              & 4{,}800  & 0.920 & 5{,}217 \\
Tower BFT         & 48{,}000 & 12.8  & 3{,}750 \\
Tendermint        & 1{,}200  & 1.180 & 1{,}017 \\
HotStuff          & 5{,}100  & 1.640 & 3{,}110 \\
Casper FFG        & 30       & 768   & 0.039 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Throughput-weighted finality at $n=100$. Higher is better.}
\label{tab:tps-finality}
\end{table}

For applications requiring both high throughput and fast finality (DEX, payments, bridge
relays), Quasar occupies the optimal region of the throughput--finality Pareto frontier.
