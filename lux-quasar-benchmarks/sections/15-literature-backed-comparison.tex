\section{Literature-Backed Comparison Scaffold}
\label{sec:lit-backed-comparison}
%% ============================================================================

The benchmark tables in Sections~\ref{sec:finality}--\ref{sec:related} report
the Quasar engine on Lux internal testbeds. To make those numbers
\emph{independently falsifiable} against named competitors we publish here a
clean scaffold whose competitor columns are filled \textbf{from cited
published numbers}, and whose Quasar columns are explicitly
\texttt{[TBD-measure]} pending a single controlled re-run on the
competitor's own testbed shape. \textbf{This is the table that wins or
loses the ``better than any other solution'' claim.}

\subsection{Competitors and Why}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Mysticeti-C}~\cite{babel2024mysticeti} --- 2024 NDSS;
        published WAN-latency floor for non-PQ DAG-BFT.
  \item \textbf{HotStuff-2}~\cite{malkhi2023hotstuff2} --- 2023; ePrint;
        canonical linear-comm BFT.
  \item \textbf{Tendermint / CometBFT}~\cite{buchman2016tendermint,cometbftgithub}
        --- production reference BFT (every Cosmos zone).
  \item \textbf{Avalanche Snowman}~\cite{rocket2019avalanche,amores2024avalanche}
        --- probabilistic-finality prior art, the protocol Quasar's
        Photon/Wave layer descends from.
  \item \textbf{Solana TowerBFT}~\cite{yakovenko2018solana} --- highest
        public-network TPS but minute-scale economic finality.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Reproducibility Specification}
\label{sec:lit-repro}

The \texttt{[TBD]} cells in Table~\ref{tab:quasar-lit-comparison} are
fillable only with an apples-to-apples run. Spec:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Hardware.} 21- and 100-node WAN deployments; AWS
        \texttt{c6i.8xlarge} (32~vCPU Ice~Lake, 64\,GiB) across
        \texttt{us-east-1}, \texttt{eu-west-1}, \texttt{ap-southeast-1}
        --- matches \cite[\S6.1]{babel2024mysticeti}.
  \item \textbf{Workload.} 512\,B transactions, open-loop Poisson
        injector sweeping $\lambda \in \{10^3, 10^4, 10^5, 2\!\times\!10^5\}$
        TPS per the Mysticeti methodology.
  \item \textbf{Quasar build.} \texttt{luxfi/consensus@v0.1.0-rc1-pq-consensus-freeze},
        \texttt{luxfi/pulsar}, \texttt{luxfi/lens}, \texttt{luxfi/warp}
        at the same tag; GPU off (CPU-BLS) and GPU on (Metal/CUDA) as
        two distinct rows.
  \item \textbf{Competitor builds.}
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
    \item Mysticeti-C: \texttt{MystenLabs/sui@main}, \texttt{cargo run --release --bin orchestrator -- benchmark}.
    \item HotStuff-2: \texttt{nayak-lab/hotstuff-2@main}, default config.
    \item CometBFT: \texttt{cometbft/cometbft@v0.38}, \texttt{tm-load-test} workload.
  \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Metrics.} p50 / p99 commit latency at fixed $\lambda$;
        sustained TPS at p99 commit~$\leq$~1\,s; messages exchanged per
        commit measured at the OS socket level (\texttt{ss -i}).
  \item \textbf{Decision rule.} Publish the table with the actual numbers
        and the verdict (win / tie / lose) per cell. \emph{Do not} re-tag
        the Quasar paper with new headline numbers until every
        \texttt{[TBD]} is replaced by a measured value with a 95\%
        confidence interval over $\geq$~5 runs of $\geq$~600\,s each.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What this scaffold replaces.} The internal benchmarks in
Sections~\ref{sec:finality}--\ref{sec:related} report Quasar in isolation;
they are not a substitute for the cross-protocol re-run specified here.
The headline ``X$\times$ faster than Y'' claims in the abstract should be
treated as \emph{hypotheses to be tested} by the experiment in this
section, not as established facts.
