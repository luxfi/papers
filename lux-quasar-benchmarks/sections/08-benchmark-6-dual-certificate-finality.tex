\subsection{Overhead of Dual-Certificate vs. Single-Certificate}

To isolate the cost of dual-certificate finality, we ran Quasar in two modes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{BLS-only}: Classical single-certificate (no Pulsar).
\item \textbf{Dual-cert}: Full Quasar with both BLS and Pulsar.
\end{itemize}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Mode} & \textbf{Finality (ms)} & \textbf{TPS} & \textbf{Cert Size (B)} & \textbf{Overhead} \\
\midrule
BLS-only   & 192  & 11{,}200  & 144     & baseline \\
Dual-cert  & 204  & 10{,}400  & 1{,}168 & +6.3\% latency, $-$7.1\% TPS \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Cost of adding Pulsar post-quantum certificate to Quasar at $n=100$.}
\label{tab:dual-overhead}
\end{table}

\textbf{Key finding}: Dual-certificate finality adds only 12\,ms (6.3\%) latency and
reduces throughput by 7.1\%. The cost of quantum security is a $<$10\% performance
penalty---well within operational margin.

\subsection{Why the Overhead is Small}

Three design decisions minimize dual-certificate cost:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Piggybacking}: Pulsar shares are sent alongside BLS partial signatures
      in the same network message. No additional round trip.
\item \textbf{Threshold vs. multi-sig}: Pulsar combination is a single $\sim$7\,ms
      computation, not $n$ individual verifications.
\item \textbf{Parallelism}: BLS aggregation and Pulsar combination run concurrently
      on separate CPU cores. The finality deadline is
      $\max(\text{BLS}, \text{Pulsar})$, not their sum.
\end{enumerate}
