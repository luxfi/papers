\section{Benchmark 1: Finality Time}

\label{sec:finality}
%% ============================================================================

Finality time is measured as wall-clock elapsed time from transaction broadcast to the
point at which the transaction is irreversibly committed and cannot be reverted without
violating the protocol's safety assumptions.

\subsection{Definitions}

Protocols define ``final'' differently:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Quasar}: Block has both valid BLS aggregate certificate and valid Ringtail
      threshold certificate ($\geq 2n/3$ contributors each).
\item \textbf{Tendermint}: Block committed after \texttt{Precommit} phase with $\geq 2n/3$
      signatures~\cite{tendermint}.
\item \textbf{HotStuff}: Block finalized after 3-chain rule (propose, pre-commit,
      commit, decide)~\cite{hotstuff}.
\item \textbf{Narwhal+Tusk}: DAG vertex committed after Tusk's zero-message-overhead
      commit rule~\cite{narwhal-tusk}.
\item \textbf{Casper FFG}: Checkpoint finalized after two consecutive justified
      epochs~\cite{casper-ffg}. Each epoch = 32 slots $\times$ 12\,s = 384\,s.
\item \textbf{Tower BFT}: 32 layers of exponentially increasing vote lockout
      periods~\cite{solana-wp}.
\item \textbf{Snow}: Probabilistic finality after $\beta$ consecutive successful
      sampling rounds~\cite{snow-paper}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Analysis}

Quasar's finality advantage stems from two design decisions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Parallel certificate collection}: BLS and Ringtail signatures are collected
      concurrently. The critical path is the slower of the two (BLS aggregation at
      $\sim$195\,ms over WAN), not their sum.
\item \textbf{Responsive finality}: Like HotStuff, Quasar finalizes at actual network
      speed, not at a fixed timer. With 38\,ms median intra-cluster RTT, finality
      completes well under the block time.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{GPU BLS acceleration}: When validators use GPU-accelerated BLS aggregation
(Metal or CUDA shaders), the BLS round completes in $\sim$10\,ms instead of
$\sim$195\,ms. In single-datacenter deployments with sub-millisecond RTT, this
yields \textbf{10\,ms finality}---the primary target for co-located validator sets.
CPU BLS remains available as a 100\,ms fallback on commodity hardware.

Ethereum's Casper FFG is architecturally incapable of sub-minute finality: two full
epochs (12.8\,min) are required by design~\cite{casper-ffg}. Solana's Tower BFT
requires 32 layers of exponentially locked votes, producing $\sim$12.8\,s irreversible
finality regardless of network conditions~\cite{solana-wp}.
