\section{Why Securities Must Act Now}

\label{sec:urgency}
%% =====================================================================

\subsection{The 30-Year Argument}

A corporate bond issued in 2026 with a 30-year maturity expires in
2056.  The private key that signed the issuance transaction, the
signatures on every ownership transfer, and the key encapsulation
protecting confidential terms---all must remain secure until 2056.

Conservative estimates place CRQC availability at 2035--2045.  This
means a bond issued today with ECDSA signatures will have its
ownership history become forgeable during its lifetime.

\subsection{Harvest-Now, Decrypt-Later}

Nation-state adversaries are known to record encrypted communications
and signed transactions for future quantum decryption.  For securities:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item Recorded ECDSA signatures can be forged retroactively once
        the public key is recovered from the signature.
  \item Recorded ML-KEM ciphertexts (if not PQ-protected) can be
        decrypted, revealing confidential deal terms.
  \item Recorded key exchange transcripts (ECDH) can be replayed to
        recover session keys.
\end{itemize}

Post-quantum cryptography deployed today protects against harvest-now
attacks, even if CRQCs are decades away.

\subsection{Regulatory Imperative}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{NIST.}  Post-quantum standards finalized August 2024.
        Federal agencies must begin migration.
  \item \textbf{NSA CNSA 2.0.}  National Security Systems must support
        post-quantum algorithms by 2025 and exclusively use them by
        2033.
  \item \textbf{CISA.}  Issued guidance for critical infrastructure
        to inventory cryptographic dependencies and plan migration.
  \item \textbf{SEC.}  Rule 17a-4 record-keeping integrity depends
        on unforgeable signatures.  If ECDSA is broken, the records
        are compromised.
\end{itemize}
