\section{Disaster Recovery}

\label{sec:dr}
%% =====================================================================

The architecture distinguishes \emph{availability} loss (a share is
unreachable) from \emph{authority} loss (a share has been compromised).
Both must be recoverable; the recovery procedures differ.

\subsection{Sealed-Envelope Recovery Procedure}

The sealed-envelope recovery is the architecture's residual-risk
floor. It is exercised only when three or more cold shares are
simultaneously unrecoverable. The procedure:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item Custody Lead and a second authorised principal travel to all
three vault locations (US, Switzerland, Singapore).
\item Each envelope is inspected by both principals before opening;
tamper-evident seals are photographed.
\item The three Shamir shards $P_1, P_2, P_3$ are reassembled to
recover passphrase $P$.
\item $P$ is used to decrypt the M-Chain-stored encrypted ceremony
transcript.
\item The transcript is replayed inside a freshly provisioned SCIF
on freshly procured hardware; share material is reconstructed.
\item A new ceremony then runs to migrate to a new public key; the
old key is retired and any remaining assets at the old key are
moved.
\end{enumerate}

This procedure takes approximately seven days end-to-end and is
intentionally slow.

\subsection{Quarterly Live Drill}

A live disaster-recovery drill is run quarterly. One real share is
taken offline (cycled across shares quarter-to-quarter so that every
share is exercised over a multi-quarter window) and a real signing
ceremony is completed using the remaining quorum. The drill is
recorded on M-Chain and reviewed at the next quarterly board meeting.

\subsection{Insurance Interaction}

The crime-and-specie policy distinguishes \emph{authority} loss
(insured up to policy tower) from \emph{availability} loss (not
insured but bounded by SLAs). The architecture's recovery procedures
are designed so that an availability incident never escalates to an
authority incident: the time-lock on the hot tier, the four-eyes
review on the warm tier, and the airgap on the cold tier ensure that
an attacker cannot leverage an availability incident to forge a
signature.

\subsection{Business Continuity}

In the event of a sustained availability incident on the warm tier
(e.g.\ a multi-day cloud outage at two of three providers), trading
operations fall back to one of two contingencies:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Tier-2 elevation of a hot share.} A hot share's
spending cap can be temporarily raised, subject to Custody Lead
approval, to maintain trading-desk liquidity for up to seventy-two
hours.
\item \textbf{Cold-tier emergency draw.} A cold ceremony can be
scheduled within twenty-four hours to refill the warm tier from
cold reserves.
\end{enumerate}

Either contingency is logged and reviewed at the next monthly review.
