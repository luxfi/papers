\section{Airgapped Signing Protocol}

\label{sec:airgap}
%% =====================================================================

The cold-tier airgap share $\mathbf{c}_5$ never participates in
network-attached signing. Communication is by file or QR transport
across an air gap. This section specifies the protocol; it
implements LP-137 \cite{lp137}.

\subsection{Threat Model for the Airgap}

The airgap exists to defeat one specific class of adversary: a
remote adversary that has compromised the orchestrator host and is
capable of exfiltrating any keying material on a network-reachable
machine. Against such an adversary, the airgap is sufficient and a
network-attached cold HSM is not. Consequently the airgap is
mandatory for cold-tier participation.

We do \emph{not} claim the airgap defeats:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item A physically present adversary at the vault site (mitigated by
physical security and FIPS 140-2 Level 3 device tamper response).
\item Side-channel emanations during signing (mitigated by Faraday
shielding of the signing room).
\item A compromised firmware in the airgap device itself (mitigated
by the firmware-update policy in Section~\ref{sec:hsm}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Four-Round Protocol}

Round numbering follows LP-137; we summarise here.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Request.} Online orchestrator produces a signing
request containing: chain, transaction body, derivation path, share
indices required, and a request-nonce. The request is encoded as a
deterministic JSON object, hashed (SHA-256), and the hash is signed
by the orchestrator's online HSM-resident key.
\item \textbf{Transport-out.} The request and its signature are
written to a single-use, write-locked storage medium. The medium of
record is a QR-code printout for sub-2~kB requests and a USB drive
in a tamper-evident bag for larger requests. The medium is hand-carried
by the cold operator into the airgapped signing room.
\item \textbf{Approval and partial-sign.} The airgap operator
verifies the orchestrator signature against a locally cached
public-key bundle. The operator independently displays the
human-readable transaction summary on the airgap workstation and
on a second printout. Two operators (four-eyes principle) confirm
the displayed summary matches the printed summary. The airgap
HSM emits its CGGMP21 partial signature.
\item \textbf{Transport-back.} The partial signature is written to
the return medium (QR or USB), carried out, and combined by the
online orchestrator with the other partial signatures. The combined
signature is broadcast to the chain.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Display, Verification, and the Confused-Deputy Defence}

The most realistic remaining attack is a \emph{confused-deputy}
attack: the online orchestrator presents a transaction that looks
plausible to a hurried operator but in fact transfers funds to the
adversary. The protocol counters this with three separate confirmation
surfaces.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Independent printout.} The transaction summary is
printed on the online side and read in the airgap room.
\item \textbf{Independent decode.} The airgap workstation decodes
the QR/USB and renders the same summary; the two displayed strings
must match.
\item \textbf{Allowlist.} Destination addresses are matched against
a per-tier signed allowlist; non-allowlist destinations require an
out-of-band escalation that itself runs through the airgap protocol.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Pseudocode for the Online and Airgap Sides}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Online orchestrator side}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $req \gets \{chain, tx, path, shares, nonce\}$
\State $h \gets \mathrm{SHA256}(canonicalise(req))$
\State $\sigma_o \gets \mathrm{HSMSign}(K_{online}, h)$
\State emit $(req, \sigma_o)$ to QR or USB
\State \textbf{wait} for return medium
\State $\{\sigma_5\} \gets \mathrm{decode}(\textit{return medium})$
\State $\sigma \gets \mathrm{CGGMP21.combine}(\sigma_3, \sigma_4, \sigma_5)$
\State broadcast $(tx, \sigma)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Airgap side}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State read $(req, \sigma_o)$ from inbound medium
\State \textbf{verify} $\sigma_o$ against $\mathrm{PK}_{online}$
\State render $human(req)$ to airgap display
\State \textbf{require} two operators to confirm $human(req)$ matches printout
\State \textbf{require} $req.dest \in allowlist$ or escalation token present
\State $\sigma_5 \gets \mathrm{HSMPartialSign}(K_{share5}, req)$
\State write $\sigma_5$ to outbound medium
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Operational Cadence}

The airgap protocol is intentionally slow. The default service
level is one ceremony per business day, with twenty-four-hour
emergency turnaround for incidents. This is appropriate for
ninety-five-percent-of-AUM cold storage and is not meant to be
fast: the slowness is a feature that creates a human checkpoint on
every cold movement.

\subsection{Mapping to LP-137}

The protocol implements LP-137 \cite{lp137} \S13 four-round flow
verbatim, with the addition of:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Two-operator confirmation in step 3 (LP-137 specifies one
operator).
\item Per-tier allowlist matching in step 3.
\item M-Chain audit log entry on every successful round trip
(LP-006 \cite{lp006}).
\end{itemize}
