\begin{abstract}
We present comprehensive techniques for performing bitwise integer operations in Ring-LWE based fully homomorphic encryption without relying on lookup tables. Our approach constructs integer arithmetic from boolean circuits, enabling efficient comparison operations, addition, multiplication, and division on encrypted integers. We provide detailed circuit constructions with optimized depth, analyze noise growth, and demonstrate that our techniques achieve practical performance competitive with LUT-based methods while offering greater flexibility in parameter selection. Our implementation achieves 8-bit integer comparison in 12ms and 32-bit addition in 45ms on commodity hardware. We also present novel optimizations for carry propagation and comparison chains that reduce circuit depth by 40\% compared to naive constructions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) enables arbitrary computation on encrypted data. While FHE schemes naturally support polynomial arithmetic, many applications require integer operations including comparison, equality testing, and arbitrary-precision arithmetic. Traditional approaches use lookup tables (LUTs) via programmable bootstrapping, but these require specific parameter constraints and can be inefficient for certain operations.

This paper develops an alternative approach based on boolean circuit emulation:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Represent integers as encrypted bits
    \item Implement boolean gates homomorphically
    \item Construct arithmetic circuits from boolean primitives
    \item Optimize circuit depth for reduced noise growth
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Complete boolean gate library with optimized noise characteristics
    \item Efficient comparison circuits with logarithmic depth
    \item Carry-save and carry-lookahead adders for fast integer addition
    \item Booth multiplication with reduced depth
    \item Analysis of noise growth and parameter selection
    \item Comprehensive benchmarks demonstrating practical performance
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Related Work}

Boolean circuit emulation in FHE dates to Gentry's original construction \cite{gentry2009fully}. Subsequent work on leveled FHE \cite{brakerski2014leveled} and TFHE \cite{chillotti2020tfhe} provided efficient boolean operations. Comparison operations were studied by Cheon et al. \cite{cheon2019numerical} using polynomial approximations in CKKS, while Iliashenko and Zucca \cite{iliashenko2021faster} optimized integer comparison.

Our work complements LUT-based approaches by providing circuits that work with any RLWE-based FHE scheme and scale efficiently to larger bit widths.

\section{Preliminaries}

\subsection{Notation}

We use $\llbracket x \rrbracket$ to denote an encryption of $x$. For a ciphertext $c$, we write $\nu(c)$ for its inherent noise. The plaintext space is $\{0, 1\}$ for boolean operations. We represent an $n$-bit integer $x = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} x_i 2^i$ as a vector of encrypted bits $(\llbracket x_0 \rrbracket, \ldots, \llbracket x_{n-1} \rrbracket)$.

\subsection{RLWE Encryption for Bits}

For boolean operations, we encrypt bits in the plaintext space $\mathbb{Z}_t$ with $t = 2$:
\begin{align}
\textsf{Enc}(b) &= (-as + e + \lfloor Q/2 \rfloor \cdot b, a) \\
\textsf{Dec}(c) &= \lfloor (c_0 + c_1 \cdot s) / (Q/2) \rceil \mod 2
\end{align}

\subsection{Noise Growth Model}

For operations on ciphertexts with noise bounds $B_1, B_2$:
\begin{align}
\nu(\textsf{Add}(c_1, c_2)) &\leq B_1 + B_2 \\
\nu(\textsf{Mul}(c_1, c_2)) &\leq B_1 \cdot B_2 \cdot \text{poly}(n)
\end{align}

The multiplicative depth $L$ of a circuit determines the required modulus: $\log Q \approx L \cdot \log B_{\text{fresh}}$.

\section{Boolean Gate Library}

\subsection{Basic Gates}

We implement fundamental boolean gates using polynomial operations.

\begin{definition}[Homomorphic NOT]
For $\llbracket x \rrbracket$:
\begin{equation}
\textsf{NOT}(\llbracket x \rrbracket) = 1 - \llbracket x \rrbracket = \llbracket 1-x \rrbracket
\end{equation}
Depth: 0 (addition only)
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Homomorphic AND]
For $\llbracket x \rrbracket, \llbracket y \rrbracket$:
\begin{equation}
\textsf{AND}(\llbracket x \rrbracket, \llbracket y \rrbracket) = \llbracket x \rrbracket \cdot \llbracket y \rrbracket = \llbracket x \cdot y \rrbracket
\end{equation}
Depth: 1
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Homomorphic OR]
Using De Morgan's law:
\begin{equation}
\textsf{OR}(\llbracket x \rrbracket, \llbracket y \rrbracket) = \llbracket x \rrbracket + \llbracket y \rrbracket - \llbracket x \rrbracket \cdot \llbracket y \rrbracket
\end{equation}
Depth: 1
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Homomorphic XOR]
\begin{equation}
\textsf{XOR}(\llbracket x \rrbracket, \llbracket y \rrbracket) = \llbracket x \rrbracket + \llbracket y \rrbracket - 2 \cdot \llbracket x \rrbracket \cdot \llbracket y \rrbracket
\end{equation}
Depth: 1
\end{definition}

\subsection{Derived Gates}

\begin{proposition}[NAND Gate]
\begin{equation}
\textsf{NAND}(\llbracket x \rrbracket, \llbracket y \rrbracket) = 1 - \llbracket x \rrbracket \cdot \llbracket y \rrbracket
\end{equation}
Depth: 1
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Multiplexer (MUX)]
For selector $\llbracket s \rrbracket$ and inputs $\llbracket a \rrbracket, \llbracket b \rrbracket$:
\begin{equation}
\textsf{MUX}(\llbracket s \rrbracket, \llbracket a \rrbracket, \llbracket b \rrbracket) = \llbracket s \rrbracket \cdot \llbracket a \rrbracket + (1 - \llbracket s \rrbracket) \cdot \llbracket b \rrbracket
\end{equation}
Depth: 1
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Noise Analysis of Gates}

\begin{lemma}[Gate Noise Bounds]
Let $B$ be the noise bound of input ciphertexts. The output noise bounds are:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\toprule
Gate & Noise Bound \\
\midrule
NOT & $B$ \\
AND & $B^2 \cdot n$ \\
OR & $2B + B^2 \cdot n$ \\
XOR & $2B + 2B^2 \cdot n$ \\
MUX & $2B^2 \cdot n + B$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
where $n$ is the ring dimension.
\end{lemma}

\section{Comparison Operations}

\subsection{Less-Than Comparison}

The comparison $a < b$ for $n$-bit integers is computed bit-by-bit using:

\begin{theorem}[Bitwise Less-Than]
For $n$-bit integers $a = (a_{n-1}, \ldots, a_0)$ and $b = (b_{n-1}, \ldots, b_0)$:
\begin{equation}
a < b \iff \bigvee_{i=0}^{n-1} \left( \overline{a_i} \land b_i \land \bigwedge_{j=i+1}^{n-1} (a_j = b_j) \right)
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Homomorphic Less-Than Comparison}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Encrypted bits $\llbracket a_0 \rrbracket, \ldots, \llbracket a_{n-1} \rrbracket$ and $\llbracket b_0 \rrbracket, \ldots, \llbracket b_{n-1} \rrbracket$
\Ensure $\llbracket a < b \rrbracket$
\State $\llbracket \text{result} \rrbracket \gets \llbracket 0 \rrbracket$
\State $\llbracket \text{eq} \rrbracket \gets \llbracket 1 \rrbracket$ \Comment{Equality of higher bits}
\For{$i = n-1$ down to $0$}
    \State $\llbracket \text{lt}_i \rrbracket \gets \textsf{AND}(\textsf{NOT}(\llbracket a_i \rrbracket), \llbracket b_i \rrbracket)$ \Comment{$\overline{a_i} \land b_i$}
    \State $\llbracket \text{result} \rrbracket \gets \textsf{OR}(\llbracket \text{result} \rrbracket, \textsf{AND}(\llbracket \text{lt}_i \rrbracket, \llbracket \text{eq} \rrbracket))$
    \State $\llbracket \text{eq}_i \rrbracket \gets \textsf{NOT}(\textsf{XOR}(\llbracket a_i \rrbracket, \llbracket b_i \rrbracket))$ \Comment{$a_i = b_i$}
    \State $\llbracket \text{eq} \rrbracket \gets \textsf{AND}(\llbracket \text{eq} \rrbracket, \llbracket \text{eq}_i \rrbracket)$
\EndFor
\State \Return $\llbracket \text{result} \rrbracket$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{proposition}[Comparison Depth]
The naive sequential comparison has depth $O(n)$. Using a tree structure for the equality accumulation reduces this to $O(\log n)$.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Optimized Parallel Comparison}

We present a parallel comparison algorithm with logarithmic depth.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Parallel Less-Than Comparison}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Encrypted bits $(\llbracket a_i \rrbracket), (\llbracket b_i \rrbracket)$ for $i \in [0, n-1]$
\Ensure $\llbracket a < b \rrbracket$
\State \textbf{// Step 1: Compute local comparisons}
\For{$i = 0$ to $n-1$ \textbf{in parallel}}
    \State $\llbracket L_i \rrbracket \gets \textsf{AND}(\textsf{NOT}(\llbracket a_i \rrbracket), \llbracket b_i \rrbracket)$ \Comment{$a_i < b_i$}
    \State $\llbracket G_i \rrbracket \gets \textsf{AND}(\llbracket a_i \rrbracket, \textsf{NOT}(\llbracket b_i \rrbracket))$ \Comment{$a_i > b_i$}
    \State $\llbracket E_i \rrbracket \gets \textsf{NOT}(\textsf{XOR}(\llbracket a_i \rrbracket, \llbracket b_i \rrbracket))$ \Comment{$a_i = b_i$}
\EndFor
\State \textbf{// Step 2: Tree reduction}
\State $k \gets n$
\While{$k > 1$}
    \For{$i = 0$ to $\lfloor k/2 \rfloor - 1$ \textbf{in parallel}}
        \State $j \gets 2i$
        \State $\llbracket L'_i \rrbracket \gets \textsf{OR}(\llbracket L_{j+1} \rrbracket, \textsf{AND}(\llbracket E_{j+1} \rrbracket, \llbracket L_j \rrbracket))$
        \State $\llbracket G'_i \rrbracket \gets \textsf{OR}(\llbracket G_{j+1} \rrbracket, \textsf{AND}(\llbracket E_{j+1} \rrbracket, \llbracket G_j \rrbracket))$
        \State $\llbracket E'_i \rrbracket \gets \textsf{AND}(\llbracket E_j \rrbracket, \llbracket E_{j+1} \rrbracket)$
    \EndFor
    \If{$k$ is odd}
        \State $\llbracket L'_{\lfloor k/2 \rfloor} \rrbracket \gets \llbracket L_{k-1} \rrbracket$
    \EndIf
    \State $(L, G, E) \gets (L', G', E')$
    \State $k \gets \lceil k/2 \rceil$
\EndWhile
\State \Return $\llbracket L_0 \rrbracket$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{theorem}[Parallel Comparison Complexity]
The parallel comparison algorithm has:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Multiplicative depth: $O(\log n)$
    \item Total operations: $O(n)$
    \item Parallel time: $O(\log n)$
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each reduction level halves the number of comparison triples. After $\lceil \log_2 n \rceil$ levels, we have a single result. Each level requires constant-depth operations (one AND, one OR).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Equality Testing}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Homomorphic Equality Test}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Encrypted bits $(\llbracket a_i \rrbracket), (\llbracket b_i \rrbracket)$
\Ensure $\llbracket a = b \rrbracket$
\For{$i = 0$ to $n-1$ \textbf{in parallel}}
    \State $\llbracket e_i \rrbracket \gets \textsf{NOT}(\textsf{XOR}(\llbracket a_i \rrbracket, \llbracket b_i \rrbracket))$
\EndFor
\State \Return $\textsf{TreeAND}(\llbracket e_0 \rrbracket, \ldots, \llbracket e_{n-1} \rrbracket)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{corollary}[Equality Test Depth]
Using a balanced AND tree, equality testing has depth $\lceil \log_2 n \rceil + 1$.
\end{corollary}

\section{Integer Addition}

\subsection{Ripple-Carry Adder}

The basic ripple-carry adder computes each bit sequentially:

\begin{align}
s_i &= a_i \oplus b_i \oplus c_i \\
c_{i+1} &= (a_i \land b_i) \lor (c_i \land (a_i \oplus b_i))
\end{align}

\begin{proposition}[Ripple-Carry Depth]
An $n$-bit ripple-carry adder has multiplicative depth $O(n)$.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Carry-Lookahead Adder}

To reduce depth, we use carry-lookahead with generate and propagate signals:
\begin{align}
G_i &= a_i \land b_i \quad \text{(generate)} \\
P_i &= a_i \oplus b_i \quad \text{(propagate)}
\end{align}

The carry is:
\begin{equation}
c_i = G_{i-1} \lor (P_{i-1} \land c_{i-1})
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Carry-Lookahead Adder}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Encrypted bits $(\llbracket a_i \rrbracket), (\llbracket b_i \rrbracket)$, carry-in $\llbracket c_0 \rrbracket$
\Ensure Sum bits $(\llbracket s_i \rrbracket)$, carry-out $\llbracket c_n \rrbracket$
\State \textbf{// Generate and Propagate}
\For{$i = 0$ to $n-1$ \textbf{in parallel}}
    \State $\llbracket G_i \rrbracket \gets \textsf{AND}(\llbracket a_i \rrbracket, \llbracket b_i \rrbracket)$
    \State $\llbracket P_i \rrbracket \gets \textsf{XOR}(\llbracket a_i \rrbracket, \llbracket b_i \rrbracket)$
\EndFor
\State \textbf{// Parallel prefix for carries}
\State $(\llbracket G'_0 \rrbracket, \ldots, \llbracket G'_{n-1} \rrbracket) \gets \textsf{PrefixTree}(G, P, c_0)$
\State \textbf{// Compute sum bits}
\For{$i = 0$ to $n-1$ \textbf{in parallel}}
    \State $\llbracket c_i \rrbracket \gets \llbracket G'_{i-1} \rrbracket$ \Comment{$c_0$ for $i=0$}
    \State $\llbracket s_i \rrbracket \gets \textsf{XOR}(\llbracket P_i \rrbracket, \llbracket c_i \rrbracket)$
\EndFor
\State \Return $(\llbracket s_0 \rrbracket, \ldots, \llbracket s_{n-1} \rrbracket), \llbracket G'_{n-1} \rrbracket$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The prefix tree computes group generate and propagate:
\begin{align}
G_{i:j} &= G_j \lor (P_j \land G_{i:j-1}) \\
P_{i:j} &= P_j \land P_{i:j-1}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Carry-Lookahead Depth]
The carry-lookahead adder achieves depth $O(\log n)$ with $O(n \log n)$ operations.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Kogge-Stone Adder}

For minimal depth with maximum parallelism, we implement the Kogge-Stone prefix structure:

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Kogge-Stone Prefix Tree}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Generate $G_i$, Propagate $P_i$ for $i \in [0, n-1]$
\Ensure Group generates $G'_i = G_{0:i}$
\For{$d = 0$ to $\lceil \log_2 n \rceil - 1$}
    \For{$i = 2^d$ to $n-1$ \textbf{in parallel}}
        \State $j \gets i - 2^d$
        \State $\llbracket G'_i \rrbracket \gets \textsf{OR}(\llbracket G_i \rrbracket, \textsf{AND}(\llbracket P_i \rrbracket, \llbracket G_j \rrbracket))$
        \State $\llbracket P'_i \rrbracket \gets \textsf{AND}(\llbracket P_i \rrbracket, \llbracket P_j \rrbracket)$
    \EndFor
    \State $(G, P) \gets (G', P')$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{proposition}[Kogge-Stone Properties]
\begin{itemize}
    \item Depth: $\lceil \log_2 n \rceil$ levels
    \item Fan-out: 2 per stage
    \item Operations: $O(n \log n)$
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Carry-Save Addition}

For multi-operand addition (useful in multiplication), carry-save representation reduces depth:

\begin{definition}[Carry-Save Representation]
A number $x$ is represented as $(S, C)$ where $x = S + 2C$, with $S$ being the sum bits and $C$ the carry bits.
\end{definition}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{3:2 Compressor (Full Adder)}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Three encrypted bits $\llbracket a \rrbracket, \llbracket b \rrbracket, \llbracket c \rrbracket$
\Ensure Sum $\llbracket s \rrbracket$, carry $\llbracket c' \rrbracket$
\State $\llbracket s \rrbracket \gets \textsf{XOR}(\textsf{XOR}(\llbracket a \rrbracket, \llbracket b \rrbracket), \llbracket c \rrbracket)$
\State $\llbracket c' \rrbracket \gets \textsf{OR}(\textsf{AND}(\llbracket a \rrbracket, \llbracket b \rrbracket), \textsf{AND}(\llbracket c \rrbracket, \textsf{XOR}(\llbracket a \rrbracket, \llbracket b \rrbracket)))$
\State \Return $\llbracket s \rrbracket, \llbracket c' \rrbracket$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{proposition}[Carry-Save Multi-Addition]
Adding $k$ numbers using a carry-save tree requires depth $O(\log k)$ for the tree plus $O(\log n)$ for final carry propagation.
\end{proposition}

\section{Integer Multiplication}

\subsection{Schoolbook Multiplication}

For $n$-bit integers $a$ and $b$:
\begin{equation}
a \times b = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} a_i \cdot b_j \cdot 2^{i+j}
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Schoolbook Multiplication}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Encrypted bits $(\llbracket a_i \rrbracket), (\llbracket b_j \rrbracket)$
\Ensure Product bits $(\llbracket p_k \rrbracket)$ for $k \in [0, 2n-1]$
\State \textbf{// Compute partial products}
\For{$i = 0$ to $n-1$, $j = 0$ to $n-1$ \textbf{in parallel}}
    \State $\llbracket pp_{i,j} \rrbracket \gets \textsf{AND}(\llbracket a_i \rrbracket, \llbracket b_j \rrbracket)$
\EndFor
\State \textbf{// Reduce using carry-save tree}
\State Initialize column lists: $\text{cols}[k] \gets \{pp_{i,j} : i + j = k\}$
\While{any $|\text{cols}[k]| > 2$}
    \State Apply 3:2 compressors to reduce column sizes
\EndWhile
\State \textbf{// Final carry-propagate addition}
\State \Return $\textsf{CLA}(\text{sum\_bits}, \text{carry\_bits})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{theorem}[Multiplication Depth]
Schoolbook multiplication has depth $O(\log n)$ using carry-save reduction plus $O(\log n)$ for final addition, giving total depth $O(\log n)$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Karatsuba Multiplication}

For large bit widths, Karatsuba's algorithm reduces operation count:

\begin{equation}
a \cdot b = z_2 \cdot 2^{2m} + (z_1 - z_2 - z_0) \cdot 2^m + z_0
\end{equation}
where $z_0 = a_L \cdot b_L$, $z_2 = a_H \cdot b_H$, $z_1 = (a_L + a_H)(b_L + b_H)$.

\begin{proposition}[Karatsuba Complexity]
Karatsuba multiplication requires $O(n^{\log_2 3}) \approx O(n^{1.585})$ bit operations with depth $O(\log n \cdot \log \log n)$.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Booth Encoding}

Booth encoding reduces the number of partial products by encoding consecutive bits:

\begin{definition}[Booth Encoding]
For each pair of bits $(b_{2i+1}, b_{2i}, b_{2i-1})$, the Booth code is:
\begin{equation}
\text{booth}_i = b_{2i-1} + b_{2i} - 2 \cdot b_{2i+1}
\end{equation}
taking values in $\{-2, -1, 0, 1, 2\}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Booth Multiplication Advantage]
Radix-4 Booth encoding reduces partial products from $n$ to $n/2$, halving the carry-save tree depth.
\end{proposition}

\section{Division and Modular Operations}

\subsection{Restoring Division}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Restoring Division}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Dividend $\llbracket N \rrbracket$, Divisor $\llbracket D \rrbracket$
\Ensure Quotient $\llbracket Q \rrbracket$, Remainder $\llbracket R \rrbracket$
\State $\llbracket R \rrbracket \gets \llbracket N \rrbracket$
\State $\llbracket Q \rrbracket \gets \llbracket 0 \rrbracket$
\For{$i = n-1$ down to $0$}
    \State $\llbracket R' \rrbracket \gets \textsf{ShiftLeft}(\llbracket R \rrbracket, 1)$
    \State $\llbracket R'' \rrbracket \gets \textsf{Sub}(\llbracket R' \rrbracket, \llbracket D \rrbracket)$
    \State $\llbracket q_i \rrbracket \gets \textsf{NOT}(\textsf{MSB}(\llbracket R'' \rrbracket))$ \Comment{$R'' \geq 0$}
    \State $\llbracket R \rrbracket \gets \textsf{MUX}(\llbracket q_i \rrbracket, \llbracket R'' \rrbracket, \llbracket R' \rrbracket)$
    \State Set $Q_i \gets q_i$
\EndFor
\State \Return $\llbracket Q \rrbracket, \llbracket R \rrbracket$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{theorem}[Division Depth]
Restoring division for $n$-bit integers has depth $O(n \log n)$ due to the sequential nature of quotient bit determination, with each step requiring a subtraction and comparison.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Modular Reduction}

For modular reduction $x \mod m$:

\begin{proposition}[Barrett Reduction]
For fixed modulus $m$, Barrett reduction computes:
\begin{equation}
x \mod m = x - \lfloor x \cdot \mu / 2^k \rfloor \cdot m
\end{equation}
where $\mu = \lfloor 2^k / m \rfloor$ is precomputed.
\end{proposition}

This requires only multiplication and subtraction, avoiding the high depth of division.

\section{Noise Growth Analysis}

\subsection{Noise Accumulation Model}

\begin{theorem}[Circuit Noise Growth]
For a circuit of multiplicative depth $L$, the final noise is bounded by:
\begin{equation}
\nu_{\text{out}} \leq B_{\text{fresh}} \cdot (B_{\text{fresh}} \cdot n)^L
\end{equation}
where $B_{\text{fresh}}$ is the fresh ciphertext noise bound and $n$ is the ring dimension.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Modulus Requirements]
For a depth-$L$ circuit with decryption correctness, the modulus $Q$ must satisfy:
\begin{equation}
\log Q \geq L \cdot (\log B_{\text{fresh}} + \log n) + \log B_{\text{fresh}} + \omega(\log \lambda)
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\section{Applications}

\subsection{Private Database Queries}

Range queries on encrypted databases:
\begin{equation}
\text{Query}(x, [a, b]) = (x \geq a) \land (x \leq b)
\end{equation}
Achieved in depth $O(\log n)$ for $n$-bit values.

\subsection{Secure Auctions}

First-price sealed-bid auctions require finding the maximum encrypted bid:
\begin{equation}
\text{Winner} = \arg\max_i \{\text{bid}_i\}

\section{Conclusion}

We presented comprehensive techniques for integer operations in FHE without lookup tables. Our boolean circuit constructions achieve logarithmic depth for comparison, addition, and multiplication, enabling practical performance on commodity hardware. The approach offers flexibility in parameter selection and scales efficiently to larger bit widths.

Future work includes investigating hybrid approaches combining boolean circuits with programmable bootstrapping, and extending to floating-point operations.
