\subsection{Geographic Distribution}

With 5 parties across US-East, US-West, EU-West, Asia-Pacific, and South America
(100--250\,ms inter-region latency), end-to-end signing for a 3-of-5 threshold was
dominated by network round-trips rather than computation, comfortably within the
finality-certificate cadence. Because Round~1 is message-independent, it can be
precomputed, leaving only the Round~2 round-trip and the non-interactive combine on the
critical path.

Corona's $\sim$33\,KB signature is larger than classical schemes by roughly three orders
of magnitude, but this is acceptable for block certificates (one signature per block). As
the rank-1 base, Corona is more compact than the rank-$k$ Pulsar generalisation, since a
single ring element replaces a length-$k$ vector of ring elements. The signing time is
dominated by network latency rather than computation, and the $\sim$1.6\,ms verification
is competitive with classical schemes.
