\section{The Corona Scheme}

\label{sec:scheme}

Corona is a two-round Ring-LWE threshold signature over the single ring $R_q$. The
public key is one ring relation $b = a \cdot s + e$ with $a, s, e \in R_q$; there is no
matrix. We first describe the leaderless distributed key generation (no trusted dealer),
then the two-round signing protocol, then the non-interactive combine and verification.
Throughout, $\mathcal{S}$ denotes a signing set with $|\mathcal{S}| \ge t$, and
$\lambda_i \in \mathbb{Z}_q$ are the Lagrange coefficients of~\eqref{eq:lagrange} for the
set $\mathcal{S}$.

\subsection{Distributed Key Generation (Leaderless, No Trusted Dealer)}

The public ring element $a \in R_q$ is a fixed, publicly derivable parameter (a nothing-
up-my-sleeve hash output bound to the ceremony root), so no party controls it. The secret
$s \in R_q$ is generated jointly: each party contributes an additive summand and a
verifiable Shamir sharing of that summand, so the group secret is
$s = \sum_{i} s^{(i)}$ and no party ever sees $s$.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Corona Distributed Key Generation}
\label{alg:keygen}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Threshold $t$, parties $n$, parameters $(N, q, \sigma_e)$, ceremony root $\rho$
\State $a \gets H_{R_q}(\text{``Corona-pk''} \,\|\, \rho)$ \Comment{public ring element, derived (no dealer)}
\For{each party $i \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$} \Comment{run in parallel; leaderless}
    \State $s^{(i)} \stackrel{\$}{\leftarrow} D_{\sigma_e}$, \quad $e^{(i)} \stackrel{\$}{\leftarrow} D_{\sigma_e}$ \Comment{$i$'s short secret/error summands $\in R_q$}
    \State $f^{(i)}(X') \gets s^{(i)} + \sum_{\ell=1}^{t-1} a^{(i)}_\ell (X')^\ell$, \quad $a^{(i)}_\ell \stackrel{\$}{\leftarrow} R_q$ \Comment{Shamir poly over $R_q$}
    \State Broadcast Feldman/Pedersen commitments to $s^{(i)}$ and $\{a^{(i)}_\ell\}$
    \State Send share $s^{(i)}_j \gets f^{(i)}(\mathrm{id}_j)$ privately to each party $j$
\EndFor
\For{each party $j$} \Comment{verify and aggregate}
    \State \textbf{abort/identify} any party $i$ whose share $s^{(i)}_j$ contradicts its commitments
    \State $s_j \gets \sum_{i} s^{(i)}_j$ \Comment{$j$'s aggregate share of $s = \sum_i s^{(i)}$}
\EndFor
\State $b \gets a \cdot s + e$, \quad $e = \sum_i e^{(i)}$ \Comment{public key, recombined from commitments}
\State \Return pk $= (a, b)$, \; shares $\{s_j\}_{j=1}^{n}$ \Comment{group secret $s$ never reconstructed}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent The public key $b = a \cdot s + e$ is a Ring-LWE sample (Definition~\ref{def:rlwe}):
recovering $s$ from $(a, b)$ is the Ring-LWE search problem. Because $a$ is derived from
the ceremony root and every contribution is committed, the protocol is leaderless and
robust against an adversary controlling up to $t-1$ parties; corrupted shares are caught
by commitment verification (identifiable abort). Resharing for a changed validator set
re-runs Algorithm~\ref{alg:keygen} over the new set, with the group public key $b$ held
fixed across generations (see~\S\ref{sec:integration}).

\subsection{Signing Protocol (2 Rounds)}

Round~1 is message-independent and can be precomputed. Each signer commits to a short
masking element $y_i \in R_q$ via $w_i = a \cdot y_i$; correlated randomness with
pairwise cancellation keeps the aggregate mask short, and per-message MACs authenticate
commitments against malicious injection.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Corona Signing --- Round 1 (Message-Independent)}
\label{alg:sign-r1}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Party $i$, signers set $\mathcal{S}$ with $|\mathcal{S}| \geq t$, session id $\mathsf{sid}$
\State $r_i \gets \text{PRF}(\text{key}_i, \mathsf{sid} \| i) \bmod D_{\sigma^*}$ \Comment{$i$'s short mask $\in R_q$}
\For{each party $j \in \mathcal{S} \setminus \{i\}$}
    \State $r_{i,j} \gets \text{PRF}(\text{seed}_{i,j}, \mathsf{sid})$ \Comment{pairwise correlated randomness $\in R_q$}
\EndFor
\State $y_i \gets r_i + \sum_{j : j > i} r_{i,j} - \sum_{j : j < i} r_{j,i}$ \Comment{masked randomness, pairwise cancellation}
\State $w_i \gets a \cdot y_i$ \Comment{commitment (single ring multiplication)}
\For{each $j \in \mathcal{S} \setminus \{i\}$}
    \State $\text{mac}_{i \to j} \gets \text{HMAC}(\text{MACkey}_{i \to j}, \mathsf{sid} \| w_i)$
\EndFor
\State \textbf{broadcast} $(w_i, \{\text{mac}_{i \to j}\}_{j \in \mathcal{S}})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Corona Signing --- Round 2 (Message-Dependent)}
\label{alg:sign-r2}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Party $i$, message $\mu$, Round 1 data $\{(w_j, \text{mac}_j)\}_{j \in \mathcal{S}}$
\For{each $j \in \mathcal{S} \setminus \{i\}$}
    \State Verify $\text{mac}_{j \to i}$ using $\text{MACkey}_{j \to i}$; \textbf{abort} (identify $j$) if it fails
\EndFor
\State $w \gets \sum_{j \in \mathcal{S}} \lambda_j \cdot w_j$ \Comment{combined commitment $\in R_q$}
\State $c \gets H(a, b, w, \mu)$ \Comment{challenge: ternary polynomial in $R_q$, weight $\kappa$}
\State $z_i \gets y_i + c \cdot \lambda_i \cdot s_i$ \Comment{partial response (ring multiplication)}
\If{$\|z_i\|_\infty > B$} \Comment{Fiat--Shamir-with-aborts rejection sampling}
    \State \textbf{abort} and restart with a fresh session id
\EndIf
\State \textbf{broadcast} $z_i$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Combine and Verification}

The combine is non-interactive: any party or external aggregator sums the partial
responses into a single ring element
\[
z \;=\; \sum_{i \in \mathcal{S}} \lambda_i \cdot z_i \;\in\; R_q ,
\]
and outputs the signature $(c, z)$. By linearity and the Lagrange reconstruction
of~\eqref{eq:lagrange}, $z = y + c \cdot s$ where $y = \sum_{i} \lambda_i y_i$ masks the
short secret $s$, so $a \cdot z - c \cdot b = a \cdot y - c \cdot e \approx w$.

Verification of $(c, z)$ against pk $= (a, b)$ and message $\mu$ checks:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item $\|z\|_\infty \leq B'$ (the response is short, for the combined bound $B'$);
\item recompute $w' \gets a \cdot z - c \cdot b \in R_q$ and check $c = H(a, b, w', \mu)$.
\end{enumerate}
Both checks are single ring operations over $R_q$ plus one hash, giving the $\sim$1.6\,ms
verification of \S\ref{sec:evaluation}. A successful forgery on a fresh message yields a
short $z^*$ with $a \cdot z^* \approx w^* + c^* \cdot b$, i.e.\ a short Ring-SIS solution
relative to $a$ (Definition~\ref{def:rsis}); this is the basis of the reduction
in~\S\ref{sec:security}.
