\begin{abstract}
We specify the Curve25519 point operation precompile on the Lux EVM, providing
native-speed point addition and scalar multiplication over the Montgomery curve
$y^2 = x^3 + 486662 x^2 + x$ modulo $p = 2^{255} - 19$. Curve25519 is the
foundation for X25519 key exchange (RFC~7748), Ed25519 signatures (RFC~8032),
and the VRF construction used in Lux validator selection. The precompile exposes
the full group law (not just the $x$-only Montgomery ladder), enabling on-chain
verification of Ed25519 signatures re-encoded as Curve25519 points, VRF proof
validation, and Pedersen commitments over the 25519 field. Gas costs are set at
120 gas for addition and 5\,000 gas for scalar multiplication, reflecting the
efficiency of the $2^{255}-19$ pseudo-Mersenne prime.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Curve25519~\cite{bernstein-curve25519} is a Montgomery curve over $\mathbb{F}_p$
with $p = 2^{255} - 19$, providing approximately 128 bits of security against
classical attacks. It is birationally equivalent to Edwards25519 (the curve
underlying Ed25519). The pseudo-Mersenne modulus enables fast reduction via
a single multiplication by 19.

On-chain Curve25519 operations are needed for:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item Verifying Ed25519 signatures from external systems (Solana, Cosmos, Substrate).
  \item Validating VRF outputs for fair randomness proofs.
  \item Pedersen vector commitments for confidential transactions.
  \item Interoperability with the X25519/Ed25519 key ecosystem.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Curve Parameters}

\begin{definition}[Curve25519]
Montgomery form: $B v^2 = u^3 + A u^2 + u$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ with:
\begin{align}
  p &= 2^{255} - 19 \\
  A &= 486662 \\
  B &= 1 \\
  |E(\mathbb{F}_p)| &= 8 \cdot \ell, \quad \ell = 2^{252} + 27742317777372353535851937790883648493
\end{align}
The cofactor is $h = 8$ and $\ell$ is prime.
\end{definition}

The birational equivalence to Edwards25519 ($-x^2 + y^2 = 1 + d x^2 y^2$,
$d = -121665/121666$) is:
\begin{align}
  u &= \frac{1+y}{1-y}, \quad v = \frac{u}{x} \cdot \sqrt{-486664}
\end{align}

\subsection{Address and Operations}

\subsection{Point Representation}

Points are encoded in extended Edwards coordinates $(x, y)$, each 32 bytes
(little-endian, matching RFC~8032 convention). The identity is $(0, 1)$.

Internally, computations use extended coordinates $(X : Y : Z : T)$ where
$x = X/Z$, $y = Y/Z$, $T = XY/Z$ for unified addition formulas.

\subsection{Addition}

Extended Edwards addition~\cite{hisil-extended}: 8 multiplications, 1 doubling of
a field element, no inversions. The formula is complete for Edwards25519
($a = -1$, $d$ non-square).

\subsection{Scalar Multiplication}

The Montgomery ladder on the $u$-coordinate provides constant-time scalar
multiplication in $\lceil\log_2 \ell\rceil = 253$ steps. When both coordinates
are needed (e.g., for signature verification), the implementation uses the
Edwards form with a $w$-ary non-adjacent form ($w = 5$).

\section{Security Analysis}

\subsection{Cofactor}

Input points are multiplied by the cofactor $h = 8$ in all equality checks,
or equivalently, all comparisons are performed in the prime-order subgroup.
The precompile does \emph{not} silently project inputs to the subgroup; it
validates that inputs are on the curve and returns an error for points not
in the prime-order subgroup.

\subsection{Clamping}

For scalar multiplication, the scalar is \emph{not} clamped (unlike X25519 RFC~7748).
The precompile provides raw group operations. Protocols that require clamping must
apply it before calling the precompile. This preserves composability.

\subsection{Field Reduction}

Coordinates are validated to be in $[0, p)$. Scalars are reduced modulo $\ell$.
The field arithmetic uses 5$\times$51-bit limb representation with carry
propagation, matching the \texttt{filippo.io/edwards25519} library.

\section{Gas Cost Justification}

The $2^{255}-19$ modulus allows reduction via a single multiply-by-19, making
field operations $\sim$20\% faster than BN254 base field ($\sim$254$-$bit
non-Mersenne prime). Gas costs reflect this:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Operation} & \textbf{Time ($\mu$s)} & \textbf{Gas} \\
\midrule
Point Add & 1.8 & 120 \\
Scalar Mul & 78 & 5\,000 \\
Decompress & 3.1 & 200 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\section{Conclusion}

The Curve25519 precompile provides on-chain access to the most widely deployed
elliptic curve in modern cryptography. The complete Edwards addition law,
constant-time Montgomery ladder, and cofactor-aware validation enable secure
composition with Ed25519, X25519, and VRF protocols.
