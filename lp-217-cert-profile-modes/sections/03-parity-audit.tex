\section{Corona / Pulsar Parity Audit (2026-06-03)}
\label{sec:parity}

\textbf{Verdict: Pulsar-first is locked.}

The Corona/Pulsar parity audit
(\texttt{lux/threshold/protocols/parity/parity\_test.go},
completed 2026-06-03) measured the two schemes apples-to-apples on
Apple M1 Max + DGX Spark GB10 and found Corona is
\emph{$141\times$ slower than Pulsar at $N{=}64$ validators} (CPU
baseline; GPU acceleration pending for both). This is not an
optimization gap -- it is algorithmic.

\subsection{Why the gap is algorithmic}

\paragraph{Pulsar is a ``reconstruct-on-aggregator'' Shamir scheme.}
The aggregator briefly materializes the 32-byte master sk via
Shamir reconstruction over GF($257$), runs unmodified FIPS 204
ML-DSA sign once on that host, zeroizes. Output is byte-equal to
vanilla ML-DSA -- verifiable by any FIPS 204 verifier (circl,
BoringSSL, RustCrypto). \cite{fips204}

\paragraph{Corona is a genuine 2-round threshold lattice scheme}
(Corona family). Each party performs $392$
NTT polynomial multiplies in Round~1 plus $256$ independent
Gaussian eliminations in Round~2 plus $2t$ PRF expansions. No party
ever materializes the master sk. Different security property;
different cost class.

\paragraph{At $N{=}64$ on the same hardware} Pulsar lands at
\SI{28.5}{\milli\second} total; Corona lands at \SI{4031}{\milli\second}
total. The $141\times$ gap is the price of ``never trust the
aggregator.'' Worth it for high-value chains; not worth it for the
minimum-PQ tier.

\subsection{Vector availability}

Pulsar has committed KAT vectors (\texttt{lux/pulsar/vectors/*.json})
and cross-impl validation against circl. Corona's KAT vectors are
pending; the audit harness produced the seed for the first set.

\subsection{The architectural decision}

The audit locks two architectural choices:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item \textbf{PQ-fast $=$ BLS $+$ Pulsar.} Pulsar is cheap (\SI{28.5}{\milli\second})
  CPU, FIPS 204 interoperable, and has committed KAT vectors. It is
  the minimum-PQ tier.
\item \textbf{PQ-strict adds Corona on top.} For chains that need
  the never-trust-aggregator property (custody, treasury, regulated
  securities), the $\sim 100\times$ latency cost buys a real
  security upgrade (threshold lattice without aggregator trust),
  not a redundant lane. Corona is therefore on top of Pulsar at
  PQ-strict, not in place of it.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{GPU caveat.} Once GPU-accelerated Corona lands (the
NTT-batched aggregate kernel is in flight, per LP-203 follow-up),
the absolute cost drops by an estimated $50\times$
(\SI{4.0}{\second} $\to$ $\sim$\SI{80}{\milli\second}); the
\emph{ratio} to Pulsar drops by less because Pulsar's aggregate
also benefits from GPU. The architectural ranking (Pulsar cheap,
Corona expensive) is preserved.

\paragraph{This paper adds:} the Corona/Pulsar parity audit verdict
as a \emph{first-class section} of the LP. The LP itself references
the audit; this paper makes the verdict explicit, names the
$141\times$ ratio, and ties it to the LP-217 mode ladder locking
decision.

\subsection{Update from the Quasar matrix bench (2026-06-04)}

The matrix bench (Section~\ref{sec:modes}) measured PQ-strict
total wall-clock at $N{=}64$ on Spark = \SI{595}{ms}, vs the parity
audit's per-host Corona total of \SI{4.0}{s}. The reconciliation:
the parity-audit measurement is single-host CPU work; the matrix
bench is the parallel deployment model where each validator runs
its R1/R2/sign on its own host with the whole machine to itself,
then the slowest validator's wall-clock dominates. With $N{=}64$
validators each doing $\approx 30$ ms of Corona work in parallel,
the max-validator wall-clock is bounded by the slowest single-host
ceremony plus aggregation, not by $N \times$ ceremony cost.

The architectural ranking from the parity audit stands -- Pulsar is
intrinsically cheaper per-validator than Corona -- but production
deployments see the parallel wall-clock, not the per-host total.
PQ-strict at $N{=}64$ inside \SI{600}{ms} on Spark validates the
``Corona + Pulsar at PQ-strict'' choice: the cross-family lattice
diversity arrives inside the round budget when measured at
deployment scale.

Pulsar's per-validator sign on Spark is \SI{175}{\micro\second}, well
under Corona's per-validator ceremony of $\sim$\SI{30}{ms}. Pulsar
therefore composes ``for free'' on top of Corona in the wall-clock
sense: each validator's Pulsar leg completes during the Corona R1
window. This is why PQ-strict's wall-clock is barely above PQ-fast
($\sim$\SI{595}{ms} vs $\sim$\SI{585}{ms} at $N{=}64$ Spark) --
the Pulsar leg overlaps the Corona R1 leg.
