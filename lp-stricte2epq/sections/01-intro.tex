\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The gap end-to-end PQ closes}

The post-quantum blockchain literature has converged on a fast answer: replace
the consensus signature. Threshold ML-DSA \cite{tmldsa2025}, Threshold Raccoon
\cite{thresholdraccoon}, lattice-based DKGs \cite{krt24,gks24}, and the Lux
Pulsar family \cite{lp073} all PQ-harden the certificate that closes a block.
None of them PQ-harden the bytes that authorise the transactions \emph{inside}
the block, the bytes that authorise the cross-chain handshakes around the
block, the bytes that authorise the smart-contract spend, or the trusted-setup
SRS that the proof system underneath assumes.

A Shor-capable adversary against the resulting stack does not need to attack
the threshold lane. The adversary forges an ECDSA transaction signature, an
EIP-2612 permit, a BLS bridge admission, a Diffie-Hellman handshake, or a
Groth16 verification key. Any one of those primitives is sufficient to drain
funds, hijack a contract, or admit a malicious block.

The NIST Multi-Party Threshold Cryptography report
\cite{nistir8214c} names this layer (the ``cryptographic surface'') and lists
fourteen categories of bytes that participate in chain state transitions. A
proper PQ posture binds \emph{every} category to a quantum-resistant primitive.
Anything less is theatre.

LUX\_STRICT\_E2E\_PQ is the locked profile under which every one of those
fourteen surfaces is pinned to a FIPS 203 / 204 / 205 primitive
\cite{fips203,fips204,fips205} or a SHA3-family hash
\cite{fips202,sp800185}, and that pinning is consumed at boot, at handshake,
at transaction admission, at contract permit verification, at finality
sealing, at bridge admission, and at every other gating point in the stack.

\subsection{What the profile is, mechanically}

The profile is a single Go struct, \texttt{ChainSecurityProfile}
(\texttt{luxfi/consensus@v1.23.6}, \texttt{config/security\_profile.go:244}),
with the following well-known forms:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item \texttt{ProfileLuxStrictPQ} --- the locked production profile this
    paper specifies, golden hash \texttt{93efd103\ldots323bfcd0}.
  \item \texttt{ProfileLuxFIPS} --- equivalent posture, FIPS-only branding,
    distinct hash for audit attestation.
  \item \texttt{ProfileLuxPermissive} --- testnet / devnet profile with
    classical-compat affordances; \emph{never loaded on mainnet}.
\end{itemize}

The struct has eleven enum-or-flag fields plus aggregator lists; \emph{every}
enum field is zero-value-invalid. A profile produced by zero-initialising the
struct fails \texttt{Validate} (\texttt{security\_profile.go:Validate}) at
boot. The profile's identity is its SHA3-384 digest under
\texttt{ComputeHash} (\texttt{security\_profile.go:835}), which is in turn
TupleHash256-bound under customization \texttt{LUX\_CHAIN\_SECURITY\_V1} so any
byte flip changes the digest. The digest is what genesis pins. A divergent
node sees a different digest and refuses to start.

\subsection{What surface coverage means}

Surface coverage is the proposition: every byte that the consensus engine,
the execution engine, the bridge, the wallet, or a peer treats as
authoritative for a state transition is bound, through transcripts and
signatures, to a post-quantum primitive.

The fourteen surfaces, in dependence order:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Profile boot.} \texttt{ChainSecurityProfile} loaded from
    genesis, \texttt{ComputeHash} matches pinned digest, banner emitted on
    boot (\texttt{luxfi/node@9df72a6f55}).
  \item \textbf{Identity registration.} Validator pubkeys (FIPS 204 ML-DSA)
    are registered through Z-Chain \texttt{OpRegisterKey} with a proof of
    possession over the new key's own AccountID.
  \item \textbf{Identity rotation.} \texttt{OpRotateKey} signed under the
    \emph{outgoing} key, gated by the profile's
    \texttt{IdentitySchemeID}.
  \item \textbf{Identity revocation.} \texttt{OpRevokeKey} signed under the
    revoked key plus a profile-pinned reason code.
  \item \textbf{Session authorisation.} \texttt{OpAuthorizeSession} binds an
    ephemeral session pubkey under an account's long-term identity key, for
    bounded TTL and bounded capability scope.
  \item \textbf{Transaction authorisation.}
    \texttt{TxAuthEnvelope} (\texttt{protocol/auth/tx\_envelope.go:108}),
    17 fields TupleHash256-bound under \texttt{LUX\_TX\_AUTH\_V1}. Replaces
    RLP-keccak for raw transactions.
  \item \textbf{Smart-contract permits.} \texttt{PQPermit}
    (\texttt{protocol/auth/permit.go:70}), 11 fields under
    \texttt{LUX\_PQ\_PERMIT\_V1}. Replaces EIP-2612.
  \item \textbf{Z-Chain commitment.} \texttt{ZRegistryRoots} seven-root
    aggregator (\texttt{registry/roots.go:40}) bound under
    \texttt{LUX\_ZCHAIN\_REGISTRY\_ROOTS\_V1}.
  \item \textbf{Execution-side auth verification.}
    \texttt{VerifyAuthPassed} (\texttt{registry/auth\_proof.go:118}) hot-path
    Merkle-inclusion-proof verifier consumed by the EVM, S-VM, and X-VM
    state transition functions.
  \item \textbf{Finality binding.} Q-Chain
    \texttt{ComputeRoundDigest} binds 23 axes (canonical QBlock layout)
    consumed by the Pulsar finality certificate.
  \item \textbf{Finality signature.} Pulsar-65
    (\texttt{luxfi/pulsar}), Module-LWE threshold scheme over a
    43-of-64 committee with identifiable abort. No BLS fallback.
  \item \textbf{Proof verification.} p3q
    (\texttt{luxfi/p3q@v0.0.1}, ten crates on crates.io) --- STARK / FRI
    over SHA3 Merkle commitments. No KZG, no BN254, no Groth16 wrapper.
  \item \textbf{P2P transport.} ML-KEM-768 / ML-KEM-1024 KEM and ML-DSA-65
    static identity (\texttt{luxfi/node@dc906d281b}), five cSHAKE
    customizations.
  \item \textbf{Bridge admission.} \texttt{BridgeProfile}
    (\texttt{luxfi/bridge@v1.0.7}) refused-primitive list, \texttt{RPC} label
    \texttt{post\_quantum\_end\_to\_end} as the affirmative public statement.
\end{enumerate}

This paper is the surface-by-surface specification of the fourteen claims.

\subsection{Why not just classical compat}

Two well-defined regimes coexist on Lux: the strict-PQ regime LUX\_STRICT\_E2E\_PQ
specifies, and a classical-compat regime under
\texttt{ProfileLuxPermissive} that the testnet / devnet uses for migration
testing and that retains an EIP-1559 / EIP-2930 / legacy raw-tx path. The
classical-compat regime is the responsibility of the
\texttt{ClassicalCompatRegistry} (\texttt{luxfi/node}, mempool admission), is
gated by the profile bit \texttt{RequireTypedTxAuth}, and never appears on a
locked production profile. Section~\ref{sec:security-considerations}
discusses what classical-compat means precisely; the short answer is that on
LUX\_STRICT\_E2E\_PQ the mempool refuses any transaction that is not a
\texttt{TxAuthEnvelope}, full stop.

The classical-compat path exists because a phased migration is the only
realistic posture for a chain that hosts billions of dollars of incumbent
contracts. The strict-PQ path is what mainnet finalises under once the
migration window closes (HIP-0078 §6 names the cut-over event). This paper
specifies the strict-PQ path. The permissive path is not a fallback at the
strict-PQ profile level; it is a distinct profile with its own digest.
