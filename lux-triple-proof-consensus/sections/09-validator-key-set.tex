\section{Validator Key Set}


Each Lux validator maintains four cryptographic keys:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
\textbf{Key Type} & \textbf{Crypto} & \textbf{Purpose} & \textbf{NIST Standard} \\
\midrule
EC & secp256k1 (ECDSA) & EVM transactions, addresses & --- \\
BLS & BLS12-381 & Consensus aggregation & --- \\
ML-DSA & Dilithium-65 & PQ identity & FIPS 204 \\
Pulsar & Module-LWE & PQ anonymous signing & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The ML-DSA key is the validator's \textit{long-term identity}. It certifies the BLS and Pulsar keys via ML-DSA signatures stored at validator registration (not per-block). If BLS is broken, the ML-DSA certificates prove which validator owned which BLS key.
