\section{Introduction}

\label{sec:introduction}
%% ============================================================================

Shor's algorithm~\cite{shor1994} breaks the elliptic-curve discrete logarithm
problem in polynomial time on a cryptographically relevant quantum computer
(CRQC). Every BLS, ECDSA, and Schnorr-based blockchain is therefore vulnerable
to a harvest-now-decrypt-later attack: an adversary records signed blocks
today and forges certificates once a CRQC becomes available. NIST estimates
this threshold at 2030--2035~\cite{mosca2018}.

The standard response---swap a classical signature for a post-quantum
one---is insufficient for two reasons. First, no single post-quantum scheme
has survived the cryptanalytic decade that broke each successive ``next big
thing'' in classical cryptography; relying on a single post-quantum
assumption swaps one single point of failure for another. Second, the natural
post-quantum signatures are large: ML-DSA-65 (FIPS~204) signatures are
$3{,}309$ bytes versus 48 bytes for an aggregated BLS signature, so a
straightforward per-validator ML-DSA scheme increases header size by two
orders of magnitude and degrades network throughput.

Quasar 3.0, activated on the Lux primary network on 25 December 2025,
resolves both problems in a single artifact. Each finalized block carries a
\emph{QuasarCert} composed of three independent verification lanes:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,label=\textbf{Lane \arabic*:}]
\item \textbf{BLS} --- a 48-byte BLS12-381 aggregate signature on
      $\mathbb{G}_1$, providing the classical fast path and rapid
      catch-up verification.
\item \textbf{Corona} --- a two-round Module-LWE threshold signature
      whose key material is rooted on Q-Chain via an epoch-bound DKG
      ceremony~\cite{corona}.
\item \textbf{MLDSAGroth16} --- a Z-Chain Groth16 rollup proof that attests
      to $N$ validator ML-DSA-65 signatures, encoded as a 192-byte SNARK
      plus a 32-byte public-inputs hash. \emph{This is not a raw
      per-validator ML-DSA list.} The Z-Chain replaces the $N \times 3{,}309$
      byte attestation with one pairing check.
\end{enumerate}

The three lanes rest on three algebraically independent hardness assumptions:
discrete logarithm on BLS12-381 (broken by Shor), Module-LWE for both
Corona and ML-DSA (with disjoint parameters), and Module-SIS underlying
ML-DSA's commitment relation. No known reduction connects the hardness of
one lane to another. An adversary must simultaneously break all three to
forge a QuasarCert; any compromise that leaves at least one lane intact
preserves safety. The third lane in particular forecloses the alternative of
\emph{threshold} ML-DSA: as detailed in Appendix~A of the companion
soundness proof~\cite{qcert-proof} and in Section~\ref{sec:why-not-threshold},
threshold ML-DSA is non-standardized, requires unsafe relaxation of FIPS~204
rejection sampling, and cannot be deployed in production. The Z-Chain
Groth16 rollup gives the same on-chain footprint as a threshold scheme
without altering the underlying primitive.

\subsection{The P/Q/Z/A/B/M/F Chain Pipeline}

QuasarCert generation is distributed across seven specialized chains, each
contributing a chain-specific Merkle root that is bound into the
certificate subject (Section~\ref{sec:chain-sep},
Section~\ref{sec:replay}):

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item \textbf{P-Chain} --- validator and stake authority; emits
      $\mathsf{pchain\_validator\_root}$.
\item \textbf{Q-Chain} --- Corona consensus DKG; emits
      $\mathsf{qchain\_ceremony\_root}$.
\item \textbf{Z-Chain} --- Groth16 verifying-key registry and rollup; emits
      $\mathsf{zchain\_vk\_root}$.
\item \textbf{A-Chain} --- unified attestation chain; emits
      $\mathsf{achain\_attestation\_root}$.
\item \textbf{B-Chain} --- bridge messaging chain; emits
      $\mathsf{bchain\_bridge\_root}$.
\item \textbf{M-Chain} --- MPC ceremonies (CGGMP21, FROST,
      Corona-general); emits the $\mathsf{mchain\_mpc\_root}$.
\item \textbf{F-Chain} --- FHE compute (TFHE, encrypted EVM); emits the
      $\mathsf{fchain\_fhe\_root}$.
\end{itemize}

M-Chain and F-Chain share an internal substrate library
(\texttt{lux/chains/thresholdvm}) but are operationally orthogonal. There
is \emph{no} monolithic ``T-Chain''; MPC and FHE are independent chains
with distinct validator sets, ceremonies, and failure domains.

\subsection{Replay Protection}

Every QuasarCert is bound to a \emph{certificate subject}:
\[
\mathsf{cert\_subject} \;=\; \mathsf{keccak}\!\bigl(
\mathsf{chain\_id} \mathbin\| \mathsf{epoch} \mathbin\| \mathsf{round}
\mathbin\| \mathsf{mode} \mathbin\|
P_\text{val} \mathbin\| Q_\text{cer} \mathbin\| Z_\text{vk} \mathbin\|
A_\text{att} \mathbin\| B_\text{br} \mathbin\| M_\text{mpc} \mathbin\|
F_\text{fhe} \mathbin\| h_{\text{parent}} \mathbin\| \mathsf{state\_root}
\mathbin\| \mathsf{exec\_root} \mathbin\| \mathsf{gas\_limit} \mathbin\|
\mathsf{base\_fee} \bigr).
\]
All three lanes sign this exact 32-byte digest. Any change to validator
set, epoch, round, mode, parent state, or execution context alters the
subject, so a certificate cannot be replayed across chains, epochs,
rounds, or forks. We prove this is structurally complete in
Section~\ref{sec:replay}.

\subsection{GPU-Native Verification}

The three lanes are verified independently. BLS aggregate verification
($n{=}100$) executes in ${\sim}875\,\mu$s of CPU; Corona batch verify is
$O(1)$ amortized after DKG; the Groth16 pairing check runs in
200--500\,$\mu$s when batched on a GPU. On the 21-validator mainnet board,
the full three-lane verifier completes in 0.5--1.5\,ms, well inside the
500\,ms target block time. The QuasarGPU adapter (LP-132)
schedules these lanes onto the same kernel stream that drives EVM
execution, so consensus verification consumes no incremental
host-CPU budget.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item A three-lane finality certificate (QuasarCert) whose unforgeability
      reduces simultaneously to discrete logarithm on BLS12-381,
      Module-LWE for Corona, and MLWE/MSIS for ML-DSA, with the
      Z-Chain Groth16 lane bringing the third lane on-chain in
      $192{+}32$ bytes regardless of $N$.
\item A formal certificate-subject construction binding seven chain
      roots, mode, and execution context, ruling out cross-chain and
      cross-epoch replay structurally
      (Theorem~\ref{thm:replay-impossible}).
\item Theorems for safety, liveness, single-compromise insufficiency,
      QuasarCert soundness, parallel liveness, and post-quantum safety,
      with machine-checked proofs in Lean~4 and Tamarin and a companion
      proof sketch~\cite{qcert-proof}.
\item Production benchmarks for the 25 December 2025 release: 0.5--1.5\,ms
      per-cert verify on GPU at $n{=}21$, 500\,ms block time, and
      a sustained throughput target of $\geq$100K TPS per chain.
\end{enumerate}
