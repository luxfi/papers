\section{The Compact-Certificate Evidence Layer (Released, \texttt{consensus v1.26.0})}
\label{sec:compact-cert}
%% ============================================================================

The three-lane QuasarCert of \S\ref{sec:quasarcert} is refined, in the
released implementation (\texttt{github.com/luxfi/consensus}, tag
\texttt{v1.26.0}, package \texttt{protocol/quasar}), into a typed
\emph{compact-certificate} envelope (\texttt{ConsensusCert}). The refinement
makes three changes, all motivated by the arrival of a byte-equal threshold
ML-DSA seal (Pulsar / TALUS, LP-073~\cite{lp073}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item the post-quantum threshold seal becomes the \emph{primary} finality
        object --- one Pulsar threshold signature compresses the entire
        quorum to a single FIPS-204 signature --- so the per-validator
        ML-DSA Groth16 rollup of \S\ref{sec:lane3} is demoted to a
        \emph{fallback} evidence kind;
  \item every threshold lane shares one group-key-per-era (\texttt{KeyEra});
  \item relying parties select finality posture from an operator-facing
        policy table over the evidence kinds.
\end{enumerate}
The envelope is $O(1)$ in committee size.

\subsection{Four typed evidence kinds}
\label{sec:compact-cert:kinds}

The envelope carries evidence drawn from exactly four typed kinds
(\texttt{compact\_evidence.go}):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item \texttt{EvidenceBeamBLS} (\texttt{"beam-bls"}) --- the classical
        BLS12-381 aggregate (the Beam).
  \item \texttt{EvidencePulsarThresholdMLDSA}
        (\texttt{"pulsar-threshold-mldsa"}) --- one Pulsar (TALUS) threshold
        signature, byte-equal to FIPS-204 ML-DSA and verifying under the
        \emph{stock} FIPS-204 verifier.
  \item \texttt{EvidenceCoronaRingtail} (\texttt{"corona-ringtail"}) --- one
        Corona Ring-LWE threshold signature (the natively-dealerless leg,
        LP-220~\cite{corona}).
  \item \texttt{EvidenceP3QMLDSARollup} (\texttt{"p3q-mldsa-rollup"}) ---
        the \emph{fallback}: a rollup of $N$ per-validator ML-DSA signatures
        (the Lane-3 construction of \S\ref{sec:lane3}), bound to the
        committed validator set.
\end{itemize}
Beam, Pulsar, and Corona are each \emph{one} $O(1)$ signature. P3Q is the
only kind whose footprint grows with $N$, and it is used only as a fallback
(migration, recovery, bridge, audit), never as the primary finality object
when a compact Pulsar threshold leg is available.

\begin{remark}[Four kinds, not four legs]
Underneath, dispatch resolves each \texttt{EvidenceKind} to a
\texttt{(LegKind, EvidenceMode)} pair. A fifth leg kind --- Magnetar /
SLH-DSA (FIPS-205, the Polaris profile) --- exists \emph{outside} this
four-kind vocabulary for hash-based cross-family diversity; it is not one of
the four compact evidence kinds, and STRICT finality does not require it.
\end{remark}

\subsection{\texttt{KeyEra}: one group key per era (value, not place)}
\label{sec:compact-cert:keyera}

Each threshold kind verifies against a single group public key pinned to one
validator-set era. The released code decomplects this into a single generic
\texttt{KeyEra} type (\texttt{key\_era.go}): a registry record qualified by
its \texttt{SchemeID} (ML-DSA \emph{vs} Ringtail), not a Pulsar-specific
place --- \emph{one type, all threshold lanes}. The same \texttt{KeyEra}
serves both the Pulsar and the Corona lane; the \texttt{SchemeID} says how to
interpret the group-key bytes. Beam (a classical aggregate) and P3Q (a
per-validator rollup) are \emph{not} compact group-key threshold lanes and
are explicitly refused by the era-suite resolver. One group key verifies a
whole committee's threshold signature; this is precisely what makes the
certificate $O(1)$.

\subsection{The policy table: operator-facing posture}
\label{sec:compact-cert:policy}

A relying party selects a posture from a four-row policy table
(\texttt{cert\_policy\_table.go}). Each posture names the set of evidence
legs that MUST be present and verify (AND-composition):

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Posture} & \textbf{Required legs} & \textbf{Use} \\
\midrule
\texttt{BLS\_FAST}             & $\mathrm{Beam}$
  & classical fast path (no PQ) \\
\texttt{HYBRID\_PQ\_CHECKPOINT} & $\mathrm{Beam} \wedge (\mathrm{Pulsar} \vee \mathrm{P3Q})$
  & PQ checkpoint (compact leg or fallback) \\
\texttt{STRICT\_QUASAR}        & $\mathrm{Beam} \wedge \mathrm{Pulsar} \wedge \mathrm{Corona}$
  & dual-lattice AND-mode strict finality \\
\texttt{RECOVERY\_MODE}        & $\mathrm{Beam} \wedge \mathrm{P3Q}$
  & degraded path (compact legs unavailable) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The four released finality postures. \texttt{STRICT\_QUASAR} is the
never-trust-a-single-lattice-leg posture: it requires both the Pulsar
(byte-FIPS-204) and the Corona (Ring-LWE) threshold seal, and it
structurally refuses the P3Q rollup mode. \texttt{HYBRID\_PQ\_CHECKPOINT}
accepts either the compact Pulsar leg \emph{or} the P3Q fallback.}
\label{tab:policy}
\end{table}

Enforcement (\texttt{VerifyConsensusCert}) is a straight AND: the verifier
rejects the certificate if any required leg is missing, is disallowed by the
posture, or fails its per-lane verifier, plus a cardinality check that each
required leg appears exactly once. Because every required leg must verify,
an adversary must defeat \emph{every} lane of the chosen posture
simultaneously; under \texttt{STRICT\_QUASAR} that means breaking BLS
\emph{and} Pulsar's Module-LWE/MSIS \emph{and} Corona's Ring-LWE at once.

\subsection{Compact: $O(1)$ not $O(N)$}
\label{sec:compact-cert:compact}

Because Beam, Pulsar, and Corona are each a single aggregate / threshold
signature, the compact checkpoint footprint is invariant in committee size
$N$. The released proof corpus asserts the compact-to-naive ratio is at
least $100\times$ and \emph{measures} it at runtime
(\texttt{TestCompact\_O1NotON\_For1000Validators}): at $N{=}1000$ validators
(ML-DSA-65), a Beam$+$Pulsar compact checkpoint is $3{,}466$ bytes versus a
naive $1000$-signature object of $3{,}320{,}000$ bytes --- a measured
$957\times$ reduction. (The $957\times$ is the measured value; only
$\geq 100\times$ is asserted as a bound.)

This is the point of promoting Pulsar to the primary post-quantum object.
The Lane-3 Groth16 rollup (\S\ref{sec:lane3}) compresses $N$ per-validator
ML-DSA signatures into a $192$-byte SNARK plus a public-inputs hash, but it
still requires $N$ \emph{individual} signatures and a trusted-setup SNARK.
The Pulsar threshold leg instead compresses the \emph{quorum} to one
FIPS-204 signature --- no per-validator list, no trusted setup, no
pairing-based (Shor-breakable) SNARK in the finality root --- and P3Q remains
only as the fallback for when a compact threshold leg cannot be produced.

\subsection{A deliberately boring verifier}
\label{sec:compact-cert:verifier}

The certificate verifier is deliberately straight-line. It dispatches each
required kind to exactly one per-lane verifier --- Beam $\to$ BLS aggregate,
Pulsar $\to$ \emph{stock} FIPS-204 ML-DSA, Corona $\to$ Ring-LWE --- checks
each in its own lane through the suite registry, and ANDs the results. There
are no fast paths and no clever shortcuts. The verifier is \emph{not}
constant-time: it early-returns on the first failing leg. This is
appropriate here because every input is public (signatures, group keys, the
finality message $M$); there is no secret-dependent branch. Cross-lane
confusion is structurally impossible --- the suite registry rejects any
suite whose kind does not match the verifier's lane
(\texttt{ErrSuiteLaneMismatch}).

\subsection{Decomplected extensions}
\label{sec:compact-cert:decomplect}

The dealerless post-quantum core (\texttt{consensus} $+$ \texttt{pulsar} $+$
\texttt{corona}) depends on neither the optional MPC custody extension
(\texttt{luxfi/mpc}) nor the optional TEE custody extension
(\texttt{luxfi/tee}); the quasar package imports neither. Hardware
attestation, where a deployment uses it, is a separate single verifier
(\texttt{luxfi/cc}) exposing orthogonal vendor Kinds: AMD SEV-SNP and NVIDIA
NVTrust are production verifiers, Intel TDX is a fail-loud stub, and further
vendor Kinds are the framework's extension surface. The compact-certificate
evidence layer above is independent of all three.
