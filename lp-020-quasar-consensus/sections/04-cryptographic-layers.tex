\section{Three-Lane Certificate Architecture}

\label{sec:crypto}
%% ============================================================================

A QuasarCert is the conjunction of three independent verification
\emph{lanes}. Each lane has its own primitive, its own hardness
assumption, and its own verifier. None of the three depends on the others
to verify, and the three are scheduled and executed in parallel both
during signing (validator-side) and during verification (light-client and
peer-side). This section formalizes each lane.

\subsection{Lane 1: BLS12-381 Aggregate Signatures}

BLS12-381~\cite{boneh2001short} operates over the pairing-friendly curve
$E(\mathbb{F}_p)$ with embedding degree 12 and 128-bit classical security.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item \textbf{Key generation}: $\mathit{sk} \xleftarrow{\$} \mathbb{Z}_r$;
      $\mathit{pk} = \mathit{sk} \cdot G_2$, with proof-of-possession.
\item \textbf{Signing}: $\sigma = \mathit{sk} \cdot
      H_{\mathbb{G}_1}(\mathsf{cert\_subject})$.
\item \textbf{Aggregation}: $\sigma_{\mathrm{agg}} = \sum_{i \in Q}
      \sigma_i$. Aggregated size is 48 bytes regardless of $|Q|$.
\item \textbf{Verification}: $e(G_2, \sigma_{\mathrm{agg}}) =
      e(\mathit{apk}, H_{\mathbb{G}_1}(\mathsf{cert\_subject}))$ where
      $\mathit{apk} = \sum_{i \in Q} \mathit{pk}_i$.
\end{itemize}

BLS provides the classical fast path. Verification cost is $O(1)$ in $|Q|$
and dominated by two pairings; an aggregate-of-aggregates check at
$n{=}100$ runs in ${\sim}875\,\mu$s on commodity x86 CPU
(Section~\ref{sec:performance}). This lane is broken by Shor; the other
two lanes compensate.

\subsection{Lane 2: Corona (Ring-LWE Threshold Signatures)}

Corona~\cite{corona} is a lattice-based $t$-of-$n$ threshold
signature scheme over the polynomial ring $R_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[X]/(X^N+1)$
with parameters $N = 256$, $q = 2^{48} + 2561$ (NTT-friendly), module
dimension 8, and threshold $t = \lfloor 2n/3 \rfloor + 1$.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item \textbf{DKG}: Q-Chain runs the Corona DKG once per epoch,
      producing the combined public key $\mathit{pk}^{\mathrm{RT}}$ and
      Shamir-shared secrets $\mathit{sk}_i^{\mathrm{RT}}$. The DKG
      transcript hash is $\mathsf{qchain\_ceremony\_root}$.
\item \textbf{Round 1 (precomputation)}: each party $P_i$ samples a nonce
      $\mathbf{y}_i \sim D_{\sigma'}^N$ from PRF-correlated randomness,
      commits $\mathbf{w}_i = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{y}_i$, and broadcasts
      $\mathbf{w}_i$ with a MAC.
\item \textbf{Round 2 (signing)}: given message
      $m = \mathsf{cert\_subject}$, compute challenge
      $c = H(m \,\|\, \sum_i \mathbf{w}_i)$, response
      $\mathbf{z}_i = \mathbf{y}_i + c \cdot \mathit{sk}_i^{\mathrm{RT}}$,
      reject if $\|\mathbf{z}_i\|$ exceeds the bound (rejection sampling).
\item \textbf{Combination}: the Q-Chain combiner reconstructs
      $\mathbf{z} = \sum_{i \in T} \lambda_i \mathbf{z}_i$ via Lagrange
      interpolation over the ring.
\item \textbf{Verification}: $\|\mathbf{z}\| \leq B$ and
      $H(m \,\|\, \mathbf{A}\mathbf{z} - c\mathbf{t}) = c$.
      \emph{Batch verify is $O(1)$ amortized after DKG} because the
      verifier holds a fixed $\mathit{pk}^{\mathrm{RT}}$.
\end{itemize}

Corona's security reduces to Module-LWE/SIS; the production parameters
provide $> 128$-bit classical and $> 130$-bit quantum security
(Appendix~E of~\cite{qcert-proof}).

\subsection{Lane 3: MLDSAGroth16 (Z-Chain Rollup of ML-DSA-65)}
\label{sec:lane3}

The third lane is the conceptual heart of Quasar 3.0. The naive design
would carry $N$ raw ML-DSA-65 validator signatures in every block header,
costing $N \times 3{,}309$ bytes (e.g.\ ${\sim}69$\,KB at $N{=}21$,
${\sim}330$\,KB at $N{=}100$) and $N \times 254\,\mu$s per verifier. We
instead delegate ML-DSA verification to a Z-Chain Groth16 rollup. The
on-chain artifact reduces from $N \times 3{,}309$ bytes to a fixed
\textbf{192-byte SNARK plus 32-byte public-inputs hash}, irrespective of
$N$.

\paragraph{Primitive: ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204).}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item Public key 1{,}952 B; secret key 4{,}032 B; signature 3{,}309 B.
\item NIST Level 3, $\geq$128-bit quantum security.
\item Verification: 254\,$\mu$s CPU (cold), 3\,$\mu$s with cached
      challenge (hot path).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Z-Chain rollup statement.}
The MLDSAGroth16 lane is a non-interactive zero-knowledge argument for
the relation
\[
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{ZK}} \;=\; \Bigl\{\,
\bigl(x;\, w\bigr)
\;:\;
x = (\mathsf{cert\_subject},\, h_x)
\,\land\,
w = \bigl(\{\mathit{pk}_i^{\mathrm{ML}}\}_{i=1}^N,
\{\sigma_i^{\mathrm{ML}}\}_{i=1}^N\bigr)
\,\land\,\Phi(x, w)\,\Bigr\}
\]
where $\Phi(x, w)$ holds iff:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item $h_x = H_{\text{Poseidon}}\bigl(\{\mathit{pk}_i^{\mathrm{ML}}\}_{i=1}^N
      \,\|\, \mathsf{cert\_subject}\bigr)$.
\item $\sum_{i=1}^{N} [\![ \mathsf{MLDSA.Verify}(\mathit{pk}_i^{\mathrm{ML}},
      \mathsf{cert\_subject}, \sigma_i^{\mathrm{ML}}) = 1 ]\!] \;\geq\;
      t = \lfloor 2N/3 \rfloor + 1$.
\item Each $\mathit{pk}_i^{\mathrm{ML}}$ appears in
      $P_\text{val}$ (the validator-set Merkle root) at the binding
      epoch.
\end{enumerate}

The Groth16 prover, executed by Z-Chain validators, produces
$\pi_{\mathrm{ZK}} = (A, B, C) \in \mathbb{G}_1 \times \mathbb{G}_2
\times \mathbb{G}_1$ totalling 192 bytes (compressed), and emits the
public-inputs hash $h_x$ (32 bytes).

\paragraph{Why this is not a raw per-validator ML-DSA list.}
The rollup is a \emph{single} succinct argument. The verifier never sees
individual ML-DSA signatures; it sees only $(\pi_{\mathrm{ZK}}, h_x)$.
This is critical for two reasons. First, on-chain footprint: the per-block
post-quantum identity-binding cost is $192 + 32$ bytes regardless of
validator count. Second, verification cost: $\mathsf{Groth16.Verify}$ is
three pairings, executed in 200--500\,$\mu$s when batched on a GPU
(Section~\ref{sec:gpu-native}).

\paragraph{Rejection of threshold ML-DSA.}
A different design would replace per-validator ML-DSA with a threshold
ML-DSA scheme. We reject this for the reasons elaborated in
Section~\ref{sec:why-not-threshold} and Appendix~A
of~\cite{qcert-proof}: ML-DSA's Fiat--Shamir-with-Aborts requires
rejection sampling on the \emph{combined} response $\mathbf{z}$, which
no party can evaluate locally; relaxing the rejection bound to
accommodate threshold signing reduces the security margin and is not
standardized by NIST. The Z-Chain Groth16 rollup gives the same
on-chain footprint as a threshold scheme \emph{without altering the
underlying primitive}---each validator runs the FIPS-204 ML-DSA-65
algorithm exactly as standardized, and the rollup proves the existence
of a quorum of valid individual signatures.

\paragraph{Trusted setup.}
Z-Chain's Groth16 verifying key is the output of a multi-party trusted
setup ceremony (powers-of-tau plus circuit-specific phase 2, with $\geq
1024$ contributors). The verifying-key hash is rooted on Z-Chain as
$\mathsf{zchain\_vk\_root}$ and bound into the certificate subject. A
post-setup-compromise attacker can forge $\pi_{\mathrm{ZK}}$ but still
cannot produce a Corona signature or break BLS, so single-compromise
insufficiency (Theorem~\ref{thm:single}) holds.
