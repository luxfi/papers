\begin{abstract}
\noindent Lux is not merely adding post-quantum signatures to a chain; it defines a hybrid finality architecture for DAG-native consensus, with protocol-agnostic threshold lifecycle, post-quantum threshold sealing, and cross-chain propagation of Horizon finality.

We present Quasar 3.0, the post-quantum Byzantine fault tolerant consensus
protocol activated on the Lux primary network on 25 December 2025. Quasar 3.0
finalizes a block by producing a single \emph{QuasarCert} that aggregates
three independent verification \emph{lanes}: a BLS12-381 aggregate signature
(classical fast path, discrete logarithm), a Corona two-round Module-LWE
threshold signature (Q-Chain ceremony root), and a Z-Chain Groth16 proof that
rolls up $N$ ML-DSA-65 validator signatures into a single 192-byte SNARK plus
a 32-byte public-inputs hash. The third lane is \emph{not} a raw per-validator
ML-DSA list; it is a Z-Chain proof artifact, allowing the per-block on-chain
cost of post-quantum identity binding to drop from $N \times 3{,}309$ bytes
to $192 + 32$ bytes regardless of validator count. Every QuasarCert is bound
to a \emph{certificate subject} that hashes the chain id, epoch, round, mode,
and the seven chain-specific roots
($P_\text{val} \mathbin\| Q_\text{cer} \mathbin\| Z_\text{vk} \mathbin\|
A_\text{att} \mathbin\| B_\text{br} \mathbin\| M_\text{mpc} \mathbin\|
F_\text{fhe}$) together with the parent block hash, parent state root,
parent execution root, gas limit, and base fee, making cross-epoch and
cross-chain replay structurally impossible. The three lanes are verified
independently by the QuasarGPU substrate (LP-132): BLS aggregate verify
(${\sim}875\,\mu$s for $n{=}100$ on CPU), Corona batch verify ($O(1)$
amortized after DKG), and a single Groth16 pairing check (200--500\,$\mu$s
GPU batched). On a 21-validator mainnet board, full three-lane GPU
verification runs in 0.5--1.5\,ms; the network targets a 500\,ms block time
and sustained $\geq$100K TPS per chain. An adversary must simultaneously
break the discrete logarithm on BLS12-381, Module-LWE for Corona, and
Module-LWE/MSIS for ML-DSA to forge a QuasarCert; breaking any one or any
two leaves the remaining lane intact.

\textbf{Released refinement (\texttt{consensus v1.26.0}).} The
implementation refines this three-lane certificate into a typed
\emph{compact-certificate} envelope (\S\ref{sec:compact-cert}) over four
evidence kinds --- Beam-BLS, a Pulsar threshold ML-DSA seal (byte-equal to
FIPS-204), a Corona Module-LWE (Ringtail/Raccoon) threshold seal, and a P3Q
ML-DSA rollup. The
Pulsar threshold leg compresses the entire quorum to a \emph{single}
FIPS-204 signature, so the per-validator ML-DSA rollup of \S\ref{sec:lane3}
is retained only as a \emph{fallback} kind; the strict posture is the
dual-lattice AND $\mathrm{Beam} \wedge \mathrm{Pulsar} \wedge \mathrm{Corona}$,
with one group-key-per-era (\texttt{KeyEra}) shared across the threshold
lanes. The envelope is $O(1)$ in validator count --- a measured $957\times$
reduction at $N{=}1000$.
\end{abstract}
