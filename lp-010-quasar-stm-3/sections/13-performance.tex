\section{Performance}

\label{sec:performance}
%% ============================================================================

All measurements were taken on commodity hardware (Apple M1~Max,
10~CPU cores, 32~GPU cores, 64\,GB unified memory) under macOS,
with the Lux \texttt{cevm} substrate at the QuasarSTM~3.0 spec-freeze
revision (December~15, 2025). The CUDA backend was sanity-checked
on an NVIDIA H100 (80\,GB) with identical structural parity.

\toprule

\textbf{Repairs} & \textbf{Commits} \\
\midrule
\texttt{block\_stm\_independent\_txs} (64 disjoint keys) & 0   & 0   & 64 \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Block-STM behaviour under disjoint-key and same-key
contention. The 16 same-key transactions produce exactly
$\binom{16}{2} = 120$ pairwise conflicts and $120$ repairs,

\cite{gelashvili2022blockstm} reference behaviour, on GPU.}
\label{tab:hot-key}
\end{table}

\begin{remark}[Why the conflict count is exact]
\label{rem:exact-conflict}
The pairwise count $120$ is structural: under canonical-index
ordering, transaction $T_j$ at position $j$ conflicts with each
of $T_0, \ldots, T_{j-1}$ on the shared key. Summing over $j$ from
$1$ to $15$ yields $\sum_{j=1}^{15} j = 120$. The value is the
same on Metal and CUDA and on the CPU reference, by
Invariant~\ref{inv:cross-backend}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Cross-Backend Determinism}

The Metal and CUDA backends share the layout types
(\texttt{quasar\_gpu\_layout.hpp}) byte-for-byte; structural drift is
caught by \texttt{static\_assert} at compile time. The CPU reference
(\texttt{evmgpu} Go) is the differential-fuzzing oracle for both GPU
backends. The 19-binary GPU test surface
(parity, modes, host-bridge, pipeline, unified, gpu-state, opcodes,
dispatch, refund, access-list, stack-depth, storage-overflow, $\ldots$)
passes on M1~Max; CUDA parity on H100 is verified at v0.41.
