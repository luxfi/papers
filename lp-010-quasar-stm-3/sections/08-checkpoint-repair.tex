\section{Incremental Repair via Fiber Checkpoints}

\label{sec:repair}
%% ============================================================================

Block-STM's classical repair is full transaction re-execution at the
next incarnation. For complex EVM flows (multi-hop swap routers,
compliance precompiles, batched settlement) this is wasteful: the
invalidated read often occurs late, and re-running prefixes of pure
arithmetic and warm storage accesses dominates wall time.

QuasarSTM repairs against \emph{fiber checkpoints} taken at known
boundaries during execution. A repair rolls back to the most recent
checkpoint preceding the invalidated read, retains the warm bytecode,
calldata, journal prefix, and earlier validated reads, and resumes the
fiber from that point with the new visible version.

\subsection{Checkpoint Boundaries}

The Metal/CUDA EVM fiber takes checkpoints at six structural
boundaries:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item \textsc{Call} boundaries (\texttt{CALL}, \texttt{STATICCALL},
      \texttt{DELEGATECALL}, \texttt{CALLCODE}; deferred to v0.40+).
\item \textsc{Sload} / \textsc{Sstore} boundaries.
\item Precompile boundaries (entry and exit of any registered
      precompile).
\item Basic-block budget boundaries (every $B$ opcodes, where $B$ is
      a per-fiber budget chosen to bound checkpoint memory).
\item DEX semantic-operation boundaries (order append, cancel, match,
      fee accumulate, balance settle---the Tier-3 reducer-eligible
      operations).
\item Cold-state resume boundaries (when a fiber resumes from a
      \texttt{StateResp}, the resume point is implicitly a
      checkpoint).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{definition}[Fiber checkpoint]
\label{def:checkpoint}
A fiber checkpoint is a tuple
\[
\mathit{EvmFiberCheckpoint} =
(\mathit{tx\_id},\, \mathit{pc},\, \mathit{frame\_offset},\,
 \mathit{stack\_offset},\, \mathit{memory\_offset},\,
 \mathit{journal\_offset},\, \mathit{read\_set\_count},\,
 \mathit{gas\_used})
\]
where the offsets index into the fiber's per-round arenas. A
checkpoint is a constant-size record (typically 32\,B) that does not
copy memory; it pins arena prefixes by index.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Repair Policy}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Repair transaction $T$ after invalidated read at canonical
position $p$}
\label{alg:repair}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $K \gets$ the most recent checkpoint of $T$ with checkpoint
       position $\leq p$.
\State \textbf{if} $K$ exists \textbf{then}
\State \quad Roll back fiber: restore
       $\mathit{pc}, \mathit{stack\_offset},\,
       \mathit{memory\_offset},\, \mathit{journal\_offset},\,
       \mathit{read\_set\_count}$ to $K$'s recorded values.
\State \quad Discard read records and write records appended after
       $K.\mathit{read\_set\_count}$.
\State \quad Increment $T.\mathit{incarnation}$.
\State \quad Re-enter \textsc{Executing} state at $K.\mathit{pc}$,
       observing the current visible version.
\State \textbf{else}
\State \quad Increment $T.\mathit{incarnation}$.
\State \quad Re-enter \textsc{Executing} from start (full re-exec).
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Cost Reduction}

\begin{lemma}[Repair cost upper bound]
\label{lem:repair-cost}
Let $T$ be a transaction with fiber checkpoints at gas-cumulative
positions $0 = g_0 < g_1 < \cdots < g_K \leq G(T)$, where $G(T)$ is
$T$'s total gas. Let $g^*$ be the gas-cumulative position of the
invalidated read. Then the gas re-executed under
Algorithm~\ref{alg:repair} is bounded above by $G(T) - g_j$ where
$g_j = \max\{g_i : g_i \leq g^*\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The rollback restores state at $g_j$; execution proceeds from $g_j$
to the end. By construction $g_j \leq g^* \leq G(T)$, so the cost is
at most $G(T) - g_j$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[5--30$\times$ on routers]
\label{rem:repair-router}
On router and DEX-settlement workloads (bench~E,
\S\ref{sec:performance}), measured repair cost falls 5--30$\times$
relative to full re-execution. This is the fattest single optimisation
in the repair path; without it, hot-frontier rounds suffer
substantial repair amplification.
\end{remark}
