\begin{abstract}
Lux Financial operates a complete banking and card surface --- accounts, payments, FX, cards, and crypto settlement --- with SF Private Bank LTD (International Banking License L17601/SFPB, Union of Comoros Offshore Finance Authority) as the issuer of record. This audit records the full production surface Lux exposes today, the issuer API surface received to date (the Account Management and Virtual Cards integration guides), and the catalog of issuer APIs still required to activate every remaining product. Each requirement is stated as a concrete endpoint contract with the Lux surface it powers, priced by launch priority. The intent is a single punch list: when the APIs in Section~\ref{sec:req} are issued, the full BaaS and CaaS ships through \texttt{/v1/bank} and \texttt{/v1/cards} --- consumer at lux.finance, platform at lux.financial, and agent access through api.hanzo.ai.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Lux Financial is one ladder of products across three brand surfaces. \textbf{lux.finance} is the consumer home --- landing, membership plans (Silver \$29 / Gold \$99 / Black \$299 / Sovereign \$999), signup and login through Lux IAM (lux.id), and the demonstration surface for the native DeFi stack (Liquid Protocol lending, vaults, non-custodial wallets). \textbf{lux.financial} is the business side --- the BaaS platform for white-label partners, its documentation at docs.lux.financial, and the partner console. \textbf{lux.credit} is the card program funnel. Agents and API customers reach the same surface through Hanzo's unified endpoint, \texttt{api.hanzo.ai}, as \ep{/v1/bank} and \ep{/v1/cards}.

Behind all of it sits one issuer abstraction (\texttt{luxfi/bank}, \texttt{issuer.go}): the banking counterparty is an implementation detail selected at deployment, never an API difference. SF Private Bank is the first live issuer; Banxe (UK, distributor of Tompay LTD, FCA EMI 931509) is second in line. The abstraction is shipped and tested; what remains is upstream API coverage.

Two integration guides have been received from SF Private Bank to date. They cover the card-account and virtual-card lifecycle well, and nothing else. This paper is the complete accounting of the difference between the product surface Lux sells and the issuer surface Lux has been given.

\section{What Ships on Receipt}

The Lux side is built and released (bankd v1.3.0). The issuer abstraction means each API above lands as an implementation detail --- no client-visible change:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Identity + accounts (\S\ref{sec:idv}, \ref{sec:acct})} activate real-money signup at lux.finance: lux.id IDV $\rightarrow$ document submission $\rightarrow$ bank approval $\rightarrow$ IBAN in the dashboard. The production flip is one deployment switch (\ep{BANK\_SANDBOX=false}) plus credentials in KMS.
\item \textbf{Payments (\S\ref{sec:pay})} activate the already-exposed \ep{/v1/bank/transfers}, \ep{/v1/bank/payments/outbound}, and FX routes against real rails, with plan allowances (free wires, ACH) metered by the membership ladder.
\item \textbf{Cards (\S\ref{sec:cards})} complete \ep{/v1/bank/cards/*} --- already live end-to-end in sandbox --- into the full CaaS.
\item \textbf{Agent surface}: the same contract publishes through api.hanzo.ai as \ep{/v1/bank} and \ep{/v1/cards} for agent and API customers, with MCP tools over the same routes.
\end{itemize}

\section{Request Summary}

Requested from SF Private Bank, in order:

\begin{enumerate}
\item The full API Reference both guides cite, with base URLs and the authentication scheme; sandbox credentials.
\item User provisioning and server-to-server IDV/KYB document submission (\S\ref{sec:idv}), with KYC decision webhooks.
\item Ewallet and IBAN account opening with account details, balances, and history (\S\ref{sec:acct}).
\item Payment origination on ACH and wires with beneficiary registration and status webhooks (\S\ref{sec:pay}).
\item The card lifecycle beyond ordering: physical orders, activation, load, lock, and authorization webhooks (\S\ref{sec:cards}).
\item The webhook delivery contract and event catalog (\S\ref{sec:ops}); reconciliation and fee reporting to follow.
\end{enumerate}

With items 1--3, real-money accounts open at lux.finance. With 4, money moves. With 5--6, the complete BaaS and CaaS operates at production quality across every Lux surface.
