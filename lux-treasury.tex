\begin{abstract}
Sustainable blockchain networks require mechanisms for funding public goods, development, and ecosystem growth. This paper presents a formal framework for decentralized treasury management, addressing fund accumulation, proposal evaluation, and allocation mechanisms. We analyze the treasury as a multi-objective optimization problem under incentive constraints, prove bounds on treasury sustainability, and design allocation mechanisms that resist capture and collusion. Our implementation on the Lux network demonstrates effective grants program management with measurable ecosystem impact.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Blockchain networks generate value but face challenges in funding collective goods: core protocol development, security audits, documentation, developer tooling, and ecosystem growth. Traditional funding models---venture capital, foundations, pre-mines---concentrate control and create principal-agent problems.

Decentralized treasuries address this by:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Accumulating funds from protocol revenue (fees, inflation)
    \item Enabling stake-weighted governance over fund allocation
    \item Distributing funds to proposals that benefit the ecosystem
\end{enumerate}

This paper formalizes treasury management as a mechanism design problem and provides concrete protocols for the Lux network.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Formal model of treasury dynamics including inflows, outflows, and sustainability constraints
    \item Analysis of funding mechanisms: quadratic funding, conviction-weighted allocation, milestone-based grants
    \item Game-theoretic analysis of proposer incentives and collusion resistance
    \item Implementation specification for Lux treasury management
    \item Empirical evaluation framework for measuring grant program effectiveness
\end{itemize}

\section{Treasury Model}

\subsection{Treasury State}

\begin{definition}[Treasury]
A treasury $\mathcal{T}$ is characterized by:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Balance $B(t) \geq 0$ at time $t$
    \item Inflow rate $I(t)$ (funds entering per unit time)
    \item Outflow rate $O(t)$ (funds disbursed per unit time)
    \item Reserve ratio $\rho \in [0, 1]$ (minimum balance as fraction of peak)
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

The treasury balance evolves as:
\[
\frac{dB}{dt} = I(t) - O(t)
\]

\begin{definition}[Treasury Sustainability]
A treasury is sustainable if for all $t$:
\[
B(t) \geq \rho \cdot \max_{t' \leq t} B(t')
\]
The treasury maintains at least fraction $\rho$ of its historical maximum balance.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Inflow Mechanisms}

\subsubsection{Transaction Fee Allocation}

A fraction $\phi_f$ of transaction fees flows to the treasury:
\[
I_f(t) = \phi_f \cdot F(t)
\]
where $F(t)$ is total fees collected at time $t$.

\subsubsection{Inflation Funding}

A fraction $\phi_i$ of block rewards flows to the treasury:
\[
I_i(t) = \phi_i \cdot R(t)
\]
where $R(t)$ is the block reward at time $t$.

\subsubsection{Total Inflow}

\[
I(t) = I_f(t) + I_i(t) = \phi_f F(t) + \phi_i R(t)
\]

\subsection{Outflow Constraints}

\begin{definition}[Spending Limit]
The maximum outflow rate is bounded:
\[
O(t) \leq \min\left( O_{max}, \frac{B(t) - B_{min}}{T_{drain}} \right)
\]
where $O_{max}$ is the absolute maximum, $B_{min}$ is minimum reserve, and $T_{drain}$ is the minimum time to drain available funds.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Sustainability Bound]
Under spending limit constraints with $T_{drain} > 0$ and $B_{min} = \rho \cdot B(0)$, the treasury remains sustainable if:
\[
\mathbb{E}[I(t)] \geq \frac{(1-\rho) B(0)}{T_{drain}}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The maximum sustainable outflow rate is $(B(t) - B_{min})/T_{drain}$. Starting from $B(0)$, draining to $B_{min}$ requires time $T_{drain}$. If expected inflow exceeds this drain rate, the treasury remains above $B_{min}$ in expectation.
\end{proof}

\section{Proposal System}

\subsection{Grant Proposals}

\begin{definition}[Grant Proposal]
A grant proposal $g = (a, r, d, M, \pi)$ consists of:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Applicant address $a$
    \item Requested amount $r > 0$
    \item Description $d$ (IPFS hash of detailed proposal)
    \item Milestones $M = \{m_1, \ldots, m_k\}$ with amounts $r_i$ summing to $r$
    \item Proposer stake $\pi$ (collateral)
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Milestone]
A milestone $m_i = (d_i, r_i, t_i, c_i)$ specifies:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Deliverable description $d_i$
    \item Payment amount $r_i$
    \item Deadline $t_i$
    \item Completion criteria $c_i$
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Proposal Lifecycle}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Submission}: Proposer submits $g$ with stake $\pi \geq \pi_{min}$
    \item \textbf{Review}: Community reviews and discusses proposal
    \item \textbf{Voting}: Stake-weighted vote on approval
    \item \textbf{Execution}: Approved proposals receive milestone-based funding
    \item \textbf{Completion}: Final deliverables verified, stake returned
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Proposal Validation}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Proposal Validation}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Procedure{ValidateProposal}{$g$}
    \Require $g.r \leq R_{max}$ \Comment{Maximum single grant}
    \Require $g.\pi \geq \pi_{min}$ \Comment{Minimum stake}
    \Require $\sum_{m \in g.M} m.r = g.r$ \Comment{Milestones sum correctly}
    \Require $|g.M| \geq 2$ \Comment{At least 2 milestones}
    \Require $\forall m \in g.M: m.t > t_{current}$ \Comment{Future deadlines}
    \State \Return \texttt{VALID}
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Allocation Mechanisms}

\subsection{Direct Voting}

The simplest mechanism: stake-weighted vote on each proposal.

\begin{definition}[Direct Approval]
Proposal $g$ is approved if:
\[
\frac{\sum_{v: \text{voted YES}} s_v}{\sum_{v: \text{voted}} s_v} \geq \tau
\]
where $\tau$ is the approval threshold (e.g., $\tau = 0.6$).
\end{definition}

Approved proposals are funded in order of approval ratio until the spending limit is reached.

\subsection{Quadratic Funding}

Quadratic funding amplifies small contributions, reducing plutocratic influence.

\begin{definition}[Quadratic Funding]
For proposal $g$ receiving contributions $c_1, \ldots, c_k$ from distinct participants:
\[
F(g) = \left( \sum_{i=1}^{k} \sqrt{c_i} \right)^2
\]
The treasury matches the difference $F(g) - \sum_i c_i$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Quadratic Funding Efficiency]
Quadratic funding maximizes aggregate utility under the assumption that participant contributions reflect true valuations and utility is quasi-linear.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
See Buterin, Hitzig, and Weyl (2019). The quadratic formula is the unique mechanism satisfying certain efficiency and neutrality axioms.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Collusion Resistance}

Quadratic funding is vulnerable to Sybil attacks: splitting contributions across multiple identities increases matching.

\textbf{Mitigation}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Minimum contribution threshold
    \item Identity verification for contributors
    \item Pairwise coordination coefficients that discount contributions from coordinated groups
\end{itemize}

\begin{definition}[Pairwise Coordination Coefficient]
For contributors $i$ and $j$ with historical co-contribution pattern, define coefficient $\phi_{ij} \in [0, 1]$ where $\phi_{ij} = 0$ indicates perfect coordination (contributions discounted) and $\phi_{ij} = 1$ indicates independence.
\end{definition}

Adjusted quadratic funding:
\[
F'(g) = \sum_{i} c_i + 2 \sum_{i < j} \phi_{ij} \sqrt{c_i c_j}
\]

\subsection{Conviction Voting}

Conviction voting allocates funds based on sustained support over time.

\begin{definition}[Conviction]
For participant $p$ supporting proposal $g$ with stake $s$ starting at time $t_0$:
\[
\text{conv}_p(t) = s \cdot (1 - \alpha^{t - t_0})
\]
where $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ is the decay factor. Conviction grows from 0 to $s$ as support duration increases.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Conviction Threshold]
Proposal $g$ requesting amount $r$ is funded when total conviction exceeds:
\[
C_{threshold}(r) = \frac{r \cdot \beta}{B(t) - r}
\]
where $\beta$ is a tuning parameter. Larger requests relative to treasury require more conviction.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Conviction Fairness]
Under conviction voting, the expected time for a proposal to reach threshold is proportional to its requested amount and inversely proportional to its support.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Milestone-Based Disbursement}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Milestone Disbursement}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Procedure{ProcessMilestone}{$g, m_i$}
    \If{$t_{current} > m_i.t$}
        \State \Return \texttt{DEADLINE\_MISSED}
    \EndIf
    \State $\text{verified} \gets \text{VerifyDeliverable}(m_i.d, m_i.c)$
    \If{$\text{verified}$}
        \State Transfer $m_i.r$ to $g.a$
        \State \Return \texttt{DISBURSED}
    \Else
        \State Slash $g.\pi \cdot (m_i.r / g.r)$
        \State \Return \texttt{FAILED}
    \EndIf
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Verification Mechanisms}

Milestone verification options:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Governance Vote}: Stake-weighted vote on completion
    \item \textbf{Oracle Committee}: Designated reviewers assess deliverables
    \item \textbf{Optimistic Verification}: Approved by default unless challenged
\end{enumerate}

\begin{definition}[Optimistic Verification]
A milestone is considered complete $T_{challenge}$ time after submission unless a challenge is raised. Challenges trigger governance vote; successful challenges slash proposer stake and reward challenger.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Evaluation Criteria}

Proposals are evaluated on:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Impact}: Expected benefit to ecosystem
    \item \textbf{Feasibility}: Technical and organizational capacity
    \item \textbf{Cost-effectiveness}: Value relative to requested funds
    \item \textbf{Team}: Track record and qualifications
    \item \textbf{Alignment}: Consistency with network goals
\end{enumerate}

\begin{definition}[Proposal Score]
The composite score for proposal $g$:
\[
\text{Score}(g) = \sum_{i=1}^{5} w_i \cdot \text{Criterion}_i(g)
\]
where $w_i$ are category weights and each criterion is scored 0--10.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Conflict of Interest}

\begin{property}[Conflict Disclosure]
Voters with material interest in a proposal must disclose the conflict. Undisclosed conflicts, if discovered, result in stake slashing.
\end{property}

\begin{definition}[Recusal Requirement]
A voter must recuse if:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Voter is proposal applicant or team member
    \item Voter has financial interest exceeding threshold
    \item Voter has familial or contractual relationship with applicant
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\section{Game-Theoretic Analysis}

\subsection{Proposer Incentives}

\begin{definition}[Proposer Utility]
For proposer with grant $g$ approved:
\[
U_p = r - c - \pi \cdot P(\text{fail})
\]
where $r$ is the grant amount, $c$ is execution cost, and $P(\text{fail})$ is failure probability.
\end{definition}

Rational proposers submit only if $U_p > 0$, creating a natural filter against frivolous proposals.

\subsection{Voter Incentives}

\begin{definition}[Voter Utility]
For voter $v$ on proposal $g$:
\[
U_v = s_v \cdot \left( \text{Impact}(g) \cdot P(\text{success}) - \text{Cost}(g) \right)
\]
Voters internalize proposal impact proportional to their stake.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Stake-Weighted Efficiency]
Under stake-weighted voting, the equilibrium outcome maximizes stake-weighted social welfare.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each voter's influence is proportional to stake, and each voter maximizes stake-weighted utility. The aggregated decision maximizes total stake-weighted welfare.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Collusion Analysis}

\begin{definition}[Collusion]
A collusion is a binding agreement among voters to coordinate votes in exchange for side payments.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Collusion Cost]
Under stake-weighted voting, successful collusion requires bribes proportional to the stake margin:
\[
\text{Bribe} \geq (1 - 2\tau) \cdot S \cdot V(g)
\]
where $\tau$ is the approval threshold, $S$ is total stake, and $V(g)$ is the per-stake value of approval.
\end{theorem}

Mitigation: High approval thresholds ($\tau > 0.5$) increase collusion costs.

\section{Implementation on Lux}

\subsection{Treasury Contract}

\begin{verbatim}
type TreasuryState struct {
    Balance      uint64
    Proposals    map[ProposalID]*Proposal
    Allocations  map[ProposalID]uint64
    Parameters   TreasuryParams
}

type TreasuryParams struct {
    FeeShare       uint64  // Fraction of fees (1e18 scale)
    InflationShare uint64  // Fraction of inflation
    SpendingCap    uint64  // Max per epoch
    ReserveRatio   uint64  // Minimum reserve
}
\end{verbatim}

\subsection{Transaction Types}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \texttt{SubmitProposal(proposal, stake)} - Submit grant proposal
    \item \texttt{VoteProposal(proposalID, vote)} - Cast approval vote
    \item \texttt{SubmitMilestone(proposalID, milestoneID, evidence)} - Report completion
    \item \texttt{ChallengeMilestone(proposalID, milestoneID, reason)} - Challenge completion
    \item \texttt{ResolveMilestone(proposalID, milestoneID)} - Finalize milestone
    \item \texttt{WithdrawFunds(proposalID)} - Claim approved disbursement
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Epoch Processing}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Treasury Epoch Processing}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Procedure{ProcessEpoch}{}
    \State \Comment{Collect inflows}
    \State $B \gets B + \text{FeeShare} \cdot \text{EpochFees}$
    \State $B \gets B + \text{InflationShare} \cdot \text{EpochRewards}$

    \State \Comment{Process approved proposals}
    \State $\text{budget} \gets \min(\text{SpendingCap}, B - \text{Reserve})$
    \For{$g$ in approved proposals by score}
        \If{$g.r \leq \text{budget}$}
            \State Allocate $g.r$ to $g$
            \State $\text{budget} \gets \text{budget} - g.r$
        \EndIf
    \EndFor

    \State \Comment{Process milestones}
    \For{$m$ in pending milestones}
        \State $\text{ProcessMilestone}(m)$
    \EndFor
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Evaluation Framework}

\subsection{Treasury Health Metrics}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Balance trajectory}: $B(t)$ over time
    \item \textbf{Runway}: $B(t) / \bar{O}$ (months of funding at average outflow)
    \item \textbf{Utilization}: $O(t) / \text{SpendingCap}$ (fraction of capacity used)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Grant Program Metrics}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Completion rate}: Fraction of approved grants completing all milestones
    \item \textbf{Time to completion}: Average duration from approval to final milestone
    \item \textbf{Cost efficiency}: Actual vs. requested amounts for completed grants
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ecosystem Impact Metrics}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Developer growth}: New contributors attributable to funded projects
    \item \textbf{TVL impact}: Value locked in funded protocols
    \item \textbf{Usage metrics}: Transaction volume, active addresses from funded applications
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Dash Treasury}: First on-chain treasury with masternode voting. Limited by masternode centralization.

\textbf{Decred Treasury}: Stakeholder-funded treasury with Politeia proposal system. Our work extends with quadratic funding and conviction voting.

\textbf{Polkadot Treasury}: On-chain treasury with council and referendum. We provide more formal analysis of allocation mechanisms.

\textbf{Gitcoin Grants}: Pioneered quadratic funding for Ethereum ecosystem. We integrate QF with blockchain-native governance.

\section{Conclusion}

We presented a comprehensive framework for decentralized treasury management, covering fund accumulation, proposal evaluation, and allocation mechanisms. Our analysis establishes sustainability bounds, characterizes proposer and voter incentives, and provides collusion resistance guarantees. The implementation on Lux enables effective grants program management with measurable outcomes.

Future work includes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Retroactive public goods funding
    \item Cross-chain treasury coordination
    \item Machine learning for proposal evaluation
    \item Privacy-preserving contribution mechanisms
\end{itemize}
