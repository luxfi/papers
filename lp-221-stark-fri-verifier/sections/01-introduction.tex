\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A post-quantum smart-contract platform must verify two different
cryptographic objects on-chain:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item \textbf{Signatures} (threshold or single-party) that
  authenticate \emph{who} authored some commitment. Under Quasar
  this is the LP-218~\cite{lp218} P3Q family at slot
  \texttt{0x012205}: kind-byte dispatch over Pulsar (FIPS-204
  ML-DSA-65 threshold)~\cite{fips204}, Corona (Ring-LWE threshold),
  Magnetar (FIPS-205 SLH-DSA threshold)~\cite{fips205}.
\item \textbf{SNARK proofs} that authenticate \emph{what} was
  executed -- the correctness of an arbitrary off-chain computation
  with respect to a public-input vector. Under Quasar, this is
  LP-221: a STARK-FRI verifier at slot \texttt{0x012220}.
\end{enumerate}

These are disjoint primitives. A Pulsar signature does not assert
``the rollup batch executed correctly''; it asserts ``the entity
holding the threshold key co-signed this batch.'' A STARK proof
does not assert ``the sequencer was authorized''; it asserts
``starting from \texttt{prev\_root}, executing the batch yields
\texttt{new\_root}, and the public-input \texttt{batch\_hash}
commits to the right calldata.'' Both checks are required for a
sound ZK-rollup -- and they belong at separate precompile slots
because they verify separate statements with separate parameter
schedules, separate gas profiles, and separate audit boundaries.

\subsection{Slot \texttt{0x012220}}

LP-221 specifies a dedicated EVM precompile at slot:

\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]
0x0000000000000000000000000000000000012220
\end{lstlisting}

The slot is registered normatively in LP-300 master schema
registry~\cite{lp300}. LP-221 carries the slot informatively
for self-containment.

\subsection{Distinction from LP-218 P3Q}

The naming history is instructive. The Plonky3-fork STARK / FRI
verifier originally registered at slot \texttt{0x012205} under the
package name \texttt{p3q}, and the magic-header tag \texttt{"P3Q1"}
was threaded through every proof artifact (EasyCrypt theory, Lean
theorem, Jasmin source, dudect harness). Per Hanzo Crypto Suite
\S~5.2~\cite{hanzocrypto}, \textbf{P3Q canonically means
``Post-Quantum Pulsar Proof''} -- the on-chain unified PQ
\emph{threshold-signature} verifier dispatch (Pulsar~/ Corona~/
Magnetar). The STARK / FRI dispatch occupying slot
\texttt{0x012205} under that name was an \emph{aliasing error}.

The 2026-06-03 decomplection (Section~\ref{sec:architectural-decision})
relocates the STARK-FRI verifier to its own dedicated slot at
\texttt{0x012220} and leaves \texttt{0x012205} to LP-218 / LP-220 as
the canonical P3Q signature dispatch~\cite{lp220}.

\subsection{Why STARK-FRI for ZK rollups}

The Quasar / Lux primary network needs an on-chain SNARK verifier
for two reasons:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item \textbf{ZK-rollup batch verification.} A Tier-3 ZK rollup
  (per LP-204~\cite{lp204}) commits to Z-Chain by submitting a
  \texttt{RollupBatchTx} that carries both a Pulsar signature and a
  STARK proof. The Pulsar signature authenticates the \emph{who}
  (sequencer or threshold-group identity); the STARK proof
  authenticates the \emph{what} (correct execution of the batch
  under the rollup-VM's transition relation). Without an on-chain
  SNARK verifier, Z-Chain can only run optimistic rollups with
  fraud-proof challenge windows. With LP-221, Z-Chain accepts
  ZK-rollup batches at the cost of one precompile call per batch.
\item \textbf{PQ-safe SNARK substrate.} Most production SNARK
  verifiers (Groth16, PLONK over BN254~/ BLS12-381) are
  pairing-based and break under a sufficiently large quantum
  adversary because pairing verification reduces to discrete log on
  a pairing-friendly elliptic curve. STARK proofs over a small
  prime field with collision-resistant hash commitments have
  \emph{no algebraic structure for Shor}; their post-quantum
  security reduces to collision resistance of the underlying hash
  (here, cSHAKE256, modeled as a random oracle).
\end{enumerate}

LP-221 therefore complements LP-218 (signatures) as the strict-PQ
SNARK leg of the Quasar cryptographic suite. There is no
classical-curve assumption anywhere in the LP-221 verifier.

\subsection{Why FRI, cSHAKE256, Goldilocks}

The three choices are deliberate:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item \textbf{FRI}~\cite{frieprint,starkeprint} is the canonical
  low-degree test for STARKs. Soundness reduces to a Reed-Solomon
  decoding bound; no trusted setup.
\item \textbf{cSHAKE256}~\cite{fips202,sp800185} is the FIPS-202
  / SP-800-185 customizable extendable-output function.
  Domain-separated Merkle commitments use cSHAKE256 rather than a
  Merkle-tree hash baked from a non-standard sponge. The
  standardization gates conformance and audit.
\item \textbf{Goldilocks} ($p = 2^{64} - 2^{32} + 1$) is a 64-bit
  prime field~\cite{goldilocks} with fast modular arithmetic on
  AArch64~/ x86\_64, native register width, and a smooth
  multiplicative subgroup of order $2^{32}$. Plonky3~\cite{plonky3}
  and RISC Zero converged on Goldilocks for the same reasons.
\end{itemize}

Combined: a verifier with $\sim$\SI{30}{\kilo\byte} proof size
(measured for typical Plonky3-fork batches),
$\sim$\SIrange{5}{10}{\milli\second} CPU verify on a modern x86\_64
core, and PQ security under the random-oracle model for cSHAKE256.
Throughput on Blackwell sm\_120 is projected at $\sim$\SI{50}{\micro\second}
per verify (LP-203~\cite{lp203} GPU verify dispatch; not yet
measured at LP-221 publish time).
