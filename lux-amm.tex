\begin{abstract}
We present a formal analysis of constant product automated market makers (AMMs) as implemented on the Lux blockchain. The constant product invariant $x \cdot y = k$ provides a simple yet powerful mechanism for decentralized exchange without order books. We derive the mathematical properties of liquidity provision, price impact, impermanent loss, and fee accrual. Our analysis demonstrates that constant product AMMs achieve efficient price discovery while maintaining permissionless liquidity provision. We provide rigorous proofs of key properties and discuss implementation considerations for high-throughput blockchain environments.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Decentralized exchanges (DEXs) enable trustless asset swaps without centralized intermediaries. Traditional order book models require significant infrastructure and liquidity coordination. Automated market makers (AMMs) offer an alternative paradigm where liquidity is provided to a smart contract that algorithmically determines prices.

The constant product market maker, popularized by Uniswap \cite{uniswap}, maintains the invariant:
\begin{equation}
x \cdot y = k
\end{equation}
where $x$ and $y$ represent the reserves of two assets and $k$ is a constant preserved across swaps.

This paper provides a rigorous mathematical treatment of constant product AMMs, analyzing their properties from first principles and deriving optimal strategies for liquidity providers and traders.

\section{Mathematical Framework}

\subsection{Constant Product Invariant}

\begin{definition}[Constant Product AMM]
A constant product AMM for assets $A$ and $B$ is defined by a state $(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}^2$ representing reserves, with the invariant:
\begin{equation}
\phi(x, y) = x \cdot y = k
\end{equation}
where $k > 0$ is constant except during liquidity events.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Marginal Price]
The marginal price of asset $A$ in terms of asset $B$ is:
\begin{equation}
P = -\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{y}{x}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Price Impact]
For a swap of $\Delta x$ units of asset $A$, the output $\Delta y$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\Delta y = \frac{y \cdot \Delta x}{x + \Delta x}
\end{equation}
and the effective price is:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{eff}} = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{y}{x + \Delta x}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The constant product invariant requires:
\begin{equation}
(x + \Delta x)(y - \Delta y) = k = xy
\end{equation}
Solving for $\Delta y$:
\begin{align}
y - \Delta y &= \frac{xy}{x + \Delta x} \\
\Delta y &= y - \frac{xy}{x + \Delta x} = \frac{y(x + \Delta x) - xy}{x + \Delta x} = \frac{y \cdot \Delta x}{x + \Delta x}
\end{align}
\end{proof}

\subsection{Liquidity Provision}

\begin{definition}[Liquidity Tokens]
Liquidity providers receive tokens representing their proportional share of the pool. For a pool with total liquidity $L$ and reserves $(x, y)$, a deposit of $(\Delta x, \Delta y)$ mints:
\begin{equation}
\Delta L = L \cdot \min\left(\frac{\Delta x}{x}, \frac{\Delta y}{y}\right)
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Proportional Deposit]
For optimal capital efficiency, deposits should satisfy:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Delta x}{\Delta y} = \frac{x}{y}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $\frac{\Delta x}{x} \neq \frac{\Delta y}{y}$, then $\min\left(\frac{\Delta x}{x}, \frac{\Delta y}{y}\right) < \max\left(\frac{\Delta x}{x}, \frac{\Delta y}{y}\right)$, and the excess of one asset is returned unused. Maximum liquidity token minting occurs when ratios are equal.
\end{proof}

\section{Impermanent Loss}

\begin{definition}[Impermanent Loss]
Let $(x_0, y_0)$ be initial reserves with price $P_0 = y_0/x_0$. After price changes to $P_1$, the value of LP position relative to holding is:
\begin{equation}
\text{IL} = \frac{V_{\text{LP}}}{V_{\text{HODL}}} - 1
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Impermanent Loss Formula]
For price ratio $r = P_1/P_0$, impermanent loss is:
\begin{equation}
\text{IL} = \frac{2\sqrt{r}}{1 + r} - 1
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Initial position: reserves $(x_0, y_0)$ with $k = x_0 y_0$.

At price $P_1 = r \cdot P_0$, the new reserves $(x_1, y_1)$ satisfy:
\begin{align}
x_1 y_1 &= k \\
\frac{y_1}{x_1} &= r \cdot \frac{y_0}{x_0}
\end{align}

Solving: $x_1 = x_0/\sqrt{r}$ and $y_1 = y_0\sqrt{r}$.

LP value at price $P_1$ (in units of asset $B$):
\begin{equation}
V_{\text{LP}} = x_1 \cdot P_1 + y_1 = \frac{x_0}{\sqrt{r}} \cdot r \cdot P_0 + y_0\sqrt{r} = 2y_0\sqrt{r}
\end{equation}

HODL value:
\begin{equation}
V_{\text{HODL}} = x_0 \cdot P_1 + y_0 = x_0 \cdot r \cdot P_0 + y_0 = y_0(r + 1)
\end{equation}

Therefore:
\begin{equation}
\text{IL} = \frac{2y_0\sqrt{r}}{y_0(1 + r)} - 1 = \frac{2\sqrt{r}}{1 + r} - 1
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

\section{Fee Mechanism}

\subsection{Swap Fees}

Trading fees are implemented by reducing the effective input:
\begin{equation}
\Delta y = \frac{y \cdot \Delta x \cdot (1 - \gamma)}{x + \Delta x \cdot (1 - \gamma)}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma$ is the fee rate (typically 0.3\%).

\begin{proposition}[Fee Accrual]
Fees accrue to liquidity providers by increasing $k$:
\begin{equation}
k' = (x + \Delta x)(y - \Delta y) > k
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Optimal Fee Selection}

\begin{theorem}[Fee-Volume Tradeoff]
Higher fees reduce volume but increase revenue per trade. The optimal fee $\gamma^*$ maximizes:
\begin{equation}
R(\gamma) = \gamma \cdot V(\gamma)
\end{equation}
where $V(\gamma)$ is the volume function decreasing in $\gamma$.
\end{theorem}

For constant elasticity demand $V(\gamma) = V_0 \cdot \gamma^{-\epsilon}$:
\begin{equation}
\gamma^* = \frac{1}{\epsilon - 1} \quad \text{for } \epsilon > 1
\end{equation}

\section{Implementation on Lux}

\subsection{Gas Optimization}

The Lux blockchain's high throughput enables efficient AMM operations. Key optimizations include:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Batched Updates}: Multiple swaps processed in single transaction
\item \textbf{Lazy Evaluation}: Fee distribution computed on withdrawal
\item \textbf{Integer Arithmetic}: Fixed-point representation avoids floating-point
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Core Swap Algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Constant Product Swap}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Input amount $\Delta x_{\text{in}}$, reserves $(x, y)$, fee $\gamma$
\Ensure Output amount $\Delta y_{\text{out}}$, new reserves $(x', y')$
\State $\Delta x_{\text{fee}} \gets \Delta x_{\text{in}} \cdot \gamma$
\State $\Delta x \gets \Delta x_{\text{in}} - \Delta x_{\text{fee}}$
\State $\Delta y_{\text{out}} \gets \frac{y \cdot \Delta x}{x + \Delta x}$
\State $x' \gets x + \Delta x_{\text{in}}$
\State $y' \gets y - \Delta y_{\text{out}}$
\State \Return $\Delta y_{\text{out}}, (x', y')$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Security Considerations}

\subsection{Flash Loan Attacks}

AMMs are vulnerable to price manipulation via flash loans. Mitigations include:
\begin{itemize}
\item Time-weighted average prices (TWAP)
\item Delayed price oracles
\item Per-block trade limits
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Front-Running Protection}

MEV extraction through front-running can be mitigated by:
\begin{itemize}
\item Commit-reveal schemes
\item Private transaction pools
\item Slippage tolerance bounds
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

Constant product AMMs provide a mathematically elegant solution to decentralized exchange. The invariant $xy = k$ ensures continuous liquidity while maintaining simple, verifiable behavior. Our analysis quantifies impermanent loss, fee accrual, and price impact, providing the foundation for rational liquidity provision decisions.

The Lux blockchain's high throughput and low latency make it well-suited for AMM deployment, enabling efficient price discovery and capital-efficient liquidity provision.
