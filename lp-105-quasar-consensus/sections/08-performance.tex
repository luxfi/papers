\section{Performance}

\label{sec:performance}
%% ============================================================================

All measurements were taken on commodity hardware (Apple M1 Max, 10 cores,
64\,GB RAM) using Go~1.24 with \texttt{luxfi/crypto} packages. Each
benchmark ran for $\geq 10^4$ iterations with \texttt{testing.B} harness.

\subsection{Full PQ Certificate Generation}

The end-to-end time to produce a complete Quasar certificate (parallel
execution of all three signing paths, followed by aggregation and
verification) is $18.8\,\text{ms}$ for $n = 5$ validators, dominated by
Corona DKG amortization. The finality \emph{check} (verifying an existing
Quasar certificate) takes $18.8\,\text{ns}$ after the BLS aggregate public
key is precomputed---a single pairing check plus cached Corona and ML-DSA
verification results.

\subsection{Memory Bounds}

Epoch-based pruning with a 6-epoch retention window bounds consensus state to
$5.9\,\text{MB}$ after $10^6$ finality events. This is verified by the
epoch manager's garbage collection, which runs at each epoch boundary and
reclaims all state older than 6 epochs. The bound holds because each epoch
stores at most $E$ block certificates and $n$ validator states, and
$6 \cdot E \cdot (|\mathcal{C}| + n \cdot |\mathit{state}|)$ is constant
for fixed parameters.
