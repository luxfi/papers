\section{Namespace 1 -- TypeKind byte}
\label{sec:typekind}

The TypeKind byte (also called the ``schema ID'') is byte 0 of every
ZAP envelope on the wire. It identifies the top-level message type
and selects the per-TypeKind ShapeKind table (\S\ref{sec:shapekind}).
LP-022 defines the framing; LP-200 layers the discriminator on top;
LP-300 owns the allocations.

The 256-byte space is partitioned into six blocks by allocator-LP.
Reserved ranges in between are held for future expansion and MUST
NOT be allocated except by an LP-300 amendment.

\subsection{Consensus-wire block (LP-182, \texttt{0x01--0x0F})}

QuasarCerts, blocks, witness proofs, and the per-leg signatures

\subsection{Broad protocol reserve (\texttt{0x10--0xBF})}

The largest contiguous reserve in the TypeKind space -- 176 bytes --
held for future protocol-level allocations. No LP owns this range
today. Likely landing zones, none committed, are future chain-internal
schemas analogous to LP-182, future P2P transport primitives analogous
to LP-201, and future signed-tx envelope variants. Any consuming LP
that wants to land here MUST open an LP-300 amendment first.

\subsection{Mempool / 2PC / rollup block (\texttt{0xE0--0xEF})}

The most-contested block in the corpus. Three LPs originally claimed
overlapping bytes here; LP-300 resolves the contention per the
LP-214 canonical handoff (\S\ref{sec:conflicts}). The final
allocation: LP-208 takes \texttt{0xE0..0xE1}, LP-211 takes
\texttt{0xE2..0xE7}, LP-218 takes \texttt{0xE8..0xEF} (shifted from
prior \texttt{0xE0..0xE3} claim). LP-219 reuses LP-211's
\texttt{0xE2..0xE7} at the rollup tier as a ShapeKind-level
extension (TxKind \texttt{0x44}), not a TypeKind-level reclamation.
