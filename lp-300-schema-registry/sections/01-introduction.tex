\section{Introduction}

LP-300 is a meta-registry. It allocates no new wire bytes, no new
precompile slots, no new schemas. Its sole job is to record -- in
one normative place -- every allocation made by every other LP in
the corpus. Where a consuming LP says ``this draft uses TypeKind
\texttt{0xE8}'', LP-300 says ``\texttt{0xE8} is owned by LP-218;
here is the row.''

\subsection{Why a master registry}

Three years of LP authoring have accumulated 25 LPs that touch one
of three address spaces -- the ZAP TypeKind byte, the per-TypeKind
ShapeKind byte, or an EVM precompile slot. The allocations live in
the ``Wire schemas'' section, the ``Precompile slot'' section, the
``Schema-ID allocation map'', or scattered tables in the LP body.
Three concrete failure modes follow:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item \textbf{Allocation drift.} LP-208 originally allocated
  \texttt{0xF0..0xFF} for DAG mempool. LP-214 then allocated
  \texttt{0xF0..0xF2} for light client. The conflict went undetected
  until LP-218 also targeted the same range. LP-208 quietly
  re-homed to \texttt{0xE0..0xE1} -- but the original Draft text in
  several places still cites \texttt{0xF0}.
\item \textbf{Slot triple-claim.} LP-208 (\texttt{0xE0..0xE1}),
  LP-211 (\texttt{0xE2..0xE7}), and an earlier LP-218 Draft
  (\texttt{0xE0..0xEF}) all claimed bytes inside \texttt{0xE0..0xEF}.
  Three Drafts cannot all be Final without arbitration.
\item \textbf{Wrapper-over-primitive ambiguity.} An early LP-218 Draft
  targeted the Plonky3-fork STARK at \texttt{0x012205}; the canonical
  allocation is \texttt{0x012205} = the P3Q signature-dispatch wrapper
  (LP-219), sharing the slot with its raw-primitive owner by kind byte,
  while the STARK relocated to its own slot \texttt{0x012220} (starkfri,
  LP-221). The wrapper-vs-primitive distinction is real but invisible
  without a registry that records both.
\end{enumerate}

The fix is decomplecting: separate \emph{authoritative allocation}
(LP-300) from \emph{wire behavior specification} (the consuming LP).
A consuming LP becomes informative-only on the allocation question.
LP-300 wins any disagreement.

\subsection{Three orthogonal namespaces}

LP-300 partitions every allocation into three orthogonal namespaces
plus one out-of-scope namespace. Cross-namespace collisions are
impossible by construction because each lives in a different address
space:

\subsection{What this paper is not}

LP-300 is a reference document. It contains no measured benches:
the registry ships no wire change, so there is nothing to measure.
It contains no engineering tradeoffs: the tradeoffs were made by
the consuming LPs and are documented there. It contains no
operator-facing config: nothing in LP-300 is configurable at
runtime.

What LP-300 \emph{does} contain: every byte allocated, every slot
allocated, the owner LP for each row, the conflict resolution
log for the four pre-existing conflicts, the cooldown policy for
deprecated allocations, and the cross-referencing pattern every
consuming LP MUST follow.

\subsection{Final from genesis}

LP-300 activates at the genesis of the new final Lux network --
unix \texttt{1766708400} = 2025-12-25T16:20:00-08:00 -- with no
migration window. A meta-registry that ships no wire change has
nothing to migrate; it is Final the moment the network is. Any
post-activation amendment is an additive new row, never a
re-allocation of a live slot. Re-allocation of a deprecated slot
requires the one-year cooldown documented in
\S\ref{sec:cooldown}.
