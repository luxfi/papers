\section{Regulated Access}
\label{sec:regulated}

Certain data---personal health information, financial records, legal
documents---is subject to regulatory requirements that mandate specific
access controls. Threshold cryptography enables compliance without
centralized key escrow.

\subsection{Compliance-Gated Reveal}

\begin{definition}[Compliance Gate]
A compliance gate $\mathcal{C} = (\pi, t_c, \{C_1, \ldots, C_{n_c}\})$
requires that a data reveal operation satisfies policy $\pi$ (evaluated
via TFHE on the T-Chain) and is co-signed by $t_c$ of $n_c$ compliance
officers. The reveal is a threshold decryption operation:
\begin{equation}
    \mathsf{Reveal}(d) = \begin{cases}
    \mathsf{ThreshDec}(t + t_c, \{s_i\}_{i \in S \cup C}) & \text{if } \mathsf{Eval}(\pi) = 1 \\
    \perp & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $S$ is the set of user-authorized shares and $C$ is the set of
compliance officer shares.
\end{definition}

The UX presents this as: ``Viewing this record requires compliance
approval. A compliance officer has been notified.'' The compliance
officer sees: ``[User] requests access to [record type]. Reason:
[stated reason]. [Approve] [Deny].'' No cryptographic ceremony is
visible to either party.

\subsection{Audit Trail}

Every compliance-gated reveal produces an on-chain receipt recording:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Who requested the reveal (user DID)
    \item Who approved (compliance officer DIDs)
    \item When the reveal occurred (block timestamp)
    \item What was revealed (hash of the decrypted data, not the data itself)
    \item Why (the policy $\pi$ that was satisfied)
\end{itemize}

This provides a tamper-evident audit trail that satisfies regulatory
requirements (HIPAA access logs, GDPR data access records, SOC~2 access
controls) without requiring a centralized audit service.
