\section{Architectural Decision: Single Slot, Kind-Byte Dispatch}
\label{sec:architectural-decision}

\subsection{The prior sibling-slots model}

The pre-decomplection draft of LP-220 (dated 2026-05-29)
allocated three EVM precompile slots:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item \texttt{0x012205} -- P3Q-Pulsar, wrapping the rollup-batch
  ABI around the Pulsar threshold-FIPS-204 verifier core.
\item \texttt{0x012206} -- P3Q-Corona, wrapping the rollup-batch
  ABI around the Corona (Corona) Module-LWE threshold verifier
  core.
\item \texttt{0x012207} -- P3Q-Magnetar, wrapping the rollup-
  batch ABI around the Magnetar threshold-SLH-DSA verifier core.
\end{itemize}

Each slot reused the corresponding LP-0120 raw-primitive verifier
as the underlying implementation (\texttt{0x012204} raw Pulsar,
\texttt{0x012206} raw Corona, \texttt{0x012207} raw Magnetar) and
layered a rollup-batch-specific wrapper on top: structural
parsing for the \texttt{(prev\_root, new\_root, batch\_hash)}
tuple, a separate FIPS context string per slot, separate gas
calibration billed inside each wrapper.

The collision between LP-220's rollup-batch wrappers and
LP-0120's raw-primitive verifiers at slots \texttt{0x012206} and
\texttt{0x012207} was the first symptom that the design was
overcomplecting two concerns:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item ``Verify a threshold signature of family $F$ over a 32-byte
  digest'' (the raw primitive concern, owned by LP-0120).
\item ``Verify a rollup-batch envelope of family $F$ where the
  digest is $H(\text{prev\_root} \,\|\, \text{new\_root} \,\|\,
  \text{batch\_hash})$ and the FIPS context binds the rollup
  pattern'' (the rollup-batch concern, owned by LP-218 /
  LP-220).
\end{enumerate}

These are two distinct concerns. They deserve two distinct
surfaces. But they do not deserve six precompile slots between
them.

\subsection{The 2026-06-03 unification}

The decomplection allocates the rollup-batch concern exactly one
slot -- \texttt{0x012205} -- and reuses the existing raw-primitive
slots (\texttt{0x012204}, \texttt{0x012206}, \texttt{0x012207})
for the raw-primitive concern. The rollup-batch slot dispatches
on a leading \texttt{kind} byte to per-primitive verifier
implementations:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Slot} & \textbf{Concern} & \textbf{Wire} & \textbf{Owner} \\
\midrule
\texttt{0x012202} & Raw ML-DSA single-party & FIPS-204 & LP-0120 \\
\texttt{0x012203} & Raw SLH-DSA single-party & FIPS-205 & LP-0120 \\
\texttt{0x012204} & Raw Pulsar (Module-LWE threshold) & FIPS-204 ctx + sig & LP-0120 \\
\textbf{\texttt{0x012205}} & \textbf{P3Q family} (rollup-batch ABI) & \textbf{kind-byte dispatch} & \textbf{LP-218 + LP-220} \\
\texttt{0x012206} & Raw Corona (Module-LWE threshold) & Corona ctx + sig & LP-0120 \\
\texttt{0x012207} & Raw Magnetar (SLH-DSA threshold) & FIPS-205 ctx + sig & LP-0120 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Precompile slot ownership after the 2026-06-03
decomplection. Slots \texttt{0x012204}, \texttt{0x012206},
\texttt{0x012207} remain owned by LP-0120 for the raw-primitive
non-rollup-batch use case; LP-218 + LP-220 own \texttt{0x012205}
as the P3Q rollup-batch family.}
\label{tab:slot-ownership}
\end{table}

A Solidity contract that needs Corona-kind verify for a
rollup-batch envelope calls
\texttt{P3Q.verify(0x02, sig, h, pk)} at \texttt{0x012205}. A
Solidity contract that needs raw Corona primitive verify -- a
non-rollup-batch use case, e.g.\ a custody attestation contract
verifying an off-chain Corona-signed certificate -- calls the
LP-0120 raw-primitive verifier at \texttt{0x012206} directly. Two
distinct callers, two distinct surfaces, two distinct slots,
zero overlap.

The P3Q family ABI is the single canonical entry point for
rollup-batch verification across all PQ kinds. This honors
LP-200~\S~``One and only one way to do everything''~\cite{lp200}.

\subsection{Kind-byte dispatch}

The unified \texttt{0x012205} precompile reads a 1-byte
\texttt{kind} discriminator at offset~0 of calldata and routes
to one of three (today) registered verifier implementations. The
dispatch is at runtime, populated by package
\texttt{init()} functions; the precompile entry-point is one
function in one file. The full dispatch logic from the reference
implementation at
\texttt{lux/precompile/p3q/contract.go::Run}:

\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]
kind := input[0]
switch kind {
case KindPulsar:     // 0x01
    // fall through to FIPS 204 ML-DSA verify path
case KindCorona,     // 0x02
     KindMagnetar:   // 0x03
    // Reserved kinds -- verifier not yet wired. Return abiFalse
    // (the LP-218 "no revert on cryptographic failure" contract)
    // so callers still see a consistent surface; once the
    // lattice/hash threshold libraries land, this branch routes
    // to its own verifier.
    return abiFalse(), remaining, nil
default:
    // Unknown kind -- abiFalse, same as any malformed input.
    return abiFalse(), remaining, nil
}
\end{lstlisting}

Three properties hold by construction:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item \textbf{Constant-time over the kind byte.} The switch is on
  a PUBLIC field (the calldata kind byte is observable on-chain).
  Branching on it does not constitute a timing side channel
  against any secret.
\item \textbf{No revert on cryptographic failure.} An unknown
  kind, an unwired reserved kind, a malformed sig, a FIPS-204
  reject -- all return \texttt{abiFalse} (a 32-byte
  zero-encoded EVM \texttt{bool}) with no Go error. The Solidity
  caller observes \texttt{false} via the return word and
  decides whether to revert. This matches LP-218~\S~``Failure
  modes.''
\item \textbf{Uniform gas billing.} The \texttt{RequiredGas}
  function is monotonic in input length and does NOT depend on
  the kind. A short malformed input pays the base gas, a
  well-formed N-byte input pays \texttt{base + N * PerByteGas}.
  No reduced price for early rejection -- uniform billing
  prevents a gas-side channel from leaking which validation
  stage rejected the input.
\end{itemize}

Per-kind gas \emph{rates} differ (Pulsar \SI{50000}{gas}, Corona
\SI{5000000}{gas}, Magnetar \SI{30000}{gas}); the resolution
happens inside the per-kind \texttt{P3QVerifier.GasCost()} method
described in section~\ref{sec:verifier-interface}. The
\texttt{0x012205} precompile multiplexes those rates over a
single observable address.

\subsection{What this is not}

It is worth being explicit about two design decisions LP-220
does \emph{not} make:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item \textbf{It is not a generic PQ-signature precompile.} The
  three kinds are not interchangeable. A Pulsar signature
  submitted under \texttt{kind=0x02} (Corona) will fail
  structurally -- Pulsar sigs are \SI{3309}{B}, Corona sigs are
  $\sim\SI{33}{KB}$, and the verifier core for kind $0x02$ is the
  Corona Module-LWE verifier (which does not parse FIPS-204
  output). The kind byte selects a specific verifier; it does
  not abstract over them.
\item \textbf{It is not a backwards-compatible upgrade of the
  sibling-slots draft.} There is no LP-220-v1 deployment in the
  wild; the activation marker
  \texttt{2025-12-25T16:20:00-08:00} places the unified-slot
  ABI at network genesis. Existing test corpora that target
  \texttt{0x012206} / \texttt{0x012207} for rollup-batch wrappers
  are obsolete (those slots are now LP-0120 raw-primitive
  surfaces with different wire format); they must be re-targeted
  to \texttt{0x012205} with the appropriate kind byte. No
  compatibility shim exists.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Activation marker}

\begin{verbatim}
activates:      2025-12-25T16:20:00-08:00
activates-unix: 1766708400
\end{verbatim}

The P3Q family ABI at slot \texttt{0x012205} with kinds $0x01$,
$0x02$, $0x03$ registered in the dispatch table is callable from
the genesis block of the new final Lux network. Rollup
deployments that target PQ-strict or PQ-heavy mode call
\texttt{P3Q.verify(kind, ...)} from height~0 with the appropriate
kind byte (subject to per-kind verifier wiring, see
LP-220--LP-222).
