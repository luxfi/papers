\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem}
% ============================================================

\begin{definition}[Bridge Capital Inefficiency]
Let $L$ denote the total value locked across all bridge protocols, and let $r$ denote the risk-adjusted opportunity cost of capital (the yield those assets would earn if not locked). The annual capital inefficiency is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inefficiency}
  \mathcal{I} = L \cdot r
\end{equation}
For $L = \$15\text{B}$ and $r = 4.5\%$ (weighted average of ETH staking and stablecoin lending yields), $\mathcal{I} = \$675\text{M/year}$.
\end{definition}

This inefficiency creates three downstream problems:

\paragraph{Higher bridge fees.} Without yield income, bridge operators must charge higher fees to cover infrastructure costs, increasing friction for cross-chain transfers.

\paragraph{Inferior bridge tokens.} Wrapped ETH (WETH) on a destination chain earns 0\% APY. Native stETH on Ethereum earns 3.5\%. A user who bridges ETH pays an implicit cost equal to the forgone staking yield for the duration of the bridge period.

\paragraph{Liquidity fragmentation.} Rational capital avoids bridges because the opportunity cost exceeds the utility of cross-chain deployment. This reduces liquidity on destination chains, widening spreads and increasing slippage.

\begin{remark}
The capital inefficiency is not merely theoretical. As of Q1 2025, approximately \$3.8B of the \$15B in bridge TVL is ETH that could be earning 3.5\% in Lido~\cite{lidofinance}, \$2.1B is stablecoins that could earn 5--8\% in Aave~\cite{aavev3}, and \$1.4B is BTC that could earn 3--5\% through Babylon~\cite{babylon2024}. The remainder consists of altcoins with varying yield opportunities.
\end{remark}
