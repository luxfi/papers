\subsection{Capital Efficiency Comparison}

We compare three modes of holding ETH across a bridge:

\begin{definition}[Capital Efficiency Ratio]
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capital_efficiency}
  \eta = \frac{\text{APY}_{\text{bridge token}}}{\text{APY}_{\text{native}}}
\end{equation}
where $\text{APY}_{\text{native}}$ is the yield available on the source chain without bridging.
\end{definition}

\subsection{The xLUX Flywheel}\label{sec:flywheel}

On Lux Network, the stakeholder vault for the Teleport OmnichainRouter is LiquidLUX (xLUX). All protocol fees flow to xLUX holders:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:xlux_yield}
  r_{\text{xLUX}} = \frac{\sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}} f_p \cdot V_p}{\text{totalStaked}_{\text{LUX}}}
\end{equation}

where $\mathcal{P} = \{\text{bridge, DEX, lending, perps, NFT AMM}\}$ is the set of fee-generating protocols, $f_p$ is the fee rate for protocol $p$, and $V_p$ is the volume processed by protocol $p$.

\begin{theorem}[Flywheel Convergence]\label{thm:flywheel}
Under the assumption that total protocol volume $V = \sum_p V_p$ is an increasing function of total liquidity $\mathcal{L}$, and liquidity is an increasing function of xLUX APY $r_{\text{xLUX}}$, the system converges to a stable equilibrium. Formally, if:
\begin{align}
  V &= g(\mathcal{L}), \quad g' > 0, \quad g'' < 0 \label{eq:fly1} \\
  \mathcal{L} &= h(r_{\text{xLUX}}), \quad h' > 0, \quad h'' < 0 \label{eq:fly2} \\
  r_{\text{xLUX}} &= \frac{f \cdot V}{\text{totalStaked}} \label{eq:fly3}
\end{align}
where $g$ and $h$ are concave increasing functions, then there exists a unique fixed point $(V^*, \mathcal{L}^*, r^*)$ satisfying all three equations.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Define $\Phi: \mathbb{R}_{>0} \to \mathbb{R}_{>0}$ by $\Phi(r) = f \cdot g(h(r)) / \text{totalStaked}$. Since $g$ and $h$ are concave and increasing, $\Phi$ is concave and increasing. For sufficiently small $r$, $\Phi(r) > r$ (the flywheel generates more yield than required). For large $r$, the concavity of $g \circ h$ ensures $\Phi(r) < r$ (diminishing returns on volume growth). By the intermediate value theorem, there exists a unique $r^*$ with $\Phi(r^*) = r^*$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Yield vs.\ Risk Frontier}

The portfolio of yield strategies traces an efficient frontier in risk-return space. Let $\bm{r} = (r_1, \ldots, r_m)$ be the vector of strategy APYs and $\bm{\Sigma}$ be the covariance matrix of strategy returns. The mean-variance optimal allocation is:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:frontier}
  \max_{\bm{w}} \bm{w}^T \bm{r} - \frac{\lambda}{2} \bm{w}^T \bm{\Sigma} \bm{w}
  \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \bm{w} \in \mathcal{W}
\end{equation}

where $\lambda$ is the risk aversion parameter and $\mathcal{W}$ is the feasible set defined by the tier constraints~(\ref{eq:allocation}) and the single-strategy cap.

\begin{remark}
The governor (DAO) sets $\lambda$ based on the chain's risk appetite. A conservative chain (e.g., institutional-focused) uses high $\lambda$, concentrating in Tier 1. An aggressive chain uses low $\lambda$, allocating more to Tier 2/3 for higher yield.
\end{remark}
