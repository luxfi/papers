\section{Algorithm}
\label{sec:algorithm}

\subsection{Round structure}

Rounds in the DAG advance at a fixed cadence -- every header
references $K$ parents from the prior round (LP-208 round
structure). Waves are anchored at even rounds ($2, 4, 6, \ldots$);
leader election runs every $2$ rounds.

\subsection{Leader election}

At even round $2k$, the leader is selected via VRF over the round
digest:

\begin{equation}
\textrm{leader}(2k) = \arg\max_{v \in \textrm{validators}}
\textrm{VRF}(v.\textrm{staking\_key}, \textrm{round\_digest}(2k))
\end{equation}

where
$\textrm{round\_digest}(r) = \textrm{sha256}(\textrm{chain\_id} \| r \| k_{\textrm{prev\_round\_root}})$.

The VRF is BLS-based (LP-075 BLS leg) for the classical case; under
PQ-heavy the VRF falls back to a Pulsar threshold-derived
randomness beacon (LP-176). Round-robin is the deterministic
fallback when no validator publishes a VRF proof within the round
timeout: $\textrm{leader}(2k) = \textrm{validators}[(2k/2) \bmod N]$.

\subsection{Wave anchoring}

At the end of round $2k{+}1$, every validator examines the DAG and
decides: \emph{did the leader's round-$2k$ vertex receive
$\geq 2f{+}1$ references in the round-$2k{+}1$ layer?}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item \textbf{Yes} $\to$ the leader vertex is the wave anchor. The
  wave commits.
\item \textbf{No} $\to$ the wave skips. The leader of round $2k{+}2$
  anchors a larger wave covering rounds $2k..2k{+}2$.
\end{itemize}

The $\geq 2f{+}1$ references condition is the Mysticeti fast-path
quorum under $<33\%$ byzantine. Under $<50\%$ byzantine (recovery
path), 3-RTT vote counting on round $2k{+}2$ votes for round-$2k{+}1$
votes for round-$2k$ anchors the wave at the cost of one extra
round.

\subsection{Causal commit set}

Once the anchor is decided, the wave's commit set is the causal
closure of the anchor in the DAG:

\begin{equation}
W(\textrm{anchor}) = \bigl\{ v \in \textrm{DAG} : v \in
\textrm{causalAncestors}(\textrm{anchor}) \bigr\}
\cup \{\textrm{anchor}\}
\setminus \bigcup_{j<k} W(\textrm{prior\_anchor}_j)
\end{equation}

Subtract prior anchors' commit sets -- a vertex is committed
exactly once, by the earliest wave whose anchor causally dominates
it.

\subsection{Total order within wave}

The wave's vertices are totally ordered by:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
\item \textbf{Round number ascending} (vertices in lower rounds
  order first).
\item Within a round, \textbf{lexicographic on
  \texttt{(NodeID, headerHash)} ascending}.
\end{enumerate}

This is the canonical sort. Every validator independently computes
the same order from the same DAG -- no coordination needed. The
order is deterministic and content-addressable.

\subsection{Cert ceremony}

Once the wave is ordered, the LP-217 cert ceremony runs on the
wave's ordered byte payload (concatenation of header bytes in
order). One QuasarCert per wave, signed by $\geq 2f{+}1$ validators
per LP-182. The cert's leg composition is per the chain's
configured \texttt{quasar.cert\_mode} (LP-217); the cert's
\texttt{blockID} field is $\textrm{sha256}(\textrm{wave\_ordered\_bytes})$.

\subsection{Stack composition}

\begin{verbatim}
LP-208 DAG mempool produces headers (continuous)
    |
    v
LP-209 wave commit (every 2 rounds; leader-anchored)
    |
    v
LP-210 Block-STM execution (parallel; per-tx MVCC)
    |
    v
LP-217 cert ceremony (one cert per wave)
    |
    v
ZapDB MVCC commit (LP-202 atomic unwind)
\end{verbatim}

LP-209 never re-orders within a header -- the tx order inside one
header is the header author's choice, sealed at LP-208 publish
time. LP-209 only orders \emph{across} headers, by the rule above.
