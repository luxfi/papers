\section{Empirical Evaluation}

\label{sec:field-evaluation}

\subsection{Multi-Valued Consensus Performance}

We evaluated Field on a 500-node testnet with $d \in \{2, 4, 8, 16\}$-valued conflicts.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrr@{}}
\toprule
$d$ & \textbf{Median Rounds} & \textbf{Correctness} & \textbf{TPS} \\
\midrule
2 & 8 & 100\% & 2,750 \\
4 & 10 & 100\% & 2,600 \\
8 & 12 & 100\% & 2,400 \\
16 & 15 & 99.99\% & 2,100 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Field protocol performance vs. conflict cardinality $d$}
\label{tab:field-multival}
\end{table}

The convergence time grows sub-linearly in $d$, consistent with the $O(\log d)$ factor
predicted by Theorem~\ref{thm:field-phase}. All experiments produced the correct outcome
(highest initial support wins) with $> 99.99\%$ reliability.

\subsection{Comparison with Binary Protocol}

For $d = 4$, a binary decomposition requires $\log_2 4 = 2$ sequential binary consensus
instances, each taking $\sim 8$ rounds, totaling $\sim 16$ rounds. The Field protocol
achieves the same result in 10 rounds, a 37.5\% improvement.
