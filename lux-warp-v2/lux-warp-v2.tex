\begin{abstract}
\noindent We present Lux, a hybrid post-quantum consensus architecture for DAG-native chains. Lux separates fast operational finality from durable post-quantum finality. Validators emit Photons over a Nebula DAG substrate; classical BLS aggregates form Beams for low-latency confirmation, while ML-DSA attestations and Pulsar lattice-threshold Pulses provide post-quantum finality over Nebula roots. Prism binds all certificate lanes to the same transcript, and Quasar reaches Horizon finality when the bound lanes verify.

\textbf{This paper.} Warp 2.0 is the cross-chain envelope that carries source-chain Horizon finality across chain boundaries. Warp 1.x \cite{lp075} signs cross-chain messages with a BLS aggregate over the source chain's validator-key registry; that classical-only design gives a destination chain no PQ guarantee about the source's state. Warp 2.0 adds a Pulsar Pulse over a canonical transcript binding the source chain's $\mathsf{NebulaRoot}$, $\mathsf{KeyEraID}$, and $\mathsf{Generation}$. The destination verifier runs Prism over the source's transcript using the source-chain key registry. Beam, ML-DSA, and Pulse all lift their soundness arguments from the consensus side (Quasar Horizon \cite{luxquasarhorizon,lp020}) to the cross-chain side via transcript binding, with one explicit theorem (Warp 2.0 envelope unforgeability, \texttt{proofs/quasar/warp-pq-soundness.tex}) covering the cross-chain case. We document the wire format (the canonical ZAP envelope encoding), the v1 $\to$ v2 migration discipline (forward-only; legacy RLP verifiers reject the ZAP magic), the L2 transitive inheritance, and the constant-time fuzz harness that surfaced one vulnerability (a lattigo $\mathsf{ReadUint64Slice}$ DoS) which is fixed in production. Warp Private (FHE-encrypted payload, optionally Pulse-signed) is orthogonal and discussed only briefly here.
\end{abstract}
