\begin{abstract}
The Lux \emph{trustless-by-default} law requires every consensus threshold
signature lane to be \emph{no-reconstruct}: no party or coordinator ever forms
the full secret key, even transiently at signing time. For the lattice lanes
(Pulsar / ML-DSA, Corona / Ringtail) this is achieved by exploiting the algebraic
linearity of the signature response. The hash-based lane, Magnetar
(threshold SLH-DSA, FIPS~205), has no such structure: the secret seed controls a
forest of one-time (WOTS$^+$) and few-time (FORS) secrets bound to the public key
\emph{only} through the non-homomorphic hash $\mathsf{SHAKE}$. This paper is a
rigorous feasibility study of \emph{no-reconstruct} threshold SLH-DSA.

We establish the central structural fact that makes the problem tractable
\emph{in part}: FIPS~205 verification never recomputes a pseudorandom function of
the seed; it only checks that the opened one-time material hashes consistently to
the single published hypertree root. Leaf secrets therefore need not be
pseudorandom images of a master seed; they need only be Merkle-consistent with the
root. We rank four approaches (distributed tree generation, threshold leaf
opening, MPC over the hash chain, and ZK-consistent partial openings) and identify
the two hard walls: (i)~the SLH-DSA hypertree has $2^{63}\text{--}2^{68}$ leaves
and cannot be materialised; (ii)~deriving a leaf from the seed is a keyed hash and
admits no algebraic threshold shortcut (the Cozzo--Smart obstruction).

We then build and prove the one concretely realisable, stock-FIPS-205-verifiable,
genuinely no-reconstruct sub-protocol: \emph{distributed FORS-leaf threshold
opening}. Leaf secrets are shared directly (no master seed exists); the combiner
interpolates only the message-selected one-time leaves, each exactly one leaf
wide; the assembled FORS signature is byte-identical to centralised FIPS~205
\textsf{forsSign} and is accepted by the unmodified FIPS~205 Algorithm~17 verifier.
We isolate the precise residual obstruction: the crux is not the no-reconstruct
property of a single opening but \emph{Byzantine-safe distributed one-time/few-time
state}---the guarantee that no WOTS$^+$ keypair is ever opened twice and no FORS
few-time budget is exceeded across a live, adversarially scheduled committee. We
prove this reduces no-reconstruct threshold SLH-DSA to a distributed burn-state
problem and explain why, until that state is proven Byzantine-safe, the full
construction must remain fail-closed and excluded from the \texttt{POLARIS\_MAX}
certificate tier. A reference implementation accompanies the paper; its signing
entry returns an explicit \texttt{ErrNoReconstructUnproven} and a structural gate
proves the opening path is incapable of reconstructing the seed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Lux finalises blocks under a post-quantum certificate whose lanes provide
cryptographic-family diversity: two lattice lanes (Pulsar, an ML-DSA / FIPS-204
threshold; Corona, a Module-LWE / Ringtail-Raccoon threshold) and one hash-based lane,
Magnetar, instantiating threshold SLH-DSA (FIPS~205, SPHINCS$^+$~\cite{fips205,sphincsplus}).
The hash-based lane is the genuine assumption diversifier: a break of the
Module-LWE/Module-SIS problems underlying both lattice lanes does not touch a
construction whose only assumption is the (second-)preimage and pseudorandomness
of a hash function.

The governing design rule is the \emph{trustless-by-default law}: every consensus
threshold scheme must run with \emph{no trusted dealer} and with
\emph{no-reconstruct signing}---no party or coordinator ever forms the full secret
key or seed, even transiently. A reveal-and-aggregate path in which a combiner
reconstructs the key for one signing call is, at best, a non-strict accelerator; it
is not admissible to the strict trustless tiers.

\paragraph{The two Magnetar tracks.}
Magnetar is split into two tracks that must not be conflated.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Track A --- Magnetar-Quorum (production, trustless today).}
Validators hold \emph{independent} ordinary FIPS-205 keys; each signs the same
finality subject; a post-quantum STARK (P3Q) proves that a weighted quorum of
stock SLH-DSA signatures verified. There is no dealer, no DKG, no shared secret,
and no threshold SLH-DSA at all---the difficulty is side-stepped entirely. This is
the near-term hash-based lane and is out of scope here.

\item \textbf{Track B --- Magnetar-Threshold (research; this paper).} A genuine
$t$-of-$n$ threshold SLH-DSA that emits \emph{one} stock FIPS-205 signature with
no-reconstruct signing. This is an open problem. The contribution of this paper is
a rigorous design, a ranked taxonomy of approaches, one concretely-buildable and
proven sub-protocol, and a precise map of what is solved versus open.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Why threshold SLH-DSA is hard.}
In ML-DSA and BLS the signing response is a (rounded) linear function of the
secret over a ring or group; Shamir/additive shares of the secret yield shares of
the response, and a threshold signature is assembled by linear combination. SLH-DSA
has no such structure. The secret is an $n$-byte seed $\skseed$; every WOTS$^+$
chain base and every FORS leaf secret is $\PRF(\pkseed,\mathrm{ADRS},\skseed) =
\mathsf{SHAKE256}(\pkseed\Vert\mathrm{ADRS}\Vert\skseed)[{:}n]$, and the public key
is a Merkle hash of an astronomically large forest of such secrets. There is no
linear map from shares of $\skseed$ to the published signature.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item A structural observation (Section~\ref{sec:obstruction}) that FIPS-205
verification \emph{never} evaluates a PRF of the seed---it only checks
Merkle/chain consistency to the single published root---so leaf secrets are free
to be jointly-random rather than pseudorandom images of a master seed. This is the
opening that makes \emph{threshold leaf opening} possible.
\item A ranked taxonomy of four approaches and the identification of the two hard
walls---tree-size and keyed-hash leaf derivation---including the Cozzo--Smart cost
bound (Section~\ref{sec:approaches}).
\item A concrete, no-reconstruct, stock-FIPS-205-verifiable sub-protocol:
\emph{distributed FORS-leaf threshold opening} (Section~\ref{sec:fors}), with a
byte-identity theorem and a leaf-width no-reconstruct theorem.
\item An exact analysis of one-time-material handling (Section~\ref{sec:onetime}):
the reduction of no-reconstruct threshold SLH-DSA to a \emph{Byzantine-safe
distributed burn-state} for WOTS$^+$ one-time and FORS few-time discipline, and
the argument that this is the true residual obstruction.
\item The obstruction map (Section~\ref{sec:map}) and the fail-closed admission
discipline that keeps Track B out of \texttt{POLARIS\_MAX} until proven
(Section~\ref{sec:impl}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background: the structure of SLH-DSA}
\label{sec:background}

We recall the parts of FIPS~205~\cite{fips205} needed below, using the SHAKE
parameter family. Fix the per-set parameters: $n$ (hash output bytes), $h$ (total
hypertree height), $d$ (hypertree layers), $h' = h/d$ (XMSS subtree height), $a$
(FORS tree height, $2^a$ leaves per tree), $k$ (number of FORS trees), and
Winternitz $w=16$. The recommended Lux set is SHAKE-192s ($n=24$, $h=63$, $d=7$,
$a=14$, $k=17$).

\paragraph{Secret key and PRF.}
The scheme seed expands deterministically to
$(\skseed, \skprf, \pkseed)$. Every secret leaf is derived by the keyed hash
\begin{equation}
\PRF(\pkseed,\mathrm{ADRS},\skseed) = \mathsf{SHAKE256}(\pkseed \Vert \mathrm{ADRS} \Vert \skseed)[{:}n],
\label{eq:prf}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{ADRS}$ is the address of the leaf in the forest. $\skseed$ is the
only secret input; $\PRF$ is a non-homomorphic hash of it.

\paragraph{WOTS$^+$ (one-time).}
A WOTS$^+$ keypair has $\mathit{len} = 2n + 3$ chains. Chain $i$ has a secret base
$\mathsf{sk}_i = \PRF(\cdots)$ and public end
$\mathsf{pk}_i = \Fhash^{\,w-1}(\mathsf{sk}_i)$, where $\Fhash$ is a public hash.
A signature on message digits $(b_1,\dots)$ publishes
$\chain(\mathsf{sk}_i, 0, b_i) = \Fhash^{\,b_i}(\mathsf{sk}_i)$ per chain. WOTS$^+$
is \emph{strictly one-time}: signing two digit-vectors $b \neq b'$ reveals chains
at two lengths; from the shorter one an adversary forges the other by extending the
public hash---full key recovery.

\paragraph{FORS (few-time).}
FORS has $k$ trees of $2^a$ leaves. Leaf secret $\mathsf{sk}[i][j] = \PRF(\cdots)$;
leaf node $= \Fhash(\mathsf{sk}[i][j])$; tree $i$ Merkle-hashes (via $\Hhash$) to a
root; $\mathsf{pk}_{\mathrm{FORS}} = \Tl(\text{root}_0 \Vert \dots \Vert
\text{root}_{k-1})$. For digest $md$, FORS opens one leaf secret per tree at the
$md$-selected index plus its $a$-node authentication path. FORS is
\emph{few-time}: signing several messages under one FORS keypair degrades security
roughly as $(\text{reuse})^{k}$.

\paragraph{Hypertree and statelessness.}
The hypertree has $d$ layers of XMSS trees ($2^h$ bottom keypairs in total). A
signature picks a bottom keypair $(\mathit{idxTree}, \mathit{idxLeaf})$ determined
by a randomiser $R = \PRFmsg(\skprf, \pkseed, M')$ and digest
$\Hmsg(R, \pkseed, \pkroot, M')$. The huge tree makes keypair collisions negligible
for a single signer over its lifetime, which is what makes SLH-DSA \emph{stateless}
and one-time-safe without explicit state.

\paragraph{Verification (the load-bearing fact).}
FIPS-205 verification (\textsf{slh\_verify}, Algorithm~20) does the following and
\emph{nothing else}: parse $(R, \sigma_{\mathrm{FORS}}, \sigma_{\mathrm{HT}})$;
recompute $md, \mathit{idxTree}, \mathit{idxLeaf}$; reconstruct
$\mathsf{pk}_{\mathrm{FORS}}$ from $\sigma_{\mathrm{FORS}}$ via the public hashes
$\Fhash,\Hhash,\Tl$ (Algorithm~17, \textsf{forsPkFromSig}); reconstruct the
hypertree root from $\mathsf{pk}_{\mathrm{FORS}}$ and the WOTS$^+$ signatures and
authentication paths; accept iff that root equals $\pkroot$. \textbf{Verification
never calls $\PRF$ and never touches $\skseed$.} It checks Merkle/chain consistency
to the single published root.

\section{Threat model, goals, and the three hard constraints}
\label{sec:model}

\paragraph{Adversary.} A static, computationally bounded, post-quantum adversary
$\adv$ corrupting up to $t-1$ of the $n$ committee members; full network control of
message delivery and scheduling; the ability to observe all broadcast and the
internal state of corrupted parties. The hash function is modelled as a (quantum)
random oracle for the FIPS-205 security argument; the threshold layer adds only
information-theoretic Shamir secrecy over $\GF_{257}$.

\paragraph{Security goal.} EUF-CMA of the produced signature under the unmodified
FIPS-205 verifier, plus \emph{key-secrecy}: the joint view of any $t-1$ parties is
independent of the global seed.

\paragraph{The three hard constraints (acceptance gates).}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em,label=\textbf{C\arabic*.}]
\item \textbf{No-reconstruct.} No party or aggregator ever forms the global seed or
full secret key, even transiently.
\item \textbf{Stock verify.} The output verifies under ordinary FIPS-205 Verify,
unmodified.
\item \textbf{One-time safety.} Any revealed WOTS$^+$/FORS one-time material is
burned forever; no reusable secret leaks.
\end{enumerate}

A subtle but decisive reading: \textbf{C1 draws the no-reconstruct line at the
\emph{global seed}, not at individual leaves.} C3 \emph{anticipates} that one-time
leaf material is revealed (``any revealed WOTS$^+$/FORS one-time material\dots'').
Opening a single one-time leaf secret is therefore permitted; assembling the global
seed is forbidden. This distinction is what separates a viable threshold-leaf
construction from a renamed seed reconstruction.

\paragraph{A cautionary baseline.}
The current production-adjacent Magnetar threshold construction (THBS-SE) and even
its ``dealerless'' DKG \emph{reconstruct the master seed transiently}: the combiner
Lagrange-interpolates all $4n$ seed bytes into a buffer (named
\texttt{derivedExpandInput} / \texttt{lagrangeScratch}) and runs a centralised
signer. Its ``strict-atom'' audit gate forbids only the variable \emph{name}
\texttt{seed}, not the reconstruction. That is Option~A (reveal-and-aggregate) and
\emph{fails C1}. Track B must do genuinely better; renaming a buffer is not a
no-reconstruct proof.

\section{The fundamental obstruction}
\label{sec:obstruction}

\subsection{Structural freedom: verification is root-relative}
\label{sec:freedom}

\begin{proposition}[Root-relative verification]
\label{prop:root}
Let $\Sigma$ be any tuple of byte strings of the FIPS-205 signature shape. The
FIPS-205 verifier accepts $\Sigma$ against $(\pkseed,\pkroot)$ for message $M$ iff
the opened one-time values and authentication paths in $\Sigma$ hash, under the
public functions $\Fhash,\Hhash,\Tl$, to a hypertree root equal to $\pkroot$.
Acceptance is independent of whether the opened secrets are images of any seed
under \eqref{eq:prf}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Immediate from the verification algorithm recalled in Section~\ref{sec:background}:
the only check is root equality; $\PRF$ and $\skseed$ never appear.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:root} is the opening. It means a committee may, at keygen, fix
the forest's secret leaves to \emph{jointly-random} values (not PRF images) and
publish a consistent root; signatures that open those leaves will still verify under
the stock verifier. There is no obligation to reproduce \eqref{eq:prf}. This is
exactly what frees \emph{threshold leaf opening} (approach~B) from needing an MPC
evaluation of $\PRF$ at signing time.

\subsection{The two walls}
\label{sec:walls}

Proposition~\ref{prop:root} does not make the \emph{full stateless} scheme free.
Two walls remain.

\paragraph{Wall 1 --- tree size.} The hypertree has $2^h$ bottom keypairs
($2^{63}$ for SHAKE-192s, $2^{64}$ for 256s). A jointly-random forest cannot be
generated, shared, or committed in full: it is exponentially large. The randomiser
$R$ selects a keypair pseudo-uniformly over the whole tree, so a committee that has
materialised only a small sub-forest cannot answer an arbitrary $R$ without changing
the scheme (and thereby breaking stock verification, which recomputes the full
index range from the published $R$).

\paragraph{Wall 2 --- keyed-hash leaf derivation.} If instead the leaves \emph{are}
derived from a compact shared seed via \eqref{eq:prf} (so the tree need not be
materialised), then producing the secret-dependent signature material requires
evaluating $\mathsf{SHAKE256}(\cdots \Vert \skseed)$ on a secret-shared $\skseed$.
$\mathsf{SHAKE}$ is non-homomorphic; there is no algebraic threshold shortcut. The
only generic route is multiparty computation of the Keccak permutation on shared
state.

\begin{remark}[Cozzo--Smart cost]
\label{rem:cs}
Cozzo and Smart~\cite{cozzosmart} analysed the cost of thresholdising each NIST
round-2 signature and concluded that hash-based schemes (then SPHINCS$^+$) require
MPC over the hash function, which is prohibitively expensive. A single SLH-DSA
signature evaluates on the order of $10^{6}\text{--}10^{7}$ hash compressions
(dominated by recomputing WOTS$^+$ public keys for authentication-path siblings);
the Keccak-$f$ permutation has $\approx 1600$ AND gates per round and $24$ rounds.
Evaluating $\sim 10^{7}$ permutations in MPC is $\gtrsim 10^{11}$ AND gates per
signature: feasible in principle, but minutes-to-hours of compute and gigabytes-to-
terabytes of communication. This is a max-assurance ceremony, never a consensus hot
path.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Consequence}
\label{sec:consequence}

\begin{theorem}[Stateless dichotomy]
\label{thm:dichotomy}
A no-reconstruct $t$-of-$n$ producer of a \emph{stock, stateless} FIPS-205 SLH-DSA
signature must, for each signature, either
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.8em]
\item materialise a jointly-random forest covering the $R$-selected keypair
(infeasible by Wall~1 for $h \geq 63$), or
\item evaluate the leaf-deriving PRF and the secret-dependent
authentication-path hashes under MPC on a shared seed (Wall~2; the Cozzo--Smart
cost of Remark~\ref{rem:cs}).
\end{enumerate}
Equivalently, route~(b)---generic MPC over $\mathsf{SHAKE}$---is the only path to a
stock, stateless, no-reconstruct SLH-DSA signature, and it is a max-assurance
ceremony, not a hot-path primitive.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:root} the leaves may be jointly random, but answering an
arbitrary $R$ requires either the full forest (route~a) or on-demand derivation
from a compact secret (route~b). Route~(a) needs $2^h$ shared leaves, contradicting
feasibility for $h \geq 63$. Route~(b) needs $\PRF$ on a shared seed; by Wall~2
this is MPC over $\mathsf{SHAKE}$, whose cost is Remark~\ref{rem:cs}. No third
option produces a stateless signature: the only way to avoid materialising the
forest \emph{and} avoid on-demand secret derivation is to restrict the leaf set,
which is statefulness (Section~\ref{sec:fors-stateful}).
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:dichotomy} is the headline negative result: \emph{stock stateless}
threshold SLH-DSA with no-reconstruct is, in essence, generic hash-MPC. The
constructive value lies in (i)~the FORS component, which is realisable and proven,
and (ii)~the \emph{stateful} relaxation, which escapes Wall~1.

\section{Distributed FORS-leaf threshold opening}
\label{sec:fors}

This is the concrete, no-reconstruct, stock-verifiable sub-protocol. It realises the
FORS bottom of an SLH-DSA signature from a $t$-of-$n$ committee.

\subsection{Construction}
\label{sec:fors-construction}

\paragraph{Sharing.} Each FORS leaf secret is an $n$-byte value. We share it
byte-wise over $\GF_{257}$ (the smallest prime $> 255$; every byte is a distinct
field element). Party $p$ at evaluation point $x_p \in [1,257)$ holds, for each
shared leaf, the vector $\big(f_b(x_p)\big)_{b<n}$ where $f_b$ is a degree-$(t-1)$
polynomial with constant term the $b$-th leaf byte and \emph{independent} random
higher coefficients per leaf (coefficient reuse across leaves leaks differences and
is explicitly avoided).

\paragraph{Setup (keygen).} For a FORS keypair address $K$ and digest $md$, the
setup provisions the $k$ message-selected leaves: it shares each selected leaf secret
and publishes the public material---the $k$ authentication paths and
$\mathsf{pk}_{\mathrm{FORS}}$. The public auth-path nodes and $\mathsf{pk}_{\mathrm{
FORS}}$ are hashes of the secret leaves; computing them from shares without exposing
unopened leaves requires either an MPC over $\Fhash$ at keygen or a setup-TCB dealer
(``dealer for setup only''). The reference uses the dealer pattern and \emph{erases}
all leaf material before returning; the dealerless+MPC keygen is the documented
keygen cost. The SIGN-side wire shape is identical under either keygen.

\paragraph{Partial open.} Party $p$ releases its $\GF_{257}$ share vectors for the
$k$ selected leaves, bound by a commitment to $(K, md, p)$ so the shares cannot be
replayed for a different keypair or message.

\paragraph{Combine (public).} Anyone collecting $\geq t$ valid partial opens
interpolates, \emph{per leaf}, the $n$-byte leaf secret over $\GF_{257}$---guarded so
each interpolation is exactly one leaf wide---then writes the leaf secrets and the
public authentication paths into the FORS signature buffer. Each opened leaf address
is burned in the one-time ledger before its secret is released.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{$\mathsf{OpenForsThreshold}$ (public combiner, no-reconstruct)}
\label{alg:open}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require public material $(K, md, \{\text{authpath}_j\}_{j<k}, \mathsf{pk}_{\mathrm{FORS}}, \pkseed, t)$; partial opens $\{P_p\}$; burn ledger $L$
\Ensure FORS signature $\sigma_{\mathrm{FORS}}$, byte-identical to centralised \textsf{forsSign}
\State validate each $P_p$ against its commit on $(K, md, p)$; collect $\geq t$ share-points per FORS tree $j$
\For{$j = 0$ to $k-1$}
  \State \textbf{assert} interpolation width $=n$ \Comment{C1 guard: one leaf, never the seed}
  \State $L.\mathsf{Burn}(\mathrm{addr}(K,j), md)$ \Comment{C3: abort on reuse (slashable)}
  \State $\mathsf{sk}_j \gets \mathsf{LagrangeAtZero}_{\GF_{257}}(\text{any } t \text{ share-points})$ \Comment{one leaf wide}
  \State write $\mathsf{sk}_j$ and $\text{authpath}_j$ into $\sigma_{\mathrm{FORS}}$ at tree $j$
\EndFor
\State \Return $\sigma_{\mathrm{FORS}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Security}
\label{sec:fors-security}

\begin{theorem}[Byte-identity]
\label{thm:byte}
If the shared leaves equal the leaves of a genuine FORS keypair $K$ and the
published authentication paths equal that keypair's, then for any $t$ honest partial
opens, $\mathsf{OpenForsThreshold}$ outputs the byte-identical FORS signature that
centralised FIPS-205 \textsf{forsSign} produces on the same $(K, md)$, and
\textsf{forsPkFromSig} of the output equals $\mathsf{pk}_{\mathrm{FORS}}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\GF_{257}$ Lagrange interpolation of $t$ shares of a degree-$(t-1)$ polynomial
returns its constant term, i.e. the exact leaf byte. Hence each interpolated
$\mathsf{sk}_j$ equals the genuine leaf secret. The combiner writes
$(\mathsf{sk}_j, \text{authpath}_j)$ into the FORS signature at the same offsets
\textsf{forsSign} uses, with identical public auth-path bytes; the outputs are
byte-equal. \textsf{forsPkFromSig} is a deterministic public function of those
bytes, so it reproduces $\mathsf{pk}_{\mathrm{FORS}}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Stock-FIPS-205 acceptance]
The assembled FORS signature is accepted by the unmodified FIPS-205 FORS
public-key reconstruction (Algorithm~17), which is byte-equal to the cloudflare/circl
reference. A full signature embedding this FORS component (with a matching
hypertree part) verifies under stock SLH-DSA Verify.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[No-reconstruct, leaf-width]
\label{thm:noreconstruct}
$\mathsf{OpenForsThreshold}$ interpolates secret material only at width $n$ (one
leaf). It never forms a buffer of the $\geq 4n$-byte seed/secret-key width, and it
never invokes the leaf-deriving PRF, the seed expansion, or a centralised signer.
The joint view of any $t-1$ parties is independent of every unopened leaf and of the
(nonexistent) master seed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The combiner's only secret-bearing operation is the per-leaf
$\GF_{257}$ interpolation at line~5 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:open}, of fixed width $n$,
asserted at line~3 (any wider width is refused before the secret is touched). The
construction has \emph{no master seed}: leaves are shared directly, so there is no
seed object to reconstruct---C1 holds vacuously for the seed and by the width-guard
for any secret. Key-secrecy of each unopened leaf is Shamir
information-theoretic secrecy over $\GF_{257}$ for a degree-$(t-1)$ polynomial given
$\leq t-1$ evaluations; the opened leaves are exactly the FIPS-205 one-time material
a signature reveals anyway, no more.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Mechanised structural gate]
The no-reconstruct property of Theorem~\ref{thm:noreconstruct} is enforced
mechanically in the reference implementation by a static gate that parses the
combiner and asserts its call graph excludes every seed-reconstruction / secret-side
primitive (the leaf-deriving PRF, the seed expansion, the centralised signer, the
share-dealing routines) and includes the leaf-width interpolation and the C1/C3
guards (Section~\ref{sec:impl}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{The stateful full-signature lane}
\label{sec:fors-stateful}

For a complete signature, Wall~1 forbids materialising the SLH-DSA hypertree. The
escape is to use a \emph{stateful} hash-based signature over a tractable tree
(XMSS$^{MT}$/LMS, $h \le 20\text{--}25$): the committee distributively generates a
secret-shared tree, publishes all public WOTS$^+$ keys and internal nodes (so
authentication paths are public lookups), and at sign time advances to the next
unused leaf and threshold-opens only that leaf's WOTS$^+$ chain material. This is
exactly threshold leaf opening (approach~B) over a materialisable tree, and it is
genuinely no-reconstruct at signing (only one one-time leaf is opened). Its public
key is a stock SP~800-208 public key and its signatures verify under the stock LMS/
XMSS verifier. The price is statefulness, which becomes the distributed burn-state of
Section~\ref{sec:onetime}; this is the NIST Haystack lane~\cite{haystack}. It is a
different FIPS standard than stateless SLH-DSA and is offered as the
\emph{max-assurance hash lane}, not as FIPS-205.

\section{One-time material: the crux}
\label{sec:onetime}

The deepest obstruction is not the no-reconstruct property of a single opening---the
FORS component achieves it---but \emph{global} one-time/few-time discipline across a
live, possibly-Byzantine committee.

\paragraph{WOTS$^+$ strict one-time.} If the committee ever opens chain material for
one WOTS$^+$ address under two distinct message digests, the shorter chains let an
adversary forge the longer ones: full WOTS$^+$ key recovery, hence a hypertree
forgery. The committee must guarantee that for every
$(\text{layer},\text{tree},\text{leaf})$ WOTS$^+$ address, chain material is opened
for \emph{at most one} message digest, forever.

\paragraph{FORS few-time.} Opening leaves of one FORS keypair across several
messages degrades few-time security; the budget set by $(k, a)$ must not be
exceeded.

\paragraph{Stateless selection becomes distributed state.} In stateless SLH-DSA the
keypair selection $(\mathit{idxTree}, \mathit{idxLeaf})$ is
$R = \PRFmsg(\skprf, \pkseed, M')$, and the $2^h$ tree makes reuse negligible for a
single signer. In a threshold setting $\skprf$ is itself shared, so $R$ must be
produced by the committee, and the committee must \emph{detect and refuse} any second
opening of an already-burned one-time address. This is the ``stateful subprotocol
under a stateless-looking certificate boundary'' of NIST Haystack~\cite{haystack}.

\begin{definition}[Burn-state]
A burn-state is a map from one-time addresses to the message digests they were
consumed for, with: \emph{(idempotence)} re-opening the same $(\text{addr},
\text{digest})$ is benign; \emph{(conflict-detection)} opening an addr for a
\emph{different} digest is refused and yields the two-digest tuple as slashable
evidence; \emph{(few-time budget)} FORS addresses admit a bounded reuse count.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Reduction to a Byzantine-safe burn-state]
\label{thm:reduction}
Assume (i)~a no-reconstruct threshold opening of every one-time leaf on the
$R$-selected path (the FORS component of Section~\ref{sec:fors} plus its WOTS$^+$
analogue over a materialisable tree), and (ii)~a burn-state that is \emph{safe} under
$t-1$ Byzantine corruptions and adversarial scheduling: no honest party ever
co-signs an opening that would re-open a strictly-one-time WOTS$^+$ address or exceed
a FORS few-time budget. Then the committee produces stock-verifiable signatures
satisfying C1, C2, C3.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
C2 (stock verify) and C1 (no-reconstruct) follow from
Theorems~\ref{thm:byte}--\ref{thm:noreconstruct} applied to each opened leaf along the
$R$-path and from Proposition~\ref{prop:root} for the assembled root. C3 (one-time
safety) is exactly assumption~(ii): the burn-state refuses every reuse before the
one-time secret is released, so no reusable secret leaks. The signature thus carries
only the single-use material a stock signature reveals, and verifies to $\pkroot$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The residual obstruction, stated exactly]
Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} isolates the open problem: a \emph{Byzantine-safe
distributed burn-state}. A local, single-process ledger (the reference
implementation) trivially satisfies the burn-state definition---it refuses reuse and
emits slashable conflicts---but does not survive crashes, restarts, network
partitions, or Byzantine equivocation, under which two honest sub-committees could be
driven to open the same WOTS$^+$ address for different messages. Lifting the
burn-state to consensus-anchored burn proofs (each opening gated on a finalised
no-reuse certificate) is the missing piece. It is a state-machine-replication
problem layered \emph{under} the cryptography, and it is why the full Track-B
construction must remain fail-closed: the crypto is no-reconstruct and stock-
verifiable, but the one-time discipline that hash-based signatures demand is not yet
proven Byzantine-safe. \textbf{One-time-material handling, not no-reconstruct
opening, is the true wall for the full scheme.}
\end{remark}

\section{Implementation and fail-closed admission}
\label{sec:impl}

The reference implementation accompanies this paper in the Magnetar package
(\texttt{ref/\allowbreak go/\allowbreak pkg/\allowbreak magnetar}). It comprises:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \texttt{threshold\_noreconstruct.go} --- the Track-B interface and the
\emph{single} admission gate. The signing entry returns
\texttt{ErrNoReconstructUnproven}; \texttt{NoReconstructProven()} is hard-wired
\texttt{false}; \texttt{AdmitMagnetarThresholdToPolarisMax(TrackB)} refuses. Track B
can therefore never be silently admitted to \texttt{POLARIS\_MAX}. It also hosts the
\texttt{BurnLedger} (the burn-state of Section~\ref{sec:onetime}) and the
constraint-1 leaf-width guard.
\item \texttt{fors\_threshold\_open.go} --- the distributed FORS-leaf opening of
Section~\ref{sec:fors}: setup, partial open, public combiner, and stock-FIPS-205
verification (Algorithm~17).
\item Tests proving: byte-identity to centralised \textsf{forsSign} and stock
acceptance across SHAKE-192s and 256s with strict $t$-of-$n$ subsets
(Theorem~\ref{thm:byte}); one-time burn refusal with slashable evidence
(Section~\ref{sec:onetime}); commit-mismatch rejection; the fail-closed signer and
admission gate; the leaf-width guard; and a \emph{structural AST gate}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:noreconstruct}, mechanised) that the combiner never calls a
seed-reconstruction primitive---contrasted against the existing reveal-and-aggregate
combiner, which does.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Policy.} The refined Quasar policy table places Track~B only in the
research tier \texttt{MAGNETAR\_EXPERIMENTAL}; \texttt{POLARIS\_MAX} uses Track~A
(Magnetar-Quorum) for the hash lane. \texttt{NoReconstructProven()} stays
\texttt{false} until a no-reconstruct producer for the \emph{complete} signature and
a Byzantine-safe burn-state are proven. Flipping it is a reviewed act gated on
Table~\ref{tab:map}'s open rows.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Threshold hash-based signatures are nascent. NIST's \emph{Haystack} effort on
threshold and distributed \emph{stateful} HBS~\cite{haystack} is the closest line;
its distributed-state techniques are directly relevant and motivate our stateful
lane and burn-state. Cozzo and Smart~\cite{cozzosmart} bounded the cost of
thresholdising NIST signatures and flagged the hash-MPC barrier we formalise in
Theorem~\ref{thm:dichotomy}. The underlying primitives are SPHINCS$^+$/SLH-DSA
\cite{sphincsplus,fips205}, XMSS/LMS \cite{xmss,sp800208}, Shamir secret
sharing~\cite{shamir}, verifiable secret sharing and dealerless DKG
\cite{feldman,pedersen,gennaro}, and generic MPC for the hash-evaluation route
\cite{gmw,spdz}. The ZK-consistency hardening (approach~D) uses transparent
post-quantum STARKs~\cite{stark}. In contrast to the lattice threshold lanes, where
the response is linear and thresholdisation is algebraic, the hash lane has no such
structure---which is precisely the gap this paper charts.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

No-reconstruct threshold SLH-DSA is not a single problem but a stack. The
no-reconstruct \emph{opening} of one-time leaves is solved and proven, and we ship a
stock-FIPS-205-verifiable FORS-leaf component to demonstrate it. Stock
\emph{stateless} SLH-DSA reduces to generic MPC over $\mathsf{SHAKE}$ and is
max-assurance only. The buildable full lane is \emph{stateful} hash-based signing
over a materialisable tree. And the binding obstruction for any live, Byzantine
deployment is a \emph{distributed burn-state} that enforces WOTS$^+$ one-time and
FORS few-time discipline---one-time-material handling, not the threshold opening, is
the wall. Until that state is proven Byzantine-safe and a complete-signature producer
exists, Magnetar-Threshold stays fail-closed and excluded from \texttt{POLARIS\_MAX};
the trustless hash lane in production is Magnetar-Quorum (Track~A).
