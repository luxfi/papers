\section{Path to public-DKG threshold HBS (open research)}
\label{sec:fullmpc}

The per-validator standalone construction of \S\ref{sec:construction}
is the architecturally correct public-BFT primitive for hash-based
post-quantum consensus today. There is, however, an open research
question worth specifying: \emph{is there a true threshold HBS
construction that does not require TEE attestation or a dealer in
the TCB?}

This section surveys the open path. We are deliberate about scope:
this section \emph{does not} specify a new construction. It
identifies the load-bearing technical obstacles, surveys the
candidate sub-protocols, and frames the open research the artefact's
\texttt{ref/go/pkg/thbs/dkg2/} skeleton scaffolds.

\subsection{Why FROST-style threshold does not apply}
\label{sec:fullmpc:no-komlo}

FROST \cite{frost,rfc9591} is the canonical two-round threshold
construction for Schnorr signatures. Its core property is the
\emph{linear share-aggregation identity}:
\begin{equation}
  z = \sum_{i \in T} z_i \quad \text{where} \quad z_i = k_i + c \cdot \lambda_i \cdot s_i,
\end{equation}
in which each $z_i$ is a per-party scalar and $z$ is the final scalar
in the Schnorr signature. Adding the per-party contributions modulo
the group order yields a valid signature without the aggregator ever
seeing the private key $s$. This pattern generalises to:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=2em]
  \item Pulsar (M-LWE): per-party $z_i \in R_q^{n_{\mathrm{vec}}}$
        adds linearly modulo $q$ to produce the joint $z$.
  \item Corona (R-LWE): per-party $z_i \in R_q$ adds linearly
        modulo $q$ to produce the joint $z$.
  \item BLS aggregate: per-party signature $\sigma_i \in \mathbb{G}_1$
        adds in the elliptic curve group to produce the joint
        $\sigma$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{SLH-DSA has no such identity.} The FIPS 205 signature is a
\emph{Merkle authentication path} composed with a \emph{WOTS+
one-time signature} on a \emph{FORS digest}. There is no per-party
scalar $z_i$ that adds linearly to produce the joint signature.
Concretely:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=2em]
  \item The WOTS+ hash chains are evaluated via $w$-time hashing of
        the FORS digest through a chain of $w$ public hash images.
        Per-party shares of the chain endpoints do not combine
        linearly --- hashing is non-linear.
  \item The XMSS-HT authentication path is a sequence of Merkle
        sibling hashes; each sibling is a hash of two child nodes,
        and per-party shares of sibling hashes do not combine
        linearly --- hash functions are not group homomorphisms.
\end{itemize}

A direct port of FROST/Pulsar's linear-aggregation pattern is
\emph{ruled out by the SLH-DSA construction}. This is a load-
bearing algorithmic obstacle: no choice of parameters, hash
function family, or per-party masking can paper over the fact that
SLH-DSA is not a linear scheme. The literature (Cozzo-Smart 2019,
Bonte-Smart-Tan 2023, NIST IR 8214) is uniform on this point.

\subsection{Candidate sub-protocols for public-DKG THBS}
\label{sec:fullmpc:topology}

A public-DKG instantiation of the McGrew threshold-HBS pattern
(\cite{mcgrew2019}; Appendix~\ref{app:thbs}) requires two layers
to be built without a dealer in the TCB. We survey the candidates.

\paragraph{Layer (A): PVSS distribution of per-element entropy.}
The standard Pedersen / Feldman / Schoenmakers PVSS
\cite{schoenmakers1999} framework distributes a secret $x =
\sum_j r_j \bmod p$ across parties without any party learning
$x$. For McGrew threshold HBS, the per-element secrets are the
individual WOTS+ chain heads $\mathbf{w}_{\mathit{layer},
\mathit{tree}, \mathit{leaf}, c}$ and FORS secret leaves
$\mathbf{f}_{\mathit{tree}, k, i}$; each needs its own PVSS
distribution, which is feasible but introduces a large
per-distribution overhead (a single SLH-DSA-SHAKE-192s key has
on the order of $2^{16}$ secret elements).

The PVSS layer ships as a research-grade SKELETON at
\texttt{ref/go/pkg/thbs/dkg2/}:
\texttt{Deal}, \texttt{Verify}, \texttt{Complaint},
\texttt{Defense}, and \texttt{Run} are wire-shape defined, with
sentinel errors \texttt{ErrSkeletonOnly} and
\texttt{ErrMPCRootNotImpl} preventing production consumption.

\paragraph{Layer (B): MPC over the SHA-256 / SHAKE hash tree.}
Once layer (A) distributes the secret leaves without any party
holding them in the clear, the Merkle root of the WOTS+ public
keys must be computed from those shared leaves without
revealing them. This requires MPC over the hash function:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item \textbf{SPDZ-style preprocessed MPC}
        (Damg{\aa}rd-Pastro-Smart-Zakarias 2012,
        Damg{\aa}rd-Keller-Larraia-Pastro-Scholl-Smart 2013).
        Generic but expensive: per-AND-gate cost is several
        orders of magnitude higher than plaintext.
  \item \textbf{Garbled-circuit MPC} (Yao;
        Wang-Ranellucci-Katz 2017 ``Global-Scale Secure
        Multi-party Computation''). Communication-heavy at
        SHAKE256 ($\sim 1600$ permutation rounds per leaf hash).
  \item \textbf{Function Secret Sharing} (Boyle-Gilboa-Ishai
        2015). Promising for specific hash function families
        but no concrete construction for SHAKE256 yet.
\end{itemize}

The MPC-root layer is open research tracked at
\texttt{BLOCKERS.md::MAGNETAR-PUBLIC-DKG-1}. Preliminary cost
estimates put a single SHAKE-192s key's MPC-root computation at
multi-second per leaf, multi-megabyte communication per leaf,
which is operationally tractable for one-time DKG setup but is
the load-bearing complexity.

\paragraph{Layer (C, alternative): MPC at sign time over selected
elements only.} The McGrew threshold-HBS construction releases
shares only of the selected (per-message-randomiser) WOTS+ chain
heads and FORS leaves. If the per-element secrets are PVSS-
distributed via layer (A), then the sign-time MPC only needs to
operate on the selected subset --- typically a few hundred
elements per signature, not the full $\sim 2^{16}$. This is a
much smaller workload than computing the full Merkle root under
MPC.

Layer (C) is the natural follow-on to layer (A): if PVSS-
distributed secrets are available, the sign-time aggregation can
recover only the selected elements via Lagrange interpolation
under MPC, which is a linear computation over the prime field
and admits standard SPDZ techniques. The cost is dominated by
the number of selected elements rather than the full tree size.

\subsection{Suggested public-DKG THBS direction}
\label{sec:fullmpc:direction}

A future public-DKG threshold HBS construction would combine:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item Layer (A) PVSS distribution of per-element secrets ---
        skeleton at \texttt{thbs/dkg2/}; production deployment
        requires complaint / defense rounds and verifiable
        dealer protocols.
  \item Layer (B) MPC root computation at DKG time, run once per
        key generation. Multi-second per leaf, parallelizable
        across leaves, tractable as a one-time setup cost.
  \item Layer (C) selected-element Lagrange under MPC at sign
        time. Per-signature cost dominated by the FORS leaf
        count ($\sim 17 \cdot 15 = 255$ leaves at SHAKE-192s)
        rather than the full tree.
\end{enumerate}

This is a multi-month research direction. The \texttt{thbs/dkg2/}
skeleton scaffolds the API surface so that future production code
slots in cleanly; every public function returns a sentinel
\texttt{ErrSkeletonOnly} or \texttt{ErrMPCRootNotImpl} that
prevents accidental consumption today.

\subsection{What public-DKG THBS would and would not solve}
\label{sec:fullmpc:limits}

\paragraph{What it would solve.} A working public-DKG THBS would
admit a $t$-of-$n$ threshold SLH-DSA construction with the same
public-BFT-safe trust posture as Pulsar / Corona threshold
schemes: no dealer, no aggregator-in-TCB, no TEE required. The
sign-time output is a single FIPS 205-shaped signature that
verifies under any unmodified verifier.

\paragraph{What it would not solve.} Even a successful
public-DKG THBS carries residual concerns:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item \textbf{One-time keys remain one-time.} The FORS / WOTS+
        per-element structure is intrinsically one-time per
        slot; same-slot reuse extracts the secret regardless of
        the DKG mechanism. Slot management (the consensus
        layer's $(\kappa, \mathit{slot})$ binding) remains a
        critical operational invariant.
  \item \textbf{MPC implementation correctness.} A
        misimplemented MPC that leaks even one bit of a secret
        element to one party defeats the security goal. Tier A
        for a public-DKG THBS would require a mechanised proof
        of the MPC's privacy (EasyCrypt or equivalent).
  \item \textbf{Per-DKG cost.} Layer (B) MPC-root computation
        at DKG time is a multi-second per leaf, multi-megabyte
        per leaf operation. Refreshing the DKG (resharing to a
        new validator set) is a heavy ceremony.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Operational implications of \emph{not} solving it.}
For public-BFT consensus, the per-validator standalone primitive
of \S\ref{sec:construction} is sufficient: each validator signs
independently and the consensus layer collects the signatures.
There is no operational pressure to deploy a public-DKG THBS
construction in the public-BFT path. The pressure is from the
TEE-only custody path (M-Chain bridge, A-Chain confidential
compute), where a public-DKG THBS would eliminate the TEE
requirement and admit a cleaner trust posture.

We do not promise that a public-DKG THBS will be available in
any specific release window. The research is open, and the path
is not a straight-line extension of the per-validator standalone
primitive. The current production posture is: public-BFT runs
the standalone primitive of this paper, and custody runs the
TEE-attested threshold forms of Appendix~\ref{app:thbs} and
Appendix~\ref{app:reveal}.

\subsection{Adjacent literature}
\label{sec:fullmpc:litreview}

The threshold-SLH-DSA literature is sparse compared to the
threshold-lattice and threshold-ECDSA literatures.

\paragraph{Cosmin and Khovratovich (SAC 2021) \cite{cosmin2021}.}
The earliest dedicated treatment of threshold SLH-DSA (then
``Threshold SPHINCS+''). The construction is a single-party
SPHINCS+ where the WOTS+ secret is shared via Shamir over the
hash function's output domain. The aggregator reconstructs the
secret at sign time --- structurally identical to the
reveal-and-aggregate of Appendix~\ref{app:reveal}. Their analysis
is in the random-oracle model with the aggregator-honest caveat.

\paragraph{McGrew et al. (IACR ePrint 2019/793) \cite{mcgrew2019}.}
The earliest treatment of true threshold HBS with selected-
element reconstruction (the THBS pattern of
Appendix~\ref{app:thbs}). The construction shares individual
WOTS+ chain heads and FORS leaves rather than the master seed.
The v1 setup is dealer-backed; the public-DKG variant is the
open research direction of this section.

\paragraph{Komlo--Stebila--Sullivan (2025 forthcoming, MPTC).}
A draft NIST MPTC project on distributed FORS evaluation for
threshold SLH-DSA. Preliminary results align with the layer-(C)
sign-time MPC approach. Magnetar tracks this work as a direct
candidate for a future public-DKG construction.

\paragraph{Threshold MAYO and Threshold UOV.} The
multivariate-quadratic family (MAYO \cite{mayo}, UOV) has
similar non-linearity issues; threshold constructions
\cite{thresholduov} also rely on reveal-and-aggregate or
hardware-enclave assumptions. The threshold-MAYO literature is
even sparser than threshold-SLH-DSA. Both families share the
``no linear share-aggregation identity'' obstacle that rules out
FROST-style constructions.

\paragraph{Generic-MPC threshold signatures.} A fully-generic
secure-multiparty-computation framework (BGW \cite{bgw1988},
GMW \cite{gmw1987}) could in principle compute any function ---
including FIPS 205 SLH-DSA --- under threshold. The constant-
factor overhead is the obstacle: per-gate costs in generic MPC
are several orders of magnitude higher than per-gate costs in
plaintext computation, and the SHAKE256 permutation has
$\sim 10^5$ gate operations per call. Generic MPC for full
SLH-DSA is not operationally viable at present.
