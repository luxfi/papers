\section{Reveal-and-Aggregate over the SLH-DSA Scheme Seed (TEE-only)}
\label{app:reveal}

This appendix documents the \emph{reveal-and-aggregate} threshold
construction shipped at \texttt{ref/go/pkg/magnetar/combine.go} under
the entry point
\texttt{magnetar.CombineWithSeedReconstruction}. The construction is
the canonical hash-based instantiation of the
``share-the-seed-and-reconstruct'' pattern: the SLH-DSA scheme seed
$S$ is byte-wise Shamir-shared over $\mathrm{GF}(257)$ across a
$(t, n)$ committee at DKG time, and at sign time the aggregator
recovers $S$ via Lagrange interpolation in memory, invokes the
single-party FIPS 205 $\textsc{SignDeterministic}$, and zeroizes the
secret material before return. The output signature is byte-identical
to a centralized FIPS 205 signature on the same
$(\mathit{seed}, \mu, \mathit{ctx})$ triple.

\paragraph{Trust model: TEE-only, by policy.} The aggregator process
holds the reconstructed scheme seed $S$ in memory for the duration of
one \texttt{Combine} call ($\sim 1$--$50$~ms wall-clock on M2 Pro at
SHAKE-192s, dominated by the SLH-DSA signing latency). An adversary
with arbitrary code-execution rights on the aggregator host during
this window can extract $S$ via the standard kernel-level vectors
(\texttt{/proc/<pid>/mem}, ptrace, coredumps, swap-file leaks). This
is the \emph{aggregator-honest} caveat, and it is the same trust
model the earliest Magnetar releases (v0.1, v0.2) shipped under as
the headline construction. The trust caveat is not a
construction-level flaw; it is intrinsic to any reveal-and-aggregate
pattern over a hash-based secret. The pattern is appropriate when
the aggregator host is hardware-attested confidential compute (AMD
SEV-SNP with remote attestation, Intel TDX, or equivalent), in
which case the trust model collapses to ``the attested enclave is
honest'' --- a defensible posture for M-Chain bridge custody and
A-Chain confidential compute.

\paragraph{NOT in scope for public-BFT consensus.} Public-BFT Lux
validator quorums use the per-validator standalone primitive of the
main paper. The reveal-and-aggregate construction is appropriate
only when the aggregator host is in the TCB by deployment policy.

\subsection{Reveal-and-aggregate protocol surface}
\label{app:reveal:surface}

The construction is byte-exact to the entry point at
\texttt{ref/go/pkg/magnetar/combine.go} and the protocol overlay
under \texttt{ref/go/pkg/magnetar/dkg.go} and
\texttt{ref/go/pkg/magnetar/threshold.go}.

\paragraph{DKG (three-round broadcast).}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Round 1 (dealer $P_i$).} Sample a contribution
        $c_i \in \{0,1\}^{8 \cdot \textsc{seed\_size}}$ and
        $2(t-1) \cdot \textsc{seed\_size}$ bytes of polynomial
        coefficient material. For each byte position $b$, define
        $f_{b,i}(x) = c_i[b] + \sum_{j=1}^{t-1} a_{b,j,i} \cdot
        x^j \pmod{257}$. For each recipient $P_k$, compute
        $\mathit{share}_{i \to k}[b] = f_{b,i}(x_k) \bmod 257$
        and broadcast the per-recipient envelope.
  \item \textbf{Round 2 (party $P_i$).} Validate every received
        envelope; compute and broadcast the transcript digest
        $D = \textsc{cSHAKE256}(
        \tau_{\mathrm{DKG}}, \ldots,
        32\,\mathrm{B})$ binding the full Round-1 record.
  \item \textbf{Round 3 (party $P_i$).} Verify every other party's
        $D$ matches the locally computed value; an equivocation
        emits identifiable abort evidence naming the offending
        dealer. Compute the per-party share of the master
        byte-sum, the master byte-sum itself (sum of dealer
        contributions modulo 257), and the committee root. Mix
        to the master seed via
        $S = \textsc{cSHAKE256}(
        \mathit{masterByteSum} \,\Vert\,
        \mathit{committee\_root}, \ldots,
        \textsc{seed\_size}\,\mathrm{B})$, derive $(sk, pk) \gets
        \textsc{slhdsa.DeriveKey}(S)$, publish $pk$ as the joint
        group public key, discard $sk$, zeroize $S$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Threshold sign (two-round commit-and-reveal).}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Round 1 (signer $P_i$).} Derive a per-attempt mask
        $r_i$ via cSHAKE256, XOR-mask the local share
        $\mathit{masked}_i = \mathit{share}_i \oplus r_i$, build
        the binding transcript $\tau_1$, and broadcast a 32-byte
        commit $D_i = \textsc{cSHAKE256}(r_i \,\Vert\,
        \mathit{masked}_i \,\Vert\, \tau_1, \ldots)$.
  \item \textbf{Round 2 (signer $P_i$).} Validate session binding;
        broadcast the reveal $r_i \,\Vert\, \mathit{masked}_i$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Combine (aggregator).} The aggregator validates the
Round-2 reveals against the Round-1 commits in constant time,
recovers $\mathit{share}_i = \mathit{masked}_i \oplus r_i$ for
each signer in the quorum, runs Lagrange interpolation over
$\mathrm{GF}(257)$ on the first $t$ recovered shares to obtain
$\mathit{reconstructedByteSum}$, recomputes
$\mathit{committee\_root}$ from the full
\texttt{KeyShare} directory, mixes via cSHAKE256 to the
reconstructed master seed
$S_{\mathrm{rec}}$, derives $(sk_{\mathrm{rec}},
pk_{\mathrm{rec}}) \gets \textsc{slhdsa.DeriveKey}(S_{\mathrm{rec}})$,
verifies $pk_{\mathrm{rec}} = pk$, invokes either
$\textsc{SignDeterministic}$ (default) or
$\textsc{SignRandomized}$ on $sk_{\mathrm{rec}}$, and zeroizes
$S_{\mathrm{rec}}$, $sk_{\mathrm{rec}}$, and the intermediate
buffers before return.

\subsection{Byte-equality to single-party FIPS 205}
\label{app:reveal:byteequal}

\begin{theorem}[Reveal-and-aggregate byte equality]
\label{thm:reveal-byte-equality}
Fix a parameter set $\textsc{Mode} \in \{\mathrm{SHAKE\text{-}192s},
\mathrm{SHAKE\text{-}192f}, \mathrm{SHAKE\text{-}256s}\}$ and let
$(n, t)$ be a valid threshold configuration. Let \texttt{DKG}
complete honestly, producing a joint public key $pk$ and shares
$\{\textsc{KeyShare}_j\}_{j=1}^n$. Let $S$ be the DKG master seed.

For any message $\mu$, context $\mathit{ctx}$, session identifier
$\mathit{sid}$, attempt $\kappa$, and quorum $Q$ with $|Q| = t$, if
every party in $Q$ executes the threshold-sign protocol honestly
and the aggregator executes \texttt{Combine} honestly with
$\mathit{randomized} = \mathrm{false}$, then
\begin{equation*}
  \textsc{Combine}(\cdots)
  \;=\;
  \textsc{slhdsa.SignDeterministic}(\textsc{slhdsa.DeriveKey}(S),
    \mu, \mathit{ctx}).
\end{equation*}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By inspection. Any quorum $Q$ of size $\ge t$ reconstructs the
same $\mathit{masterByteSum}$ from $\{\mathit{mySumShare}_i\}_{i
\in Q}$ via the standard Shamir Lagrange identity. The cSHAKE256
mix is deterministic, so the reconstructed seed equals the DKG
master seed. FIPS 205 $\textsc{DeriveKey}$ and
$\textsc{SignDeterministic}$ are deterministic functions of their
inputs, so the output signature is byte-identical to the
single-party FIPS 205 output on the same $(S, \mu, \mathit{ctx})$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Empirical pin.}
\texttt{TestN1\_ByteEquality\_ThresholdMatchesCentralized} at
\texttt{ref/go/pkg/magnetar/n1\_byte\_equality\_test.go} runs the
threshold path end-to-end against the centralized FIPS 205 path
and asserts byte-equality across $(n, t) \in \{(3,2), (5,3),
(7,4)\}$.

\subsection{Where reveal-and-aggregate lives in the Lux stack}
\label{app:reveal:where}

\paragraph{M-Chain bridge custody.} The reveal-and-aggregate
construction is the simpler of the two TEE-only custody options;
its trust model is slightly coarser than THBS (the aggregator
holds the full $S$, not just selected elements), but it is
operationally simpler to deploy and audit.
\texttt{CombineWithSeedReconstruction} is the entry point.

\paragraph{A-Chain confidential compute.} Same posture as M-Chain
bridge custody. Pick reveal-and-aggregate when operational
simplicity dominates; pick THBS (Appendix~\ref{app:thbs}) when
the tighter TCB exposure on selected elements is preferred.

\paragraph{NOT in scope for public-BFT consensus.} Public-BFT
deployments use the per-validator standalone primitive of the
main paper, never reveal-and-aggregate.

\subsection{Operational hardening (TEE deployment)}
\label{app:reveal:hardening}

For any deployment running the reveal-and-aggregate construction
inside a hardware-attested enclave, the \texttt{DEPLOYMENT-RUNBOOK.md}
\S``Aggregator hardening'' enumerates the production checklist:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item Run the aggregator in a memory-encrypted VM (AMD SEV-SNP
        or Intel TDX) with remote attestation; verify the
        attestation on every signing session.
  \item Disable coredumps (\texttt{ulimit -c 0},
        \texttt{kernel.core\_pattern \(\to\)} \texttt{/dev/null}).
  \item Disable swap (\texttt{swapoff -a}); disable hibernation
        (\texttt{systemctl mask hibernate.target sleep.target}).
  \item Disable ptrace
        (\texttt{kernel.yama.ptrace\_scope = 3}); bind the
        aggregator under \texttt{PR\_SET\_DUMPABLE = 0}.
  \item Fork-per-signature: \texttt{execve} a fresh image and exit
        immediately after \texttt{Combine} returns. This bounds
        the in-memory lifetime of $S$ to a single sign call.
  \item Bind under seccomp-bpf with a whitelist that forbids
        \texttt{ptrace}, \texttt{process\_vm\_readv}, and
        arbitrary \texttt{mmap} of \texttt{/proc/self/mem}.
  \item Run on dedicated hardware: no other user-controlled
        processes on the aggregator host.
\end{enumerate}
