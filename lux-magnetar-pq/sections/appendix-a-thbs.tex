\section{THBS --- TEE-Only Threshold HBS Construction}
\label{app:thbs}

This appendix documents the \emph{THBS} (true threshold hash-based
signature) construction shipped at \texttt{ref/go/pkg/thbs/} in the
same artefact. THBS is the canonical Magnetar instantiation of
McGrew--Fluhrer--Gazdag--Kampanakis--Morton--Westerbaan ``Coalition
and Threshold Hash-Based Signatures'' (IACR ePrint 2019/793 and the
IRTF \texttt{draft-mcgrew-hash-sigs} line of work). The construction
is a \emph{true} threshold HBS in the McGrew sense: parties hold
\emph{Shamir-shared individual WOTS+ chain heads and FORS leaves},
not shared seeds; for each message they release shares only of the
elements selected by the message-bound randomiser; the aggregator
reconstructs only those selected elements and assembles an ordinary
HBS-shaped signature. There is no master-seed reconstruction at
sign time.

\paragraph{Trust model: TEE-only, by policy.} The v1 setup is
\emph{dealer-backed}. A single dealer generates the per-element
WOTS+ chain heads and FORS secret leaves, Shamir-shares each across
the committee, and erases the master seed. The dealer learns every
secret element at setup time. This is appropriate for M-Chain bridge
custody and A-Chain confidential compute, where a hardware-attested
host (AMD SEV-SNP, Intel TDX, or equivalent confidential-compute
runtime) is in the TCB by deployment policy and the dealer-run
attestation chain transitively binds the dealer to the consensus
layer's trust roots. THBS v1 is \emph{not} public-BFT-safe: in a
public BFT setting the dealer cannot be in the TCB, and the trust
model collapses to ``the dealer signs everything.''

For public-BFT consensus on Lux the canonical primitive is the
per-validator standalone construction of the main paper
(\S\ref{sec:construction}). THBS exists alongside it as the
custodial primitive: the consensus layer uses
\texttt{ValidatorSign}; the M-Chain bridge / A-Chain confidential
compute layer uses \texttt{thbs.DealerDKG}, \texttt{thbs.SignShare},
and \texttt{thbs.Aggregate} inside an attested enclave.

\paragraph{Open research path to public-DKG THBS.} A truly
public-DKG instantiation of McGrew threshold HBS requires:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item PVSS distribution of per-element entropy contributions:
        $x_{\mathit{slot}, e} = \sum_j r_{j, \mathit{slot}, e}$
        with no party holding $x_{\mathit{slot}, e}$ in the clear
        (Schoenmakers PVSS \cite{schoenmakers1999}).
  \item MPC over the SHA-256 / SHAKE hash tree to compute the
        public Merkle root from secret-shared leaves without
        revealing the leaves (Damg{\aa}rd--Pastro--Smart--Zakarias
        SPDZ 2012, Wang--Ranellucci--Katz ``Global-Scale Secure
        Multiparty Computation'' 2017, Boyle--Gilboa--Ishai
        ``Function Secret Sharing'' 2015).
\end{enumerate}
The PVSS layer (1) ships as a research-grade skeleton at
\texttt{ref/go/pkg/thbs/dkg2/}: \texttt{Deal}, \texttt{Verify},
\texttt{Complaint}, \texttt{Defense}, and \texttt{Run} are wire-shape
defined, with sentinel errors \texttt{ErrSkeletonOnly} and
\texttt{ErrMPCRootNotImpl} preventing production consumption. The
MPC-root layer (2) is open research tracked at
\texttt{BLOCKERS.md::MAGNETAR-PUBLIC-DKG-1}; the candidate cost
estimate is multi-second-per-signature at SHAKE-192s (consistent
with the $\sim 10^4$ hash invocations under MPC). Until both layers
land, THBS is appropriate only for the TEE-attested custody path.

\subsection{THBS protocol surface}
\label{app:thbs:surface}

The wire surface lives at \texttt{ref/go/pkg/thbs/}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item \texttt{thbs.DealerDKG(params, t, n, masterSeed) \\
        \(\to\) ([]\textit{PolyKeyShare}, *\textit{ThresholdPublicKey})}
        --- the dealer-backed setup. The dealer derives the WOTS+
        chain heads $\mathbf{w}_{\mathit{layer}, \mathit{tree},
        \mathit{leaf}, c}$ and FORS secret leaves
        $\mathbf{f}_{\mathit{tree}, k, i}$ from
        $\mathit{masterSeed}$ exactly as FIPS 205
        $\textsc{KeyGen}$ does, Shamir-shares each element across
        the committee, emits one \texttt{PolyKeyShare} per party
        carrying the per-element shares, and erases
        $\mathit{masterSeed}$.
  \item \texttt{thbs.SignShare(share, slot, message, ctx) \\
        \(\to\) *\textit{PartialSignature}} --- party $i$'s
        contribution. The party deterministically derives the
        message-bound randomiser $R = \textsc{PRF}(\mathit{share}.
        \mathit{SKprf}, \mathit{ctx} \,\Vert\, \mathit{message})$
        and the FIPS 205 ADRS / leaf selectors from $R$, then
        releases shares of \emph{only} the selected WOTS+ chain
        heads and FORS leaves. Non-selected elements stay shared.
  \item \texttt{thbs.Aggregate(t, partials) \\
        \(\to\) ([]byte FIPS 205 sig)} --- the aggregator. Given
        $\ge t$ partial signatures with consistent slot binding,
        Lagrange-reconstructs the selected WOTS+ chain heads and
        FORS leaves, assembles the WOTS+ chain evaluations and the
        FORS authentication paths, and emits a wire-form FIPS 205
        signature.
  \item Slot-binding evidence: same-slot-different-message
        partial signatures emit
        \texttt{SlotConflictEvidence} carrying the offending
        message pair and signatures, suitable for on-chain
        slashing.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Selected-element reconstruction is the load-bearing
property.} Unlike reveal-and-aggregate over a master seed
(Appendix~\ref{app:reveal}), THBS at sign time reconstructs
\emph{only the WOTS+ chain heads and FORS leaves selected by $R$}
--- typically a few hundred elements per signature, not the full
secret key. The aggregator never holds $\mathit{SK.seed}$,
$\mathit{SK.prf}$, or the unselected elements. The trust posture is
strictly tighter than reveal-and-aggregate; the aggregator's TCB
exposure is the union of selected elements, not the full secret.

\paragraph{Slot guard.} The same WOTS+ chain head used to sign two
distinct messages enables WOTS+-forgery extraction by an attacker
who collects both partial signatures, which is the standard hash-
based one-time-signature failure mode. THBS guards against this by
persisting full \texttt{PartialSignature} records keyed by
$(\mathit{slot}, \mathit{digest})$: a second
\texttt{SignShare} request with the same $\mathit{slot}$ but a
different $\mathit{digest}$ aborts and emits
\texttt{SlotConflictEvidence} verifiable post-restart. The slot
counter monotonicity is enforced by the same consensus-layer
invariant Pulsar uses for $\kappa$
(cf. \cite{lp073pulsarpaper}~\S5).

\subsection{Construction-equivalence to single-party FIPS 205}
\label{app:thbs:byteequal}

The McGrew construction has the McGrew Class-N1-analog property:
the output signature is byte-identical to the single-party FIPS
205 signature that would be produced on the same
$(\mathit{masterSeed}, \mathit{message}, \mathit{ctx},
\mathit{slot})$ tuple. This is by construction: the dealer's
Shamir sharing preserves additive structure over the prime field;
Lagrange reconstruction recovers the selected chain heads and
leaves exactly; the WOTS+ chain evaluations are then computed
identically to single-party FIPS 205. The same FIPS 205 verifier
accepts the THBS output unmodified.

\subsection{Where THBS lives in the Lux stack}
\label{app:thbs:where}

\paragraph{M-Chain bridge custody.} The Lux M-Chain bridge custody
keypairs sign withdrawal authorisations under SLH-DSA. The
custody committee runs THBS v1 inside SEV-SNP-attested confidential
compute, with the dealer co-located in the same attestation chain
as the consensus layer. The setup ceremony is run once per key
generation, with the master seed inside the enclave and the
\texttt{SlotConflictEvidence} feed exposed as a public log.

\paragraph{A-Chain confidential compute.} The A-Chain confidential
compute layer uses THBS for the cross-shard confidential-attestation
keypairs. Same attestation discipline as M-Chain bridge custody.

\paragraph{NOT in scope for public-BFT consensus.} Lux P-Chain,
X-Chain, C-Chain, and the white-label EVM L1/L2s (Hanzo, Zoo, Pars
primary networks) run the per-validator standalone primitive of
the main paper, never THBS.
