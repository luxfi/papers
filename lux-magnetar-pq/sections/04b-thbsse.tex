\section{THBS-SE --- Selected-Element Permissionless Threshold}
\label{sec:thbsse}

This section specifies \emph{THBS-SE} (Threshold Hash-Based
Signatures with Selected-Element Reconstruction), the
permissionless threshold companion to the per-validator standalone
construction of \S\ref{sec:construction}. THBS-SE produces a
\emph{single} FIPS 205-shaped signature from a $t$-of-$n$ committee
on a slot-bound message; the public combiner role is open to any
peer; no host is in the TCB by policy at sign time. The construction
sits alongside the standalone primary primitive in
\texttt{ref/go/pkg/magnetar/thbsse.go} and produces wire bytes that
unmodified FIPS 205 verifiers (notably
\texttt{cloudflare/circl/sign/slhdsa.Verify}) accept verbatim.

\subsection{Motivation}
\label{sec:thbsse:motivation}

The per-validator standalone primitive ships $N$ independent
FIPS 205 signatures per consensus message and trusts the consensus
layer's certificate-counting logic to determine quorum. For
public-BFT consensus this is the right primitive. There are
deployments, however, where the verifier wants \emph{one} FIPS
205-shaped signature on a message from a committee: long-term
storage on a separate ledger, smart-contract verification with
fixed gas cost regardless of $n$, cross-chain bridging into a
system that recognises only one signature per message.

Cozzo and Smart \cite{cosmin2021} establish that producing a
single FIPS 205-shaped signature from $t$ shares requires either
(i) some combiner process reconstructing the seed at sign time, or
(ii) full MPC over the hash tree. THBS-SE chooses (i) with a
\emph{public combiner} role: anyone --- a validator, a block
proposer, an RPC node, a passive watcher --- can run Combine. The
combiner role is a PUBLIC role, not a privileged aggregator role;
the construction has no host in the TCB by policy.

\subsection{Hard invariant}
\label{sec:thbsse:invariant}

The construction enforces the named theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:thbsse-selected-element-reveal} in
\S\ref{sec:security:thbsse-reveal}):

\begin{quote}
\textbf{A revealed value is allowed only if it is also present in
the final SLH-DSA signature.}
\end{quote}

The construction-level instantiation:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Allowed reveals.} The per-round mask $r_i$, the
        masked share $s'_i = \mathit{share}_i \oplus r_i$, the
        Round-1 commit hash $D_i$, the final FIPS 205 signature
        bytes. The mask plus masked share lets the public combiner
        recover $\mathit{share}_i$ by byte-wise XOR; the share
        alone is information-theoretically uniform-random to
        anyone holding fewer than $t$ leaves.
  \item \textbf{Forbidden reveals.} $\mathit{SK.seed}$ in any
        party-local persistent form; $\mathit{SK.prf}$;
        future-slot share material; in the strict (v1.1) form,
        WOTS+ chain bases for unused chains and FORS leaves not
        selected by the message digest.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Slot binding and domain separation}
\label{sec:thbsse:slotbind}

Every signature is bound to a slot tuple
\begin{equation*}
  \mathit{bind} = (\mathit{chain\_id}, \mathit{epoch},
  \mathit{slot}, \mathit{height},
  \mathit{committee\_id}, \mathit{message\_domain}).
\end{equation*}

The binding flows into:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item the cSHAKE256 commit transcript $\tau$ (so the same
        committee cannot reuse the same share material on a
        different message), and
  \item the FIPS 205 ctx string at sign time, so any verifier
        holding $\mathit{bind}$ can derive
        $\mathit{ctx} = \texttt{lux-magnetar-thbsse-v1} \|
        \mathit{slot\_id}$ independently and route through
        unmodified \texttt{slhdsa.Verify}.
\end{enumerate}

The canonical 32-byte slot identifier is
$\mathit{slot\_id} = \textsc{cSHAKE256}(\texttt{encode}(\mathit{bind}),
32, \texttt{"MAGNETAR-THBSSE-SLOT-V1"})$. The encoding is
big-endian, length-prefixed per field --- the exact format the
reference implementation persists.

The four THBS-SE cSHAKE customisation tags are:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item \texttt{MAGNETAR-THBSSE-SLOT-V1} --- slot ID and message
        digest derivation;
  \item \texttt{MAGNETAR-THBSSE-R1-COMMIT-V1} --- Round-1 commit
        $D_i$;
  \item \texttt{MAGNETAR-THBSSE-SHARE-DEAL-V1} --- coefficient
        stream for $\mathrm{GF}(257)$ share dealing and
        \texttt{EvalPointFromID};
  \item \texttt{MAGNETAR-THBSSE-SHARE-MAC-V1} --- reserved for
        share-MAC envelope.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:thbsse:setup}

The protocol shape calls for the committee to run a leaderless
PVSS-DKG ceremony in which no party holds a master secret at any
point. The v1.0 Magnetar reference ships a
\emph{deterministic-dealer} variant
(\texttt{NewThbsSeKey}) for KAT determinism and foundation
single-host bootstrap; production deployments route through the
sibling \texttt{luxfi/threshold} DKG package and feed the result
into the same wire-shape share envelope.

The PVSS scheme of choice for the production setup is
Schoenmakers PVSS (CRYPTO 1999, \cite{schoenmakers1999}) over a
discrete-log group of prime order, with a cSHAKE256 hash-to-byte
step that lifts the group output into the byte-wise
$\mathrm{GF}(257)$ share field. Schoenmakers PVSS is publicly
verifiable: each share carries a NIZK proof of correctness against
public commitments, so any peer (the future public combiner
included) can verify the share envelope without holding any
secret. We pick Schoenmakers over a more modern hash-based PVSS
because the security argument over a curve subgroup with the DDH
assumption is well-understood, the per-share NIZK is compact
(constant number of group elements per share), and the
cSHAKE256 hash-to-byte step is FIPS 202-domain-separable into the
$\mathrm{GF}(257)$ share field cleanly. The DKG round structure
(complaint phase, qualified-set determination, joint public-key
derivation) is the standard Pedersen/Schoenmakers shape and lives
in the sibling \texttt{luxfi/threshold} package.

The deterministic-dealer reference produces:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item Sample SLH-DSA seed $S \in \{0,1\}^{8 \cdot \texttt{SeedSize}}$.
  \item Derive $(\mathit{PK}, \mathit{SK}) =
        \texttt{slhdsa.Scheme.DeriveKey}(S)$.
  \item Byte-wise Shamir-share $S$ across $(n, t)$ over
        $\mathrm{GF}(257)$ via
        \texttt{thbsseDealRandom} (\texttt{thbsse\_field.go}).
        Each byte position is shared independently as the
        constant term of a degree-$(t-1)$ polynomial; the
        coefficient stream is drawn from the RNG and stretched
        via cSHAKE256 if short.
  \item Publish $\mathit{PK}$, the committee, and $(n, t)$.
  \item Erase $S$. The dealer is in the TCB FOR SETUP ONLY;
        once \texttt{NewThbsSeKey} returns, no party including
        the dealer holds $S$.
\end{enumerate}

The output is a \texttt{ThbsSeKey} struct carrying
$(\mathit{PK}, [\mathit{share}_i], \mathit{setup\_transcript},
\texttt{Threshold}, \texttt{N}, \texttt{Params})$ where
$\mathit{setup\_transcript}$ is the
$\textsc{cSHAKE256}(\mathit{PK} \| n \| t \| \mathit{committee},
32, \texttt{"MAGNETAR-THBSSE-R1-COMMIT-V1"})$ tag --- the audit
witness that binds the public key to the share set without
leaking secret material.

\subsection{Sign Round 1 (per-party, in parallel)}
\label{sec:thbsse:round1}

Party $p_i$ with share $\mathit{share}_i$ and slot binding
$\mathit{bind}$, message $\mu$, and local slot guard $G$:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item Sample per-round mask $r_i$ uniformly random over
        $\{0,1\}^{8 \cdot 2 \cdot \texttt{SeedSize}}$ (the
        Shamir wire width).
  \item Compute the masked share
        $s'_i = \mathit{share}_i \oplus r_i$ byte-wise.
  \item Compute the Round-1 commit
        \begin{equation*}
          D_i = \textsc{cSHAKE256}\big(
            r_i \| s'_i \| \tau,
            32, \texttt{"MAGNETAR-THBSSE-R1-COMMIT-V1"}\big)
        \end{equation*}
        where $\tau = \texttt{encode}(\mathit{bind}) \| \mu \|
        \mathit{party\_id}_i$.
  \item Broadcast
        $(p_i, \mathit{slot\_id}, D_i, \mathit{Available})$.
  \item Persist $(\mathit{slot\_id}, H(\mathit{slot\_id} \| \mu))$
        in $G$. If $G$ already records the same
        $\mathit{slot\_id}$ under a different message digest,
        emit a
        $\texttt{ThbsSeEquivocationError}$ carrying the
        slashable evidence blob (see
        \S\ref{sec:thbsse:slashing}) and do NOT broadcast.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Sign Round 2 (per-party, after Round-1 quorum
observable)}
\label{sec:thbsse:round2}

Party $p_i$ reveals
\begin{equation*}
  \texttt{PartialSig}_i = r_i \| s'_i.
\end{equation*}

The wire layout is canonical: mask first, masked share second,
each $\texttt{Params.SeedSize} \times 2$ bytes wide. Idempotent
replay on the same $(\mathit{slot\_id}, \mu)$ returns the
persisted $(R1_i, R2_i)$. A genuine equivocation attempt is the
local equivocation case above.

\subsection{Combine (anyone, public)}
\label{sec:thbsse:combine}

The PUBLIC COMBINER routine $\texttt{Combine}$ takes
$(\texttt{Key},
\mathit{bind}, \mu, \{R1_i\}, \{R2_i\})$ and produces a
$(*\texttt{Signature}, [\texttt{ThbsSeShareEvidence}], \mathit{err})$
triple. Any peer can invoke it; no committee membership is
required to combine.

\textbf{Validation order:}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item Every Round-2 reveal must carry the same slot ID as
        $\mathit{bind}$. Mismatch emits
        \texttt{ThbsSeShareEvidence} with
        \texttt{Reason}=\texttt{ThbsSeShareSlotMismatch}.
  \item The PartialSig must be exactly
        $2 \cdot \texttt{Params.SeedSize} \cdot 2$ bytes.
        Wrong-size reveals emit
        \texttt{ThbsSeShareEvidence} with
        \texttt{Reason}=\texttt{ThbsSeShareWireSize}.
  \item The Round-1 commit must re-derive correctly from
        $(\texttt{PartialSig}, \mathit{bind}, \mu, p_i)$.
        Mismatch emits
        \texttt{ThbsSeShareEvidence} with
        \texttt{Reason}=\texttt{ThbsSeShareCommitMismatch}
        carrying both the expected and observed commits.
  \item The party's $\mathit{NodeID}$ must appear in the
        committee; unknown parties' reveals are dropped silently
        (the registry binding is a consumer concern).
  \item After dropping the above, the validated-share set must
        have size $\ge t$. If not, return
        \texttt{ErrInsufficientQuor} plus the collected
        evidence.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Reconstruction:}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep,start=6]
  \item For each surviving Round-2 reveal, recover
        $\mathit{share}_i = r_i \oplus s'_i$ byte-wise.
  \item Select the first $t$ shares by ascending evaluation
        point (canonical Lagrange basis); call the set $T$.
  \item Lagrange-interpolate at $x = 0$ over $\mathrm{GF}(257)$
        per byte position:
        \begin{equation*}
          S[b] = \sum_{i \in T}
            \lambda_i \cdot \mathit{share}_i[b]
            \pmod{257},
          \qquad \lambda_i = \prod_{j \in T, j \ne i}
            \frac{-x_j}{x_i - x_j} \pmod{257}.
        \end{equation*}
  \item Derive $\mathit{ctx} = \texttt{ctxFromSlot}(\mathit{bind})$
        and call
        $\sigma = \texttt{circl.slhdsa.SignDeterministic}(
        \mathit{SK} \gets \texttt{DeriveKey}(S), \mu,
        \mathit{ctx})$.
  \item Zeroize $S$, $\mathit{SK}$, the byte-sum buffer, and
        every transient working buffer before return. Return
        $(\sigma, \mathit{evidences}, \mathit{nil})$.
\end{enumerate}

Theorems~\ref{thm:thbsse-byte-identity}
and~\ref{cor:thbsse-determinism} establish that the output
$\sigma$ is byte-identical to a centralized FIPS 205 call on the
same $(S, \mu, \mathit{ctx})$, and that any two disjoint
$t$-sized sub-quora produce the same $\sigma$ byte-for-byte.

\subsection{Verify (anyone, public)}
\label{sec:thbsse:verify}

Verification is standard FIPS 205:
\begin{equation*}
  \texttt{circl.slhdsa.Scheme}(\textsc{Mode}).\texttt{Verify}(
    \mathit{PK}, \mu, \sigma, \mathit{ctx}).
\end{equation*}

The Magnetar reference exposes this as
\texttt{VerifyBytesCtx} for stateless dispatch (the wire bytes
of the group key, the message, the ctx, the wire bytes of the
signature in, a bool out). No Magnetar code runs on the
verifier path; any unmodified FIPS 205 verifier accepts.

\subsection{Slashing evidence}
\label{sec:thbsse:slashing}

\paragraph{Equivocation evidence
$(\texttt{ThbsSeEvidence})$.} A party that signs two distinct
messages at the same slot binding raises a
$\texttt{ThbsSeEquivocationError}$ carrying
\begin{equation*}
  (\mathit{slot\_id}, p_i, D^A, R1^A, R2^A,
  D^B, R1^B, R2^B).
\end{equation*}
Both Round-1 commits are publicly checkable against the
corresponding Round-2 reveals; both digests are checkable
against the slot ID; the two digests must differ. The function
$\texttt{VerifyThbsSeEvidence}$ implements all four checks as a
pure function with no committee state required. The consensus
layer's slashing pipeline applies $\texttt{VerifyThbsSeEvidence}$
to gossiped or on-chain-submitted evidence blobs and slashes the
party named in $p_i$ on success.

\paragraph{Malformed-share evidence
$(\texttt{ThbsSeShareEvidence})$.} Combine emits typed
share-malformation evidence for the three categories enumerated
above. $\texttt{VerifyThbsSeShareEvidence}$ is the third-party
pure-function verifier; for the commit-mismatch case it
recomputes the expected commit from
$(p_i, \mathit{bind}, \texttt{PartialSig}, \mu)$ and checks it
matches the named expected hash.

\subsection{Over-selected committee}
\label{sec:thbsse:overselect}

With $(n, t)$ and $n > t$, up to $n - t$ silent withholders are
tolerated. The Lagrange basis is determined by any $t$
evaluation points, so disjoint sub-quora of size $t$ produce
byte-equal final signatures
(Corollary~\ref{cor:thbsse-determinism}). The reference
implementation pins this via
$\textsc{TestThbsSE\_OverselectedCommittee}$ at
$(n=7, t=3)$ with 4 honest signers + 3 withholders and
$\textsc{TestThbsSE\_PublicCombiner\_Determinism}$ over the
$(n=7, t=3)$ committee comparing two disjoint $t$-subsets.

\subsection{Empirical gates}
\label{sec:thbsse:gates}

The reference implementation pins the construction's properties
via eight test gates at
\texttt{ref/go/pkg/magnetar/thbsse\_test.go}:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item $\textsc{TestThbsSE\_Wire\_FIPS205Verifiable}$ over
        three SHAKE modes --- the headline byte-identity
        regression.
  \item $\textsc{TestThbsSE\_RejectSeedReveal}$ --- a malicious
        party publishing a forged share that decodes to the
        seed is rejected at Combine's commit re-derivation.
  \item $\textsc{TestThbsSE\_RejectUnselectedFORS}$ --- a
        reveal with extra trailing bytes (the analog of
        ``reveal an unselected FORS leaf'') is rejected on
        wire-size grounds.
  \item $\textsc{TestThbsSE\_RejectUnselectedWOTS}$ --- a
        single-bit flip in the masked-share portion (the analog
        of ``reveal an unselected WOTS+ chain base'') is
        rejected on commit-mismatch grounds and emits
        verifiable evidence.
  \item $\textsc{TestThbsSE\_SlotReuseRejected}$ --- the local
        slot guard refuses a same-slot, distinct-message second
        signing attempt with a verifiable
        $\texttt{ThbsSeEquivocationError}$.
  \item $\textsc{TestThbsSE\_OverselectedCommittee}$ --- the
        availability claim under $n = 7$, $t = 3$ with 3
        silent withholders.
  \item $\textsc{TestThbsSE\_SlotBindingDomainSeparation}$ ---
        same message under distinct slot bindings produces
        distinct signatures; cross-binding verify fails.
  \item $\textsc{BenchmarkThbsSE\_Sign\_5of7}$ --- end-to-end
        per-signature wall-clock under SHAKE-192f at
        $< 100$~ms/op on Apple M1 Max.
\end{enumerate}

A bonus correctness gate
$\textsc{TestThbsSE\_PublicCombiner\_Determinism}$ pins the
public-combiner-determinism corollary, and
$\textsc{TestKAT\_ThbsSe}$ pins deterministic vectors at
$(n=7, t=4)$ over three SHAKE modes and three messages, regenerated
by \texttt{ref/go/cmd/genkat}.

\subsection{Bypassing the Cozzo-Smart obstruction}
\label{sec:thbsse:cosmin-smart}

Cozzo and Smart \cite{cosmin2021} establish that
threshold-by-seed-sharing for hash-based signatures requires
either reconstructing the seed at some combiner or running full
MPC over the hash tree. The literature treats the
seed-reconstruction route as putting the combiner ``in the TCB''
because the canonical instantiation has a privileged aggregator
role bound to a specific host.

THBS-SE bypasses the canonical Cozzo-Smart obstruction by making
the combiner role \emph{public}: any peer can run Combine on its
own substrate; no specific host is selected; the combiner does
not hold long-lived material. The seed reconstruction is
transient and local to the peer that chose to combine.
Bonte-Smart-Tan \cite{bonte2023threshold} extends the
infeasibility analysis to the strict
``no transient seed at any moment'' regime, which THBS-SE
v1.0 does NOT close; the strict-atom-assembly v1.1 path
(\texttt{BLOCKERS.md::MAGNETAR-STRICT-ATOM-V11}) is the planned
closer. THBS-SE v1.0 sits at the
``public-combiner-with-transient-seed'' point in the design
space --- materially stronger than TEE-attested
privileged-aggregator constructions, materially weaker than the
strict refinement.
