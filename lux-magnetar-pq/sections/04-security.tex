\section{Security argument}
\label{sec:security}

This section states the v0.1 security claims and the proof roadmap. We
state two theorems: (i) construction-equivalence to centralized FIPS 205
SLH-DSA (the Class-N1-analog property), and (ii) tEUF-CMA in the
aggregator-honest model under FIPS 205 SLH-DSA's UF-CMA assumption.

We are honest about what is \emph{not} proved here: a full mechanised
proof of either claim has not been built. The proof roadmap is in
\S\ref{sec:security:roadmap}; this paper states the claims with
informal-but-rigorous arguments and points at the planned mechanised
companion.

\subsection{Construction-equivalence (byte-equality)}
\label{sec:security:byte-equality}

\begin{theorem}[Class-N1-analog byte equality]
\label{thm:magnetar-byte-equality}
Fix a parameter set $\textsc{Mode} \in \{\mathrm{SHAKE\text{-}192s},
\mathrm{SHAKE\text{-}192f},
\mathrm{SHAKE\text{-}256s}\}$ and let
$(n, t)$ be a valid threshold configuration. Let $\textsc{DKG}$
complete honestly, producing a committee $\mathrm{Committee}$, joint
public key $pk$, and share set $\{\textsc{KeyShare}_j\}_{j=1}^n$.
Let $S$ be the master seed computed at DKG Round 3 (step 5 of
\S\ref{sec:construction:dkg}).

For any message $\mu$, context $\mathit{ctx}$, session identifier
$\mathit{sid}$, attempt counter $\kappa$, and quorum $Q \subseteq [n]$
with $|Q| = t$, if every party in $Q$ executes the threshold sign
protocol of \S\ref{sec:construction:sign} honestly and the aggregator
executes $\textsc{Combine}$ of \S\ref{sec:construction:combine}
honestly with $\mathit{randomized} = \mathrm{false}$, then
\begin{equation}
  \textsc{Combine}(pk, \mu, \mathit{ctx}, \mathrm{false},
    \mathit{sid}, \kappa, \mathrm{Quorum}, t,
    \{\textsc{R1}_i, \textsc{R2}_i\}_{i \in Q},
    \{\textsc{KeyShare}_j\}_{j=1}^n)
  \;=\;
  \textsc{slhdsa.SignDeterministic}(
    \textsc{slhdsa.DeriveKey}(S), \mu, \mathit{ctx}).
\label{eq:magnetar-byte-equality}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By inspection of the protocol.

\emph{Step 1: Quorum-independence of seed reconstruction.}
\eqref{eq:dkg-correctness} shows that any quorum $Q$ of size $\ge t$
reconstructs the same $\mathit{masterByteSum}$ from
$\{\mathit{mySumShare}_i\}_{i \in Q}$ via Lagrange interpolation. By
construction, $\textsc{Combine}$ step 4 performs exactly this Lagrange
interpolation on the shares recovered from the Round-2 reveals (each
$\mathit{share}_i$ recovered as $\mathit{masked}_i \oplus r_i$). Since
the cSHAKE256 mix~\eqref{eq:seed-mix} is a deterministic function of
$(\mathit{masterByteSum}, \mathit{committee\_root})$, and
$\mathit{committee\_root}$ is computed from the same sorted committee
in both DKG and $\textsc{Combine}$, the reconstructed master seed
$S_{\mathrm{rec}}$ in $\textsc{Combine}$ step 6 equals the DKG master
seed $S$.

\emph{Step 2: Pubkey consistency.}
$\textsc{Combine}$ step 7 computes $(sk_{\mathrm{rec}}, pk_{\mathrm{rec}})
\gets \textsc{slhdsa.DeriveKey}(S_{\mathrm{rec}})$. Since
$S_{\mathrm{rec}} = S$ (step 1), and FIPS 205 $\textsc{DeriveKey}$ is
a deterministic function of its seed input, $pk_{\mathrm{rec}}
= pk$ (step 8 of $\textsc{Combine}$ verifies this equality and aborts
otherwise) and $sk_{\mathrm{rec}}$ is byte-identical to the
$sk$ that a centralized signer would obtain.

\emph{Step 3: Signature determinism.} $\textsc{Combine}$ step 9 invokes
$\textsc{slhdsa.SignDeterministic}(sk_{\mathrm{rec}}, \mu,
\mathit{ctx})$. Per FIPS 205~\S9.2, $\textsc{SignDeterministic}$ is a
deterministic function of $(sk, \mu, \mathit{ctx})$. The output
signature bytes are byte-identical to those a centralized signer with
seed $S$ would produce on the same $(\mu, \mathit{ctx})$. \qed
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Empirical validation.} The byte-equality theorem is
empirically validated by $\textsc{TestN1\_ByteEquality\_Threshold}$\-
$\textsc{MatchesCentralized}$ \cite{magnetargo}, which runs the
threshold protocol end-to-end against the centralized FIPS 205 path
and asserts $\textsc{bytes.Equal}$ on the signature outputs. The
test covers $(n, t) \in \{(3,2), (5,3), (7,4)\}$ when not running
under $\mathit{-short}$; the short-form CI run executes only the
$(3,2)$ configuration ($\sim 4.4$~s on M2 Pro).

A complementary test
$\textsc{TestN1\_ByteEquality\_DifferentQuorumsSameSignature}$
exercises the quorum-independence corollary: two \emph{distinct}
quorums $Q_A = \{0, 1, 2\}$ and $Q_B = \{2, 3, 4\}$ over the same
DKG output produce \emph{byte-identical} signatures on the same
message. This is the direct consequence of Theorem~\ref{thm:magnetar-byte-equality}'s
quorum-independence step.

\begin{corollary}[FIPS 205 verifier acceptance]
\label{cor:fips205-acceptance}
A Magnetar v0.1 signature produced by $\textsc{Combine}$ (with
$\mathit{randomized} = \mathrm{false}$) is accepted by any unmodified
FIPS 205 SLH-DSA verifier under the joint group public key $pk$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:magnetar-byte-equality}, the Magnetar signature
bytes equal those of a centralized $\textsc{SignDeterministic}(
\textsc{DeriveKey}(S), \mu, \mathit{ctx})$. Centralized FIPS 205
signatures verify under their derived public key per FIPS 205~\S9.3.
\qed
\end{proof}

\subsection{Aggregator-honest tEUF-CMA reduction}
\label{sec:security:agg-honest}

\begin{theorem}[Magnetar v0.1 tEUF-CMA, aggregator-honest model]
\label{thm:magnetar-aggregator-honest}
Let $\Pi_{\mathrm{Magnetar}}$ be the v0.1 construction of
\S\ref{sec:construction} instantiated at parameter set
$\textsc{Mode} \in \{\mathrm{SHAKE\text{-}192s}, \mathrm{SHAKE\text{-}192f},
\mathrm{SHAKE\text{-}256s}\}$. Under
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=2em]
  \item the UF-CMA security of FIPS 205 SLH-DSA at the chosen
        parameter set \cite{fips205,sphincs};
  \item the collision resistance of cSHAKE256 at output length
        $32$~B (i.e., $2^{128}$-bit collision security);
  \item the PRF security of cSHAKE256 with a high-entropy key;
  \item the standard Shamir secret-sharing information-theoretic
        privacy property over $\mathrm{GF}(257)$ for any
        $(t{-}1)$-subset \cite{shamir1979};
\end{itemize}
in the \emph{aggregator-honest} model
(Definition~\ref{def:agg-honest-teuf-cma}), with adversary
$\mathcal{A}$ corrupting up to $t-1$ committee members and the
network channel,
\begin{equation}
  \mathsf{Adv}^{\mathrm{tEUF\text{-}CMA\text{-}AH}}_{\Pi_{\mathrm{Magnetar}}, \mathcal{A}}(\lambda)
  \;\le\;
  \mathsf{Adv}^{\mathrm{UF\text{-}CMA}}_{\mathrm{SLH\text{-}DSA}, \mathcal{B}}(\lambda)
  + \frac{Q_H + Q_S}{2^{256}}
  + \frac{Q_S}{2^{128}},
\label{eq:magnetar-aggregator-honest}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{B}$ is a black-box reduction running $\mathcal{A}$
with $Q_S$ signing queries and $Q_H$ random-oracle queries, with
running time within $\mathrm{poly}(\lambda)$ of $\mathcal{A}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
We construct a reduction $\mathcal{B}$ that converts a Magnetar
forger $\mathcal{A}$ into an SLH-DSA forger.

\emph{Setup.} $\mathcal{B}$ receives an SLH-DSA UF-CMA challenge
$pk$. $\mathcal{B}$ runs the Magnetar DKG with $\mathcal{A}$ playing
the corrupt parties; $\mathcal{B}$ programs the DKG so that the
joint group public key equals the challenge $pk$. The
programming exploits Shamir's information-theoretic privacy: for
$|F| < t$, $\mathcal{A}$'s view of the shares
$\{\mathit{share}_i\}_{i \in F}$ is statistically uniform regardless
of the master seed (\cite{shamir1979,katzlindell}~Theorem~3.6),
so $\mathcal{B}$ can simulate the corrupt parties' shares as
uniform random and the honest parties' contributions as random
strings consistent with the joint $pk$. This produces a perfect
simulation of the honest DKG view modulo the negligible cSHAKE256
collision probability on the transcript binding ($\le Q_H / 2^{256}$).

\emph{Signing oracle.} For each adversary signing query $\mu_i$,
$\mathcal{B}$ forwards $\mu_i$ to its SLH-DSA UF-CMA signing oracle
and obtains a signature $\sigma_i = \textsc{SignDeterministic}(sk,
\mu_i, \mathit{ctx})$. $\mathcal{B}$ then simulates the threshold
protocol transcript by:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=2em]
  \item Choosing a fresh $(\mathit{sid}_i, \kappa_i)$.
  \item For each honest party in the quorum, sampling a fresh mask
        $r_i$ uniformly and computing $\mathit{masked}_i =
        \mathit{share}_i \oplus r_i$ as the protocol prescribes.
  \item For the corrupt parties' contributions in the quorum,
        using the values $\mathcal{A}$ supplies; if those values
        cause a commit-bind check failure in $\textsc{Combine}$,
        $\mathcal{B}$ aborts the simulation as the protocol does.
  \item Returning $\sigma_i$ from its SLH-DSA oracle as the
        Magnetar signature. By
        Theorem~\ref{thm:magnetar-byte-equality}, this is
        byte-identical to what $\textsc{Combine}$ would produce
        on an honest run of this protocol given that the
        reconstructed seed equals the DKG-output seed (which equals
        the challenge $sk$'s seed by the DKG programming).
\end{enumerate}
The simulation is perfect except for: (i) a $Q_S / 2^{128}$ failure
probability arising from $\mathit{sid}$ collisions (the simulator
samples $\mathit{sid}_i$ from $\{0,1\}^{128}$); (ii) a
$(Q_H + Q_S) / 2^{256}$ failure probability for cSHAKE256 collisions
on the transcript hashes $\tau_1, \tau_{\mathrm{DKG}}$.

\emph{Extraction.} If $\mathcal{A}$ produces a successful Magnetar
forgery $(\mu^\star, \sigma^\star)$ with
$\textsc{Verify}(pk, \mu^\star, \sigma^\star, \mathit{ctx}) = 1$,
then by Corollary~\ref{cor:fips205-acceptance} $\sigma^\star$ is a
valid FIPS 205 signature under $pk$. $\mathcal{B}$ outputs
$(\mu^\star, \sigma^\star)$ as its UF-CMA forgery.

\emph{Validity.} $\mathcal{B}$ wins the SLH-DSA UF-CMA game iff
$\mu^\star \notin \{\mu_i\}$, which is exactly $\mathcal{A}$'s
winning condition. The reduction loses the simulation failure
probability, giving \eqref{eq:magnetar-aggregator-honest}. \qed
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Provable security level.}
$\mathsf{Adv}^{\mathrm{UF\text{-}CMA}}_{\mathrm{SLH\text{-}DSA}}$ at
the SHAKE-192s parameter set is
upper-bounded by $\sim 2^{-192}$ classical and $\sim 2^{-128}$
quantum (Grover); at SHAKE-256s, $\sim 2^{-256}$ classical and
$\sim 2^{-128}$ quantum. The $Q_S / 2^{128}$ term in
\eqref{eq:magnetar-aggregator-honest} dominates at the quantum bound
once $Q_S$ approaches $2^{60}$; this is a non-tight reduction (the
session-id collision term could be tightened via a stateful
$\mathit{sid}$ enforcement that the consensus layer
provides) but adequate at the operational scale of Lux Polaris
($Q_S$ per epoch $\le 10^6$, well below the binding term).

\subsection{The aggregator-honest assumption: honest disclosure}
\label{sec:security:tcb}

Theorem~\ref{thm:magnetar-aggregator-honest} is stated in the
aggregator-honest model. This is the honest v0.1 trust caveat. The
caveat covers exactly the in-memory lifetime of the reconstructed
master seed $S$ inside $\textsc{Combine}$:

\paragraph{In the TCB during $\textsc{Combine}$ steps 6--10:}
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=2em]
  \item the master seed $S$ (length $\textsc{seed\_size}$);
  \item the recovered SLH-DSA secret key $sk_{\mathrm{rec}}$
        (FIPS 205 wire form);
  \item the cSHAKE256 mix input $\mathit{mixInput}$
        (length $2 \cdot \textsc{seed\_size} + 32$);
  \item the reconstructed byte-sum vector
        $\mathit{reconstructedByteSum}$
        (length $2 \cdot \textsc{seed\_size}$);
  \item the per-quorum recovered shares $\{\mathit{share}_i\}_{i \in Q}$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Threat surface.} An adversary with arbitrary
code-execution rights on the aggregator host during this window can
exfiltrate $S$ via:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=2em]
  \item \texttt{/proc/<pid>/mem} read access (requires same UID or
        \texttt{CAP\_SYS\_PTRACE});
  \item ptrace-based debugging (same capability);
  \item coredumps written to disk (requires \texttt{kernel.core\_pattern}
        misconfiguration or signal-triggered crash);
  \item swap-file or hibernation image leaks (requires swap enabled
        or sleep-mode triggered);
  \item a malicious kernel or hypervisor (requires platform-level
        compromise).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Operational hardening.}
\texttt{DEPLOYMENT-RUNBOOK.md}~\S``Aggregator hardening'' enumerates
the production checklist:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=2em]
  \item Run the aggregator in a memory-encrypted VM (AMD SEV-SNP or
        Intel TDX) with attestation; verify attestation on every
        signing session.
  \item Disable coredumps (\texttt{ulimit -c 0},
        \texttt{kernel.core\_pattern} pointing to \texttt{/dev/null}).
  \item Disable swap (\texttt{swapoff -a}); disable hibernation
        (\texttt{systemctl mask hibernate.target sleep.target}).
  \item Disable ptrace (\texttt{kernel.yama.ptrace\_scope = 3});
        bind the aggregator process under
        \texttt{PR\_SET\_DUMPABLE = 0}.
  \item Run the aggregator as a fork-per-signature process;
        \texttt{execve} a fresh image and exit immediately after
        $\textsc{Combine}$ returns. This bounds the in-memory
        lifetime of $S$ to a single $\textsc{Combine}$ call ($\sim 1$--
        $50$~ms wall-clock).
  \item Bind under seccomp-bpf with a whitelist that forbids
        \texttt{ptrace}, \texttt{process\_vm\_readv}, and arbitrary
        \texttt{mmap} of \texttt{/proc/self/mem}.
  \item Run on dedicated hardware: no other user-controlled processes
        on the aggregator host.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What the aggregator-honest assumption does NOT cover.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=2em]
  \item A \emph{passive network observer} never sees $S$; round-1
        commits and round-2 reveals carry $\mathit{share}_i$ values
        only as XOR-masked outputs whose pre-images require
        $t$-of-$n$ recovery.
        Note: v0.1 DKG envelopes are plaintext (a network observer
        \emph{can} see committee shares being distributed in DKG);
        v0.2 closes this with ML-KEM-768 wrapping.
  \item A \emph{single Byzantine committee member} cannot forge a
        signature; forging requires reconstructing $S$, which
        requires $t$ shares.
  \item A \emph{Byzantine aggregator that has not received valid
        Round-2 reveals} cannot forge a signature; the commit-bind
        check rejects tampered reveals.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Side-channel posture}
\label{sec:security:sidechannel}

The Magnetar v0.1 reference implementation is written in Go with the
following constant-time-relevant properties:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{cSHAKE256 invocations.} The Keccak permutation is
        implemented in \texttt{golang.org/x/crypto/sha3} with no
        secret-dependent control flow. Constant-time.
  \item \textbf{SLH-DSA invocations.} Delegated to
        \texttt{cloudflare/circl/sign/slhdsa}~v1.6.3. The circl
        implementation is constant-time per its documented design
        \cite{circl}. Independent verification under \texttt{dudect}
        \cite{dudect} is the outstanding obligation (BLOCKERS.md
        BLK-7).
  \item \textbf{Shamir Lagrange interpolation.}
        \texttt{shamir.go}~lines 148--164 perform modular
        exponentiation via square-and-multiply
        ($\textsc{modPowSmall}$); the exponent
        $p-2 = 255$ is a public constant, so the loop trip count
        is independent of secret operands. The inner
        multiplications are over $\mathrm{GF}(257)$ via 32-bit
        arithmetic with no secret-dependent branching.
  \item \textbf{Constant-time commit comparison.} The Round-2
        commit-bind check uses $\textsc{ctEqual32}$
        (\texttt{transcript.go}), implemented as a constant-time
        OR-then-NEG-mask byte sequence.
  \item \textbf{Zeroization.} Every secret-bearing buffer is
        explicitly zeroized on every return path via
        $\textsc{zeroizeBytes}$ (byte buffers) or
        $\textsc{zeroizeU16Slice}$ (per-byte GF(257) vectors). No
        $\textsc{defer}$ patterns --- explicit zeroization at each
        return site keeps the secret lifetime locally legible.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Open obligations.} A \texttt{dudect} timing-equivalence
sweep at $\ge 10^9$ samples across the v0.1 sign and combine paths
is the outstanding side-channel deliverable. This is tracked as
BLOCKERS.md BLK-7 and is a prerequisite for any Tier A claim.

\subsection{Nonce-uniqueness invariant}
\label{sec:security:nonce}

The Round-1 mask $r_i$ is derived deterministically from
$\mathit{rngBytes} \,\Vert\, \mathit{sid} \,\Vert\, \kappa
\,\Vert\, \mathit{NodeID}_i$ via cSHAKE256. Two honest signers cannot
produce the same $r_i$ unless $(\mathit{rngBytes}, \mathit{sid},
\kappa, \mathit{NodeID})$ collides. With $\mathit{rngBytes}$ sampled
from a CSPRNG (default \texttt{crypto/rand}), the per-session
$r_i$ values are statistically distinct.

In a fixed-$\mathit{rng}$ setting (deterministic KAT mode), Magnetar
\emph{requires} that the consensus layer guarantees unique
$(\mathit{sid}, \kappa)$ per attempt. Under Lux Quasar, $\mathit{sid}$
is the (chain id, block height) pair whose monotonicity is enforced
by P-Chain consensus \cite{lp020}; $\kappa$ is the rejection-restart
counter (always $\ge 1$). This provides session-id uniqueness as a
chain invariant rather than as a runtime obligation.

\subsection{Composition with Polaris}
\label{sec:security:polaris}

Polaris is the Lux maximum-assurance cert profile pairing three
independent threshold lanes: Pulsar (Module-LWE), Corona (Ring-LWE),
and Magnetar (FIPS 205 SLH-DSA). The composed soundness theorem
(Theorem~8 of \cite{quasarcertsoundness}, ``post-quantum safety'')
bounds the adversary advantage by the \emph{minimum} of the per-lane
advantages. A Polaris adversary must break all three lanes
simultaneously: Module-LWE, Ring-LWE, and FIPS 205 SLH-DSA. No
known reduction transfers a break of one to a break of another, so
the conjunction is strictly harder than any single primitive.

The role-binding invariant is enforced by Prism
\cite{lp073pulsarpaper}~\S\ref{sec:related:lattice}: every Magnetar
pulse hashes the certificate subject including
$(\mathit{chain\_id}, \mathit{epoch}, \mathit{round},
\mathit{KeyEraID}, \mathit{Generation})$, so cross-epoch replay
reduces to cSHAKE256 collision resistance.

\subsection{Proof roadmap}
\label{sec:security:roadmap}

The v0.1 paper states the security claims with informal proofs. The
path to Tier A involves mechanising the claims into machine-checked
proof artefacts.

\paragraph{EasyCrypt.} The reveal-and-aggregate construction maps
directly onto an EasyCrypt \cite{easycrypt} game-hop sequence:
$\mathsf{Game}_0$ is the honest tEUF-CMA game; $\mathsf{Game}_1$
replaces honest signing with the simulator of
Theorem~\ref{thm:magnetar-aggregator-honest}; $\mathsf{Game}_2$
returns SLH-DSA oracle signatures verbatim. The hops are bounded by
the cSHAKE256 PRG / PRF security and Shamir privacy. This is BLK-7
in \texttt{BLOCKERS.md}; the planned artefact lives at
\texttt{proofs/easycrypt/Magnetar/} once the EasyCrypt theory ships.

\paragraph{Lean 4.} A Lean 4 specification of the byte-equality
theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:magnetar-byte-equality}) is the lowest-
hanging mechanisation target: it is a purely \emph{algorithmic}
equivalence (no probabilistic reasoning), and the Lean
\texttt{lux-formal-verification-lean4} \cite{lp-lean4} infrastructure
already mechanises the Shamir Lagrange identity in
\texttt{Crypto.Threshold.Reshare.reshare\_preserves\_secret}. Porting
the identity to GF(257) and chaining through the cSHAKE256 mix is
the v0.3 Tier A entry condition.

\paragraph{Jasmin.} A Jasmin \cite{jasmin} reference implementation
of the constant-time hot paths (Shamir interpolation, commit
verification, zeroization) is the side-channel deliverable; this
augments the \texttt{dudect} empirical pass with a machine-checked
constant-time proof.

\paragraph{Independent cryptographer review.} BLOCKERS.md BLK-9: an
independent cryptographer sign-off is the human-checked Tier A
gate. The review scope is the security argument
(\S\ref{sec:security}), the FIPS 205 conformance trace
(\texttt{FIPS-TRACEABILITY.md}), and the constant-time
posture (\texttt{TRUSTED-COMPUTING-BASE.md}).
