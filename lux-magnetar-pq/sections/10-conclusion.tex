\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{What Magnetar v0.2.0 is}
\label{sec:conclusion:is}

Magnetar v0.2.0 is the production library implementation of a
threshold FIPS 205 SLH-DSA construction at Tier B in the Lux Lyttle
tier discipline:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Reference implementation.} $\sim 3\,992$ lines of Go
        across 12 production files plus 9 test files
        (\S\ref{sec:implementation}), backed by
        \texttt{cloudflare/circl v1.6.3} \cite{circl} as the FIPS
        205 primitive.
  \item \textbf{Class-N1-analog property.} Threshold signatures are
        byte-identical to centralized FIPS 205
        $\textsc{SignDeterministic}$ on the reconstructed master
        seed (Theorem~\ref{thm:magnetar-byte-equality}), validated
        empirically by $\textsc{TestN1\_ByteEquality}$ across three
        threshold configurations.
  \item \textbf{Deterministic KAT vectors.} Five JSON files at
        \texttt{vectors/} regenerable bit-identically from a clean
        checkout via $\texttt{cmd/genkat}$.
  \item \textbf{Polaris third-lane integration.} Joins Pulsar (M-LWE)
        and Corona (R-LWE) as the hash-based leg of the
        maximum-assurance Polaris cert profile, providing
        cryptographic-family diversity against any single-family
        algorithmic break.
  \item \textbf{Honest disclosure.} The v0.1 reveal-and-aggregate
        aggregator-TCB caveat is documented at the
        construction-specification, deployment-runbook, and
        proof-claim levels. No covert reliance on aggregator
        integrity; no hidden trust assumptions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What Magnetar v0.2.0 is not}
\label{sec:conclusion:not}

Honest characterisation of the open gates:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Tier A.} No mechanised proofs (EasyCrypt theory, Lean
        bridges, Jasmin sources) have shipped. The construction-
        equivalence theorem (\ref{thm:magnetar-byte-equality}) is an
        algorithmic claim amenable to direct mechanisation; the
        tEUF-CMA reduction (\ref{thm:magnetar-aggregator-honest})
        is a probabilistic claim requiring a game-hop sequence.
        See \S\ref{sec:security:roadmap} for the planned artefact
        layout.
  \item \textbf{Independent audit.} No external cryptographer
        sign-off has been performed. BLOCKERS.md BLK-9 tracks the
        review path; the scope includes the security argument,
        the FIPS 205 conformance trace, and the constant-time
        posture.
  \item \textbf{Constant-time empirical verification.} A
        \texttt{dudect} sweep at $\ge 10^9$ samples across the
        sign and combine hot paths is the outstanding side-channel
        deliverable (BLOCKERS.md BLK-7).
  \item \textbf{Full MPC threshold.} v0.1 is reveal-and-aggregate;
        the aggregator process is in the TCB for the brief
        $\textsc{Combine}$ window. v0.2 (the research direction
        of \S\ref{sec:fullmpc}) targets a per-signature MPC over
        the SLH-DSA hash tree, but the construction is nontrivial
        because SLH-DSA has no linear share-aggregation identity.
  \item \textbf{ML-KEM-768 envelope wrapping for DKG.} v0.1 DKG
        envelopes are plaintext (a passive network observer can see
        committee shares being distributed). v0.2 will wrap each
        envelope under ML-KEM-768 to the recipient's long-term
        identity public key, matching Pulsar's BLOCKERS.md CR-8
        pattern.
  \item \textbf{Full 16-document NIST MPTC submission package.} The
        v0.2.0 scaffold under \texttt{docs/mptc/} mirrors the
        canonical structure but omits the actual proof artefacts
        and audit reports that a Category-1 submission would
        require. BLOCKERS.md BLK-8.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Path to Tier A}
\label{sec:conclusion:tier-a}

The Tier A path is mechanically structured. In approximate order
of dependency:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,nosep]
  \item Mechanise Theorem~\ref{thm:magnetar-byte-equality} in
        Lean 4 (lowest-hanging fruit; the algorithm is purely
        deterministic). The proof reuses the existing
        $\textsc{reshare\_preserves\_secret}$ Lagrange identity
        from \texttt{lux-formal-verification-lean4} \cite{lp-lean4}
        modulo a GF(257) port.
  \item Mechanise Theorem~\ref{thm:magnetar-aggregator-honest} in
        EasyCrypt as a game-hop sequence. The hops are:
        $\mathsf{Game}_0$ honest tEUF-CMA $\to \mathsf{Game}_1$
        simulator-replaced $\to \mathsf{Game}_2$ SLH-DSA oracle
        verbatim. Bounded by cSHAKE256 PRG/PRF and Shamir privacy.
  \item Ship the \texttt{dudect} empirical constant-time pass at
        $\ge 10^9$ samples for the sign and combine hot paths.
        Identify any data-dependent timing variance and remediate
        before independent review.
  \item Schedule the independent cryptographer review against the
        full v0.1 specification (\S\ref{sec:construction}), the
        security argument (\S\ref{sec:security}), the FIPS 205
        conformance trace (\texttt{FIPS-TRACEABILITY.md}), and
        the constant-time evidence
        (\texttt{TRUSTED-COMPUTING-BASE.md}).
  \item Address any audit findings; pin the post-audit commit as
        Tier A v0.3.0.
\end{enumerate}

The Tier A target window is open: it depends on the audit response
turnaround and any remediation cycles. Operationally, deployments
running Polaris today are using Magnetar v0.2.0 at Tier B with the
documented trust caveat; the safety guarantee comes from the
Polaris composition (any two of three lanes intact = post-quantum
finality) rather than from any single-lane assurance.

\subsection{Closing}
\label{sec:conclusion:closing}

The Lux PQ transition is structured around the principle that no
single primitive carries the post-quantum future of the chain.
Pulsar (M-LWE), Corona (R-LWE), and Magnetar (SLH-DSA) are three
independent constructions that share neither hash function family,
nor secret-sharing mechanism, nor underlying mathematical
assumption beyond ``the standard cryptographic-hash assumptions
hold.'' The Polaris cert profile binds all three lanes against a
common transcript and accepts only when all three verify.

Magnetar's role in this composition is the hash-based insurance
leg: the lane that is intact when the entire lattice family is
broken by some unforeseen algorithmic advance. It is the slowest
of the three lanes, which is acceptable; the insurance does not
need to be fast.

The v0.1 reveal-and-aggregate construction documented in this
paper is the operationally available starting point. The v0.2
research direction (\S\ref{sec:fullmpc}) is open. The Tier A
mechanised proofs and independent audit are scheduled. In the
meantime, Magnetar v0.2.0 ships as a library, and the deployment
discipline is to run it as one of three lanes in Polaris with
the aggregator hardening of
\texttt{DEPLOYMENT-RUNBOOK.md}~\S``Aggregator hardening.''

The cryptographic primitives are not new. The composition that
makes them into a hash-based threshold-SLH-DSA leg of a
three-lane Polaris cert profile, with byte-equality to the
unmodified FIPS 205 verifier and an honest trust-model
disclosure, is Magnetar's contribution.
