\section{Reshare Benchmarks (LSS-Pulsar and LSS-Lens)}
\label{sec:reshare-benchmarks}
%% ====================================================================

The LSS framework \cite{luxlssmpc,lp077} provides protocol-agnostic dynamic resharing across Pulsar (lattice) and Lens (curve) kernels. This section measures the end-to-end resharing time from $(t_{\mathrm{old}}, n_{\mathrm{old}})$ to $(t_{\mathrm{new}}, n_{\mathrm{new}})$ at sizes $\{3, 7, 21, 64, 128\}$.

\subsection{LSS-Pulsar resharing}

Reshare ($t_{\mathrm{old}}, n_{\mathrm{old}}) \to (t_{\mathrm{new}}, n_{\mathrm{new}})$ with $t = \lceil 2n/3 \rceil + 1$. Per-party CPU time and aggregate wall-clock for the resharing math (excludes WAN delay, includes lattice Pedersen verify and activation cert):

\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrrr@{}}
  \toprule
  $\mathbf{n_{\mathrm{old}}}$ & $\mathbf{n_{\mathrm{new}}}$ & \textbf{Per-party Reshare (ms)} & \textbf{Activation cert (ms)} & \textbf{Total wall-clock (ms)} \\
  \midrule
  3   & 3   & 110  & 250 ($K=3$ Sign1+Sign2)   & 360 \\
  7   & 7   & 220  & 350 ($K=7$)               & 570 \\
  21  & 21  & 480  & 480 ($K=21$, see below)   & 960 \\
  64  & 64  & 1340 & 1100                      & 2440 \\
  128 & 128 & 2680 & 2050                      & 4730 \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}

Reproduction: \texttt{cd threshold \&\& go test -bench=BenchmarkPulsarReshare/n=N -benchtime=3x ./protocols/lss/...}.

\paragraph{What scales with $n$.} The per-party Reshare cost scales near-linearly with $n_{\mathrm{old}}$ (each old qualified-subset member emits one $g_i(X)$ polynomial; each new party aggregates $|Q|$ polynomials). The activation cert cost scales near-linearly with $K = n_{\mathrm{new}}$ via the Sign1 + Sign2 + Combine pipeline.

\paragraph{What does not scale with $n$.} The GroupKey itself ($\mathbf{A}$, $\tilde{\mathbf{b}}$ bytes) remains byte-identical across reshare (Lemma~\ref{lem:gk-byte-identical} of \texttt{proofs/lss/lifecycle-safety.tex}). The lattice math is independent of validator-set turnover within an era; only the share distribution rotates.

\subsection{LSS-Lens resharing}

Lens resharing is the curve-side analog. Per-party time scales with curve scalar-mul cost ($\sim 75$ \textmu s per party on Ed25519 / secp256k1 / Ristretto255 baseline):

\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrr@{}}
  \toprule
  $\mathbf{n_{\mathrm{old}}}$ & $\mathbf{n_{\mathrm{new}}}$ & \textbf{Per-party Reshare (\textmu s)} & \textbf{Total wall-clock (ms)} \\
  \midrule
  3   & 3   & 250  & 0.7 \\
  7   & 7   & 580  & 1.8 \\
  21  & 21  & 1740 & 5.2 \\
  64  & 64  & 5300 & 16.5 \\
  128 & 128 & 10600 & 33.0 \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}

Lens reshare is $\sim 100$--$200\times$ faster than Pulsar reshare per party. The relative overhead is consistent with the Lens vs Pulsar single-sig ratio (Section~\ref{sec:microbenchmarks}). Reproduction: \texttt{cd threshold \&\& go test -bench=BenchmarkLensReshare/n=N ./protocols/lss/...}.

\subsection{Reshare with set rotation ($n_{\mathrm{old}} \neq n_{\mathrm{new}}$)}

A common deployment: a chain expands its validator set from $n=21$ to $n=64$ at an epoch boundary. The reshare runs from old qualified subset $|Q| = t_{\mathrm{old}}$ to the new $n_{\mathrm{new}}$ parties:

\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrr@{}}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Transition} & \textbf{Pulsar (ms)} & \textbf{Lens (ms)} & \textbf{Notes} \\
  \midrule
  $21 \to 21$ (refresh, same set)  & 720  & 4.0 & HJKY97 zero-poly path \\
  $21 \to 21$ (reshare, new set)   & 960  & 5.2 & Desmedt--Jajodia path \\
  $21 \to 64$                      & 1820 & 11.0 & Expansion \\
  $64 \to 21$                      & 1080 & 6.5 & Contraction; only old $Q \subseteq O$ runs \\
  $64 \to 128$                     & 3460 & 22.0 & Expansion; large $n_{\mathrm{new}}$ \\
  $128 \to 64$                     & 1820 & 11.0 & Contraction \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}

Reproduction: \texttt{cd threshold \&\& go test -run TestDynamicResharePulsar/$\langle$transition$\rangle$ ./protocols/lss/...}.

\paragraph{The $K=21$ activation-cert latency line.} The activation cert at $K=21$ takes the same Sign1 + Sign2 + Combine pipeline as a routine $K=21$ Pulse (Section~\ref{sec:activation-cert-latency}). The path is identical; the message is canonical \texttt{QUASAR-PULSAR-ACTIVATE-v1} (Definition \texttt{def:pulsar-activate} of \texttt{proofs/definitions/algorithms.tex}).

\paragraph{Measured Pulsar end-to-end at $K=21$ (M1 Max, 2026-05-04 evening).}
A direct measurement of the Sign1 + Sign2 + Combine pipeline at $K = 21$
via \texttt{cd pulsar \&\& GOWORK=off go run . l 5 21}
(\texttt{LocalRun(5)}, $K = 21$):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Phase} & \textbf{Mean (ms)} & \textbf{Median (ms)} \\
  \midrule
  \texttt{Gen}                              & 8.475   & 8.475   \\
  \texttt{SignRound1} (per party)           & 40.789  & 40.742  \\
  \texttt{SignRound2Preprocess} (per party) & 112.869 & 112.541 \\
  \texttt{SignRound2}  (per party)          & 6.532   & 6.532   \\
  \texttt{SignFinalize}                     & 0.090   & ---     \\
  \texttt{Verify}                           & 0.950   & ---     \\
  \texttt{Total Signing} (per party)        & 160.190 & 159.987 \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The activation-cert latency at $K = 21$ is therefore $\approx 160$ ms
of single-core CPU time per party plus the standard $\sim 0.95$ ms
verify, dominated by Round 2 Preprocess. With 21 parties signing in
parallel on independent cores, the wall-clock equals one party's
total signing time plus the Combine step ($\sim 0.09$ ms) plus 2 WAN
RTTs (Section~\ref{sec:cross-wan-latency}). Stability check: a
second run at $K=21$ produced \texttt{Total Signing} mean $159.879$ ms
(\,$\Delta < 0.5\%$).

\subsection{KAT regeneration time}

The pulsar canonical regen-kats script (\texttt{pulsar/scripts/regen-kats.sh}) regenerates every KAT consumed by the C++ / GPU ports. The full run takes \textbf{58.86\,s wall-clock on M1 Max} (Darwin 25.4.0 arm64, Go 1.26.2, 2026-05-04 evening measurement, post-fuzz battery, CPU quiescent; \texttt{75.26\,s user + 7.00\,s system, 139\% CPU}), dominated by the 16-entry \texttt{ringtail\_oracle\_v2} KAT batch (\texttt{TestEmitDeterministic} + \texttt{TestSignVerifyEntries} + \texttt{TestShamirRoundTrip}):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
  \toprule
  \textbf{KAT step} & \textbf{Wall-clock (M1 Max, 2026-05-04 evening)} \\
  \midrule
  reshare\_oracle (1 KAT)              & $\sim 4$ s \\
  dkg2\_oracle (1 KAT)                 & $\sim 8$ s \\
  activation\_oracle (1 KAT)           & $\sim 6$ s \\
  ringtail\_oracle\_v2 emit + tests (7 + sign-verify-e2e at 16 reps) & $\sim 41$ s \\
  hash NIST KAT verification           & $< 1$ s \\
  manifest sha256                      & $< 0.1$ s \\
  \midrule
  \textbf{Total wall-clock}            & \textbf{58.86 s} \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The \texttt{ringtail\_oracle\_v2} run is the long pole. The 7 emitted entries plus the 16-rep sign-verify-e2e ceremony cover the Pulsar wire format end-to-end: PRNG / BLAKE2-XOF derivation, discrete-Gaussian samples, Montgomery NTT round-trip, structs Matrix wire bytes, transcript hash, Shamir share, sign-verify-e2e. Each entry is byte-deterministic; the entire run produces output that hashes byte-equal across runs (manifest \texttt{regen-kats.manifest.sha256} re-derives identically).

Reproduction: \texttt{cd pulsar \&\& ./scripts/regen-kats.sh}; verification: \texttt{./scripts/regen-kats.sh --verify}.

\subsection{Snapshot and rollback}

LSS snapshot management (\texttt{threshold/protocols/lss/rollback.go}) persists a configurable rolling window of share states. Snapshot save and rollback latencies:

\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Operation} & \textbf{$n=21$} & \textbf{$n=128$} \\
  \midrule
  Snapshot save (encrypt + write to disk) & 3 ms & 18 ms \\
  Rollback (read + decrypt) & 4 ms & 22 ms \\
  Snapshot retention window (default) & 8 generations & 8 generations \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}

The snapshot manager retains 8 generations by default (configurable via chain governance). Rollback time is dominated by the share-state encryption decrypt + JSON unmarshal; the rollback marker bookkeeping is sub-millisecond.

\subsection{Wall-clock vs CPU time}

The reshare benchmarks here measure local-loop simulation (no WAN delay). Production resharing on Lux mainnet runs over WAN with $\sim 200\,\mathrm{ms}$ inter-party RTT; the Pulsar Sign1 + Sign2 + Combine pipeline requires 2 WAN crossings, so production wall-clock is local-loop wall-clock plus $\sim 400\,\mathrm{ms}$ for $K=21$. The cross-WAN projection table (Section~\ref{sec:cross-wan-latency}) accounts for this.
