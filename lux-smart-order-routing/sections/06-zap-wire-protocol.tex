\section{ZAP Wire Protocol}
\label{sec:zap}

\subsection{Binary Serialization}

Orders transmitted between the SOR and execution providers use the ZAP binary wire protocol \cite{lux-zap-wire-protocol} for minimal serialization overhead:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{ZAP order message format.}
\label{tab:zap-format}
\begin{tabular}{llrl}
\toprule
\textbf{Field} & \textbf{Type} & \textbf{Bytes} & \textbf{Description} \\
\midrule
magic & uint16 & 2 & Protocol identifier (0x5A41) \\
version & uint8 & 1 & Protocol version \\
msg\_type & uint8 & 1 & Message type (order, cancel, exec, \ldots) \\
flags & uint16 & 2 & Bit flags (side, TIF, compliance) \\
security\_id & bytes32 & 32 & Security identifier \\
order\_id & bytes16 & 16 & Order identifier \\
price & uint64 & 8 & Price in basis points \\
quantity & uint64 & 8 & Quantity in base units \\
timestamp & uint64 & 8 & Nanosecond timestamp \\
partition & bytes32 & 32 & Partition identifier \\
checksum & uint32 & 4 & CRC-32C checksum \\
\midrule
\multicolumn{2}{l}{\textbf{Total}} & \textbf{114} & \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

At 114 bytes per order message, ZAP achieves serialization latency under 1 microsecond on modern hardware, compared to 50--200 microseconds for JSON-based protocols.
