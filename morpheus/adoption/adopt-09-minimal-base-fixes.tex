\vspace{1.0ex}

\noindent
Fifty lines of Solidity make Morpheus settlement real, with no chain move. It is the
cheapest entry in this series.

\vspace{0.6ex}
\noindent\textbf{The problem, in their numbers.}
MOR trades at \$1.95, market cap near \$17M [verified]. Real revenue is Lido and Aave yield,
about \$476K a year and falling, down from \$3.24M a quarter at the 2024 launch [verified].
Emission outruns real revenue near ten to one [verified]. This is not a fraud: principal is
withdrawable one to one. The honest problem is dilution outrunning revenue, over a settlement
number no one can check. Settlement is \code{pricePerSecond} times elapsed time [verified]. The
provider node signs a seven-field receipt, including the input and output token counts that say
how much inference happened (\code{abi.go:12-19}) [verified]. The contract decodes five of the
seven and drops the two that matter (\code{SessionRouter.sol:237}) [verified]. Only the provider
signs (\code{SessionRouter.sol:227}). Nothing stops a provider opening a session against its own
bid (\code{SessionRouter.sol:106}). The owner key, an anonymous five-of-nine, can rewrite any
facet in one transaction, with no timelock [verified]. Morpheus built the honest half. The node
already signs the work. The contract discards it.

\vspace{0.6ex}
\noindent\textbf{The fix.}
Four edits to the \code{SessionRouter} facet in \code{Morpheus-Lumerin-Node/smart-contracts/}.
None touch the token, the treasury, the staking math, or the chain.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Decode the whole receipt.} Add two \code{uint32} fields to the decode tuple at
      \code{SessionRouter.sol:237}. Settlement then reads the tokens, not just the clock. About five lines.
\item \textbf{Make the consumer co-sign.} \code{closeSession} already holds \code{session.user}
      and the recover-and-compare helper (\code{SessionRouter.sol:543}). Run it against the
      consumer, and a provider cannot post volume the consumer will not sign.
\item \textbf{Forbid self-dealing.} One \code{require(session.user != bid.provider)} removes the
      wash trade where a provider funds a session against its own bid.
\item \textbf{Timelock the owner.} A timelock between the Safe and the owner role turns a stolen
      key into an announced upgrade with a withdrawal window, not an instant silent loss.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.4ex}
\noindent\textbf{The outcome.}
About fifty lines of Solidity [illustrative: sum of the four edits above], on the diamond that
already exists. No migration, no new token, no vote to move capital. Best case: settlement reads
work the node already signs, both parties sign the bill, self-dealing is blocked, and a
compromised owner key buys a window instead of the treasury.

\vspace{0.4ex}
\noindent\textbf{Assumptions.}
Reading \code{inputTokens} proves a count was signed, not that the tokens were produced;
attesting the work is a later step (LP-313). The receipt still settles on the provider's reported
count until a consumer disputes it. A timelock narrows the owner key's blast radius; it does not
remove it. This makes the base honest, not complete.

\vspace{0.4ex}
\noindent\textbf{Relevance.}
Lux built \code{mord}, an un-fakeable settlement engine where faked work earns zero and model
substitution is slashed; forty tests pass as of 2026-08-29 [verified]. We know which fifty lines,
and why. This fix is the first rung, and the rest of the series stands on an honest receipt.
